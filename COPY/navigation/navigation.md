### Page headline, letters page

> **Six people put it in writing.**
> Two bosses and four customers, 2003 to 2025. In full, named, nothing trimmed.

**Status:** **Recommended. Restatused twice on 11 August 2026** — first from *Five people / Three
industries / Twenty-five years* after the archive audit, then again in phase 2 after Brad's
decisions. Full page copy, with every caption and pull-quote, is now in
`COPY/letters/letters-page.md`.
**For:** Every word is true and checkable. It replaces a claim about solicitation with a claim about
span, which is the harder thing to fake. The display line has now grown correctly twice, three to
five to six, without breaking.
**Against:** "Put it in writing" is a shade legalistic. **The number is load-bearing and it is no
longer stable** — Brad is requesting a replacement reference from Deane Jessep, so six may become
seven. That is a one-word change here, which is exactly why the count must stay out of the hero
display line. See `COPY/hero-lines/hero-lines.md` round three.

**The standfirst changed, and the reason matters more than the words.**

> ~~Four industries. Twenty-two years. Full letters, named, nothing trimmed.~~

*Four industries* was called the load-bearing improvement by the audit. It is literally true and it
does not survive checking. AR Walker was Brad's managing director at Adplus; Hamish Lowry ran Hawke's
Bay Tourism, which was an Adplus account. **Advertising and tourism are the same fifteen months, seen
from opposite sides of the desk** — Lowry wrote on 12 February 2003 and Walker six weeks later. A
reader who does that arithmetic reads *four industries* as padding, and then keeps counting and finds
that the six documents cover three jobs rather than six.

*Two bosses and four customers* answers a sharper objection and cannot be picked apart: customers
like a good salesman, so what did the people who managed him think? Both employers wrote one, and
both volunteered that the resignation was Brad's own — Walker, *"Brad leaves us of his own accord"*;
Lemon, *"Brad's resignation was of his own choosing."* Full argument in
`COPY/letters/letters-page.md` §2.

*Full letters* also went, for a smaller reason that is still a reason: two of the six are not
letters. Lad's is an email and Pilgrim's is a structured reference document. *In full* is exact for
all six.

### Alternate page headline

> **Signed.**
> Six people, two bosses, four customers. In full, with their names on.

**Status:** **Held, and now compromised.** *Signed* was recommended on 11 August as the headline
because it named the physical fact and made no claim about solicitation. The audit found that
**two of the six documents are not signed** — Pilgrim's and Lad's are not signed documents. A
one-word headline that the page disproves twice is worse than a plain one. Revivable only if the page
ships with signed letters alone, which would cut the count to four.

**Corrected 11 August 2026, phase 2.** The count of unsigned documents was three when Jessep was in
the set; he is out, so it is two. The standfirst carried *four industries*, dropped for the reason
given above.
**For:** One word, and the fact does all the work. Survives any change to the count.
**Against:** Terse to the point of cold at the top of the warmest page on the site.

### Retired headline

> **Unsolicited.**
> ~~References available on request.~~ **References available.**

**Status:** Rejected 10 Aug 2026, **factually dead 11 Aug 2026.**
**Against:** Brad found the display word weird and confusing, the strikethrough gag depends on
rendering he cannot control in email previews, and the underlying claim is untrue of two of the three
letters. **The idea survives at one-fifth scale:** nobody asked Pranesh Lad for his email, and that
sentence can sit on his letter and nowhere else.
