## 1. The section as a whole

### 1.1 Heading · **Recommended**

> Land, keep, grow — since 2001.

**For.** It states the finding in five words and puts a date on it, which is the whole value of Tier
E: the argument the site already makes from the education decade turns out to run twenty-four years,
not ten. It is the shortest true form.

**Against.** *Land, keep, grow* is a sales-industry phrase, and a hiring manager who has heard it in
four other interviews this month reads it as vocabulary rather than as a claim. The date is doing
the work; the verbs are close to filler.

### 1.2 Heading alternate · **Available**

> Every job before the software decade was the same job.

**For.** Concrete, no industry vocabulary, and it earns the section rather than announcing it — the
reader has to keep going to find out what the same job was. It also does something the recommended
line cannot: it explains why a section about 2001 belongs on a 2026 site.

**Against.** Overstated as written. Canwest was media buying and 99 Corporation was direct
marketing; *the same job* is true about the account motion and false about the work. It also risks
reading as though Brad has not progressed in twenty-five years, which is the exact wrong note for a
candidate whose last role was General Manager.

### 1.3 Heading alternate · **Available**

> Four employers, twenty-five years, one motion.

**For.** Countable and checkable. Sits in the same register as the proof strip.

**Against.** "One motion" needs the next sentence to mean anything, so it cannot stand alone as a
heading the way 1.1 can. And the count is soft — four employers before 2015, but five roles with
MySimpleSiteMan, and a reader who counts the rows and gets a different number stops trusting the
line.

### 1.4 Standfirst · **Recommended, paired with 1.1**

> I kept the accounts I won. At the advertising agency, at the web agency, at the radio network and
> at the direct marketing company — billing, contracts, renewals and the relationship, not just the
> signature at the start.

**Rows:** E1, E17, E30, E41. **For.** It names the four sectors without naming the four employers,
which keeps it true while the naming checks (D11) are still open, and it lists the four functions
Brad actually said he owned rather than characterising them. **The four functions are the definition
of account management**, so the sentence does the positioning work without ever using the phrase.

**Against.** It publishes a clean four-for-four when the honest answer is three clean and one mixed
(E30). See 1.5 — the qualification has to appear somewhere and this line does not carry it.

### 1.5 Standfirst · **CONFIRMED 14 August 2026 — Brad's own wording, chosen over 1.4**

> I kept the bulk of the accounts I won. Not all of them, and not everywhere — but the billing, the
> contracts, the renewals and the relationship were mine at every job I have had since 2001.

**Rows:** E1, E17, E30, E41, footnote ⁵⁴. **For.** *Not all of them, and not everywhere* is E30's
qualification travelling with the claim, in seven words, and footnote ⁵⁴ argues that this
qualification is what makes the other three believable. **A retention claim with a concession in it
is stronger than a clean one**, because the clean one reads as a candidate who has never lost
anything, and no one believes that.

**Against.** It spends its second clause conceding, and a reader skimming the first line of a
section may take *not all of them* as the headline. It is also longer, and the homepage budget is
250 words.

**Recommendation.** 1.5. The concession is cheap and it buys the rest of the section. If Brad prefers
1.4's cleanness, then **E30's qualification must appear on the Adplus row instead** — it does not get
dropped.

## 3. The upsell arithmetic — E12

**The most direct evidence for the target role anywhere in the archive**, and it is pre-education.
It needs its own treatment because it is the only place in the whole career where the shape of
land-keep-grow is visible in two numbers.

### 3.1 · **Recommended**

> At the direct marketing company I grew the customer base by around 70 per cent and income by around
> 120 per cent.

**Rows:** E12. **For.** Both figures, stated once, plainly, in his own order. *Around* twice, which
is his hedge and it survives. No arithmetic performed on the reader's behalf.

**Against.** A reader who does the division gets the finding for free — and a reader who does not
gets two percentages and no point. **That is the deliberate cost.** Footnote ⁴⁵ bars computing the
per-customer uplift, and it bars it for us as well as for the reader; we cannot point at the gap
without doing the sum.

### 3.2 · **Unblocked 14 August 2026 — see E51/E52**

> Customers up around 70 per cent. Income up around 120 per cent. The owner puts a number on the
> mechanism too: profit per customer up around 30 per cent — the customers we already had were
> buying more.

**Rows:** E12, E51, E52. **For.** This is no longer an inference resting on E6–E11 alone. Rob
Nieuwland, the owner, independently supplied a third figure the same day (E51) — 30 per cent more
profit per customer — which confirms the *shape* of the gap (existing customers buying more) without
Brad or this file computing 120 ÷ 70 to get there. **The per-customer figure was stated, not
derived**, so the arithmetic bar at footnote ⁴⁵ is not being routed around; it is simply not needed
any more.

**Against.** E51's 70 per cent (customers) matches E12 exactly; E51's 30 per cent (profit per
customer) and E12's 120 per cent (income, business-wide) are **different measurements of different
things and must never be reconciled or presented as the same fact twice** (footnote ⁶³). This line
keeps them as two separate sourced claims — customers/income from Brad, profit-per-customer from the
owner — connected only by the plain-English point they both support. **Do not let a tighter edit
merge them into one arithmetic chain.**

### 3.2a · **CONFIRMED 14 August 2026 — Brad chose this over 3.2, Nieuwland named**

> Customers up around 70 per cent, income up around 120 per cent, in my time there. Rob Nieuwland, who
> owned the business, puts it differently but the same way: profit from each customer was up around
> 30 per cent too.

**Rows:** E12, E51, footnote ⁶³. **For.** Keeps the two sources visibly separate — Brad's figures,
then the owner's, not blended into one voice. Reads as corroborated rather than asserted. **Naming is
now cleared** — Brad, 14 August 2026: "Nieuwland can be named."

**Against.** Slightly longer than 3.2. Naming a third party this specifically invites the reader to
imagine a reference check, which is a fair reading — E51 came from Nieuwland directly, so it holds up.

### 3.3 · **Rejected before it was written**

> ~~Average customer value up around 29 per cent~~ · ~~Income grew 1.7× faster than the customer
> count~~ · ~~I nearly doubled the business~~

**Rejected 14 August 2026 by footnote ⁴⁵ and E14.** The first two are 120 ÷ 70 in two disguises. The
third is E14, superseded — same claim at lower resolution, and publishing it beside E12 makes a
reader wonder why the vaguer one was needed. **Recorded here so the ground is not covered twice.**

### 3.4 Scope note that travels with any form of E12

Brad ran sales and marketing at that company. **He is entitled to the outcome of the function he
ran, not to the business's whole performance** (footnote ⁴⁵). The recommended line says *I grew*,
which is a claim about his function. **If any version reaches "the business grew", it is out of
scope and comes back.**

## 5. The commercial instinct — E9, E22, E32

Three sectors, three different mechanisms, arrived at independently. **The rows to carry if the
section needs a second argument after retention**, because it is the only pattern in Tier E that
repeats across employers.

### 5.1 · **Recommended**

> Three times, at three different companies, I found something we were giving away and put a price
> on it — a deposit up front at one, a premium listing tier at another, a copywriting service line
> at the third.

**Rows:** E9, E32, E22. **For.** The pattern and its three instances in one sentence, none of them
quantified — which is correct, because **no amount, rate, tier count or margin exists for any of the
three** (E6, E9, E10, E22, E32 bars). *Found something we were giving away* is concrete without being
a figure.

**Against.** It is long for a site under a 250-word homepage budget, and it will not fit there. **This
line is for the career page or the CV, not the homepage.** It also flattens three quite different
moves — a cash-cycle decision, a pricing ladder and a new service line — into one shape, and the
flattening is the reason it reads well.

### 5.2 · **Available, shorter**

> At three different companies I put a price on something that was being given away free.

**For.** Fits anywhere. Same claim, no instances.
**Against.** Without the three instances it is an assertion about himself, which §7 of the
positioning brief bans in spirit — *let the customers do the praising*. The instances are what make
it a fact rather than a self-description.

### 6.1 · **Recommended**

> I was selling and holding monthly-fee products from 2001 — modules, bolt-ons, SEO packages, and a
> two-year cycle of redesigns and copy refreshes back to clients I already had.

**Rows:** E34, E20, E23. **For.** *Selling and holding* is exactly the claim and exactly not the

**Against.** It sits one word away from a claim the register bars twice (E20, E34): **agency
bolt-ons on monthly fees are the motion, not the product.** Any edit that turns *selling and
holding* into *building* or *managing a SaaS product* is out. **Flag this line for a spelling-level
check at ship time, not a skim.**

> deal~~

**Rejected by E20, E25 and E34.** Brad's own phrase is "my first true taste of SaaS products", and
the taste is the claim. E25's bar is explicit: *"Sold a SaaS deal" is barred.*

### 7.1 · **CONFIRMED 14 August 2026 — Brad's choice, figure included**

**Rows:** E48, E49. **For.** His diagnosis, published as his. Two failures named, both structural,

**Against.** It is the only failure on the site and it will be read as characteristic if it is given

### 7.2 The line that closes it · **Available, and it must not be strengthened**

**Row:** E50, and the bar in it. **For.** His own connection, his own words.
**Against.** It is one comparative away from a redemption arc, which the row bars explicitly. **Any
edit adding *everything*, *made me*, or a causal *because* is out.** If it cannot survive without
strengthening, cut it and let 7.1 end on the diagnosis.

## 9. Two things this draft deliberately left on the table

**Xero (E4).** It is the most recognisable client name in the pre-education career after Harvey
Norman, and **it is not in this draft.** Footnote ⁴² bars it as a standalone line, as a logo placed
larger or apart, and bars any implication Brad contributed to its growth. **What survives is Xero as
one name in a list of four clients, which is a CV line, not a copy line.** Putting it in this section
would be reaching for the name, and the reach is what the footnote warns about. **Available for the
CV page; recommended against here.**

**The Hawke's Bay Chamber of Commerce, four years (E19).** Cleanest retention evidence in the
pre-education career on duration — four of a four-year-two-month tenure — and it did not make the
recommended lines because §2's Xplore row went to E23 instead. **It is the better row on retention;
E23 is the better row on the motion the target job pays for.** If Brad wants Xplore to argue
retention rather than recurring revenue, the row title becomes **Hold a client for the whole time
you are there** and the Chamber is named. **Do not conflate it with Xchange** (footnote ⁴⁸) — the
Chamber was both a client and a relationship that fed the referral network, and publishing them
together implies he monetised the Chamber as a referral source, which he did not say.
