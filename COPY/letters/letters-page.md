## 1. Page headline · **Recommended, 11 August 2026**

> # Six people put it in writing.
> Two bosses and four customers, 2003 to 2025. In full, named, nothing trimmed.

**For.** The headline survives from `COPY/navigation/navigation.md` unchanged and every word of it is
still true. The standfirst is the part that changed and it is a material improvement — see §2.
"In full, named, nothing trimmed" is three promises the page then keeps in front of the reader, which
is the governing rule working at the top of the page rather than being asserted.

**Against.** "Put it in writing" is a shade legalistic. **The number is load-bearing and it is no
longer stable** — Brad is requesting a replacement reference from Deane Jessep, so the count may go
to seven. That is a one-word change here and a one-word change in the nav gloss, which is the reason
the count must stay out of the hero display line. See `COPY/hero-lines/hero-lines.md`.

**Note on the noun.** The page never calls the six "letters" as a set, and this is deliberate. Two of
the six are not letters — Lad's is an email and Pilgrim's is a structured reference document. *Put it
in writing* covers all six exactly. The route is `/letters` and that is fine; a route is not a claim.

### Alternate standfirst · **Available**

> Two employers and four customers, 2003 to 2025. In full, named, nothing trimmed.

The sober version. Use it if *bosses* reads as too informal at display size. It is very slightly less
precise — Phillip Lemon was Brad's manager rather than the employing entity — and the caption under
his document resolves that either way.

### Alternate standfirst, count-free · **Available**

> The newest is from June 2025. The oldest is from February 2003. In full, named, nothing trimmed.

Makes the reader do the arithmetic, which convinces harder than being handed the answer, and it never
needs changing when the count moves. Loses the six, which is the fact that makes a scanner stop.

## 2. Why "four industries" was dropped — the one recommendation this session overturns

**The audit called four industries "the load-bearing improvement". On the evidence it is the weakest
number in the set and it should not be printed.**

`01d` §9 and §11 recommend *Six people. Four industries. Twenty-two years* — advertising, tourism,
radio and education software. Every word is literally true. It is still the wrong line, for one
reason a reader finds without effort:

**Advertising and tourism are the same fifteen months.** AR Walker was Brad's managing director at
Adplus. Hamish Lowry ran Hawke's Bay Tourism, which was one of Adplus's accounts. Lowry wrote on
12 February 2003 and Walker wrote on 24 March 2003 — six weeks apart, about the same job, from
opposite sides of the desk. Counting them as two industries is arithmetic a reader can do, and when
they do it, *four industries* reads as padding.

The same reader keeps going and finds that the six documents cover **three jobs**, not six: Adplus
(two documents), CanWest (one), and the education-software channel role (three). The span argument survives that — twenty-two years
and every job he has held is the actual claim — but only if the site does not first invite the reader
to start counting.

**What replaces it is better, not merely safer.** *Two bosses and four customers* answers a sharper
objection than the industry count ever did. The recruiter's real doubt is not *was it all one soft
sector*. It is **customers like a good salesman; what did the people who managed him think?** Both of
Brad's bosses wrote one, and both of them volunteered, unprompted, that the resignation was Brad's own:

- Walker, 2003: *"Brad leaves us of his own accord."*
- Lemon, 2006: *"Brad's resignation was of his own choosing."*

Two employers, three years apart, each choosing to put that in a letter. That is unusual, checkable
against the documents on the page, and impossible to arrange.

**The cost of the change.** *Four industries* was the answer to the sector objection and the new line
does not answer it directly — a reader who specifically wonders whether the pattern is education-only
has to notice the 2003 and 2006 letterheads for themselves. The captions and the dateline make that
one glance away. Accepted.

**Upstream consequence.** `01d` §9 and §11, `COPY/navigation/navigation.md` and
`COPY/section-headings/section-headings.md` all carry *four industries*. All three are restatused in
this session.

## 3. The redaction note · **Recommended** · sits directly under the standfirst, small type

> **The blocks are mine.** Phone numbers, email addresses and street addresses have been covered over
> on every document, in the page's own colour so you can see where. Nothing else has been touched.

**For.** It converts a legal necessity into a trust statement, and "so you can see where" is the whole
argument for visible redaction in five words. A reader who meets this line before the first document
reads every block afterwards as care rather than as concealment. It also pre-commits the page to
*nothing else has been touched*, which the Walker caption then proves at Brad's own expense.

**Against.** It spends a beat on process before the reader has seen any evidence. The alternative is
to put it at the foot of the page, where it reads as a footnote and does none of this work.

### 3.1 Scan check for the employer name — run 12 August 2026, one ruling open

Decision 6 of `01-positioning-brief.md` §11 is that the employer is never named, on the site or on
the CV. The three documents from that decade were checked for the name in body text, signature blocks
and letterhead. Result:

| Document | Employer name present? | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bergh**, 16 Jun 2025 | **No.** Nothing to decide | Letterhead is Christian Alliance International School, Hong Kong — Bergh's own employer. Body names Schoology throughout and the vendor never |
| **Pilgrim**, 2025 | **No.** Nothing to decide | Letterhead is The Industry School. Body names Schoology throughout and the vendor never |
| **Lad**, 26 May 2025 | **Yes. One occurrence** | The `To` line of the email header: *Brad Friis <brad@[employer domain]>*. Live text in the PDF, not part of an image, so it redacts cleanly |

**The existing rule already covers it, and that is the finding.** The redaction note above commits
the page to covering *phone numbers, email addresses and street addresses* on every document. Brad's
address in the `To` line is an email address, so it was already scheduled for a block before decision
6 existed. Lad's own address and the internal ticket address on the same document are blocked under
the same rule, so the reader meets three consistent blocks rather than one conspicuous one. **Decision
6 does not force a new treatment here; it makes an existing one load-bearing.**

**What survives the block, which is the thing §4 rank 5 of the brief cares about.** The email framing
is *from, to, date* — sender name, recipient name, subject line and timestamp all remain. Only the
addresses go. *Reproduced exactly as it was sent* and *nothing else has been touched* both still hold.

**The one ruling for Brad.** Block the whole address or the domain only. **Recommended: the whole
address**, because it is what the redaction note already promises and because a block sized to exactly
the domain draws a reader's eye to the shape of the thing that was hidden. Domain-only is the option
if Brad wants his own name legible in the `To` line for authenticity.

**Not changed:** the archive transcription at
`Brad Friis Resumes/References/Brad_Friis_Rototuna_Reference_Letter.md` still carries the address in
full. That file records what the document says, and redacting the source would corrupt the record.
The redaction is a publication step, not an archive edit.

## 4. The six, newest first

Ordered strictly by date. The two 2003 documents arrive last, so the age lands as the closing move
rather than as evidence that Brad had to reach back — the argument in `01d` §4.

**Captions are first person.** The site's voice is Brad's, and a relationship disclosed in his own
words reads as volunteered. The same fact in third-person editorial voice reads as a disclaimer.

**One pull-quote per document, and none of them repeats the homepage.** A reader who arrives from the
five-campus save or the adoption section has already read the homepage quotes. Every quote below is
new to them, so the page pays them for the click.

### 4.1 Andrew Bergh · 16 June 2025

**Caption · Recommended**

> Andrew Bergh, high school principal. 16 June 2025. Nine years — first as a teacher, then as a
> senior leader at a multi-campus New Zealand school, and now as a principal in Hong Kong. We started
> working together in 2016 and he was still writing about it in 2025.

**Pull-quote · Recommended**

> He consistently brought a student-centered mindset to our strategic conversations, contributing
> invaluable insight and humility to complex, system-level discussions.

**Why this quote.** Humility, from a principal, about a software vendor's local rep. It is the least
expected sentence in the document and it is not used anywhere else on the site. The change-resistance
quote — the strongest line in the letter — stays on the homepage adoption section, where it sits
beside the claim it proves. American spelling of *student-centered* is the original's and stays.

**Held, not used here.** *"Mister Schoology"* appears inside the reproduced letter and must not be
pulled out on this page. It is held for `/record`, once, at the adoption claim.

### 4.2 Pranesh Lad · 26 May 2025

**Caption · Recommended**

> Pranesh Lad, IT Systems Engineer, Rototuna High Schools. 26 May 2025. Nobody asked Pranesh for this
> one. It arrived as an email on a Monday morning and it is reproduced exactly as it was sent.

**Pull-quote · Recommended**

> You're a real star, Brad :)

**Why.** This is the whole of the *Unsolicited* idea at one-sixth scale, in the one place on the site
where it is true, exactly as `COPY/navigation/navigation.md` reserved it. The email framing — from,
to, date — is the proof and must stay visible. The smiley stays. It does more work than any joke Brad
could write, and printing a colon-bracket at pull-quote size is the most confident thing on the page.

**Against.** A reader who has just been told two bosses wrote references then meets a thank-you email
from an IT systems engineer, and the drop in formality is real. It is second on the page rather than
sixth because the date says so, and because warmth this early is worth more than a smooth gradient.

### 4.3 Adrian Pilgrim · 2025

**Caption · Recommended**

> Adrian Pilgrim, Teaching and Learning Manager, The Industry School. 2025. Five campuses, and an
> active evaluation of a competing platform underway when we started. This is the document behind the
> case study on the home page.

**Pull-quote · Recommended**

> Brad is trusted and deeply respected by our staff. His support is effective and delivered with
> warmth, patience, and a great sense of humour.

**Why.** `COPY/section-headings/section-headings.md` flagged this as the warmest sentence any customer
has written and warned it should not be wasted on a page nobody reaches. This is that page. The
homepage keeps *"we were considering moving away from Schoology"*, which is the commercial line and
belongs beside the save.

**On the date.** Pilgrim's document carries a year and no month, so it sits third among the 2025
documents by default rather than by judgement. Placing it first because it is the strongest would be
a soft claim about a date the page cannot support, and this page cannot afford one. **The document is
also not a letter** — it is a structured reference with headings and bullets, which is why the
standfirst says *put it in writing* rather than *letters*.

### 4.4 Phillip Lemon · 11 February 2006

**Caption · Recommended** · this is the caption `01d` §4 requires, and it is not optional

> Phillip Lemon. 11 February 2006. My manager at CanWest Media, writing after we had both left. The
> letterhead is The Radio Network, where he was working by the time he wrote it.

**Pull-quote · Recommended**

> Several of Brad's past clients have mentioned his ability a number of years on from his departure.

**Why.** It is the best sentence in the archive for the site's actual proposition. Brad's claim is
that he keeps customers; this is a manager recording, in 2006, that customers kept Brad after he had
gone. It is the earliest evidence of the thing the last decade is supposed to prove.

**Why the caption is load-bearing.** The ordinary reading of a Radio Network letterhead on a CanWest
reference is that Lemon had changed employer. A hostile reader can instead read it as a reference
written on borrowed paper. One line closes the question before it is asked, and it costs nothing
because the fact is unremarkable.

### 4.5 AR Walker · 24 March 2003

**Caption · Recommended**

> AR Walker, Managing Director, Adplus Advertising. 24 March 2003. My employer, writing when I
> resigned. There is a word missing from the third paragraph. It is missing in the original, and it is
> still missing here.

**Pull-quote · Recommended**

> He was always pleasant but somehow managed to obtain an extraordinarily high strike rate from which
> many clients were converted. Often he did so with little or no material backup what so ever.

**Why the caption names the error.** The page has already promised *nothing trimmed*. Twenty lines
later there is a sentence with a word dropped out of it. Either the page names it first, in which case
the promise is demonstrated at Brad's own expense, or the reader finds it, in which case they briefly
wonder whether the transcription is careless. Naming it is the single cheapest credibility purchase
available on the page, and it is the page's one dry line — the voice rules allow exactly one.

**Against.** It draws attention to a flaw in the strongest early document, and a reader in a hurry
may take *there is a word missing* as a comment on the referee. The final clause — *and it is still
missing here* — is what turns it from apology into policy, and it should not be cut for length.

**Note.** *"with little or no material backup what so ever"* is the original's spacing. It stays.

### 4.6 Hamish Lowry · 12 February 2003

**Caption · Recommended**

> Hamish Lowry, General Manager, Hawke's Bay Tourism. 12 February 2003. A client rather than an
> employer — Hawke's Bay Tourism was one of my accounts at Adplus, and he wrote this six weeks before
> my managing director did. It is the oldest document on the page.

**Pull-quote · Recommended**

> It is my observation that Brad has assisted with a significant improvement in tourism industry
> buy-in to the core marketing activities of Hawke's Bay Tourism during this time.

**Why this closes the page.** Two documents about the same fifteen months, six weeks apart, from the
man who paid the invoices and the man who signed the payslips. Stated in the caption rather than left
for the reader to assemble, it is the most interesting structural fact in the archive and it turns
the oldest document into the page's closing argument instead of its weakest entry.

**Why the quote is the substantive one rather than the warm one.** Lowry also writes *"an individual
with integrity; plus the drive and dedication to reach and exceed sales targets"*, which is warmer and
says nothing a hundred other references do not. The chosen line is a client-side General Manager
attributing a measurable shift in his own industry to a strategy Brad worked on. Specific over
impressive.

**Verbatim note.** *"I have had pleasure in working with"* is as written, not *"the pleasure"*.

## 5. The capability block · beneath the six

### Heading · **Recommended**

> ## The three things six people said without comparing notes.

**For.** Converts a capability claim into converging evidence, which is the only form of capability
statement a hiring manager has reason to believe. The count now matches the documents above it
exactly. Nobody else can write this heading.

**Against.** Long. **The number is load-bearing** and moves if the count moves. And *without comparing
notes* is unprovable for the three 2025 documents, whose authors all work in New Zealand secondary
education and could plausibly know each other. It is provable for the pairs that matter — Walker in
2003 did not compare notes with Pilgrim in 2025 — and no reader can contradict it, which is why it
survives. It is a figure of speech for *independently*, not a factual claim of the same class as
*unsolicited*, and the distinction is real: *unsolicited* was contradicted by something Brad knew.

### Alternate heading · **Available**

> ## Three things the oldest documents say and the newest ones say again.

Drops the count and the unprovable clause, and names the mechanism instead. Quieter, and it loses the
number that makes a scanner stop. The safe choice if Brad wants nothing on the page that can be
argued with at all.

### Standfirst · **Recommended**

> Each of these is claimed by at least one document written before 2007 and one written in 2025. That
> is what makes it a pattern rather than a good year.

**Note on the dates.** *Before 2007* rather than *in 2003*, because two of the three pairs are anchored
by Lemon in 2006 and only one by Walker in 2003. *Twenty-two years apart* is true of one pair and
nineteen of the other two, so the block does not use a single span figure.

### The three · **Recommended**

> **1. The relationship outlives the transaction.**
> Phillip Lemon, 2006: *"Several of Brad's past clients have mentioned his ability a number of years
> on from his departure."* Andrew Bergh started working with me in 2016 and was still writing about it
> in 2025.
>
> **2. It works without the standard tooling.**
> AR Walker, 2003: *"an extraordinarily high strike rate… with little or no material backup what so
> ever."* Adrian Pilgrim, 2025: *"custom strategies and workarounds that fit our operations."*
>
> **3. The reluctant come along.**
> Phillip Lemon, 2006: *"a positive influence within his sales team."* Andrew Bergh, 2025: *"his
> ability to support teachers, particularly those resistant to change."*

### Closing line · **Recommended**

> These three are not a list I wrote. They are what is left when you read the six documents next to
> each other and mark what more than one person says.

**For.** States the mechanism in the plainest available words and lets the reader verify it against
the six documents directly above. It is the register spec exactly — a senior person showing you the
documents.

**Against.** It invites the reader to check, and a reader who checks will notice that Brad chose which
six documents to publish. That is survivable and worth stating: neither withheld document contradicts
any of the three, and one of them, MySimpleSiteMan, argues for number two.

**Rejected phrasing.** *"I did not choose these three."* Brad did choose the six documents the three
are derived from, so the sentence is doing more work than it can carry.

### Held · **Superpowers**

Still available as this block's heading, per `COPY/navigation/navigation.md`, where it is a joke with
six documents under it rather than a claim without any. Not recommended: it is common in tech job
advertisements, and it spends the page's one unexpected move on a word rather than on the evidence.

## 6. The contact block

### Recommended · **the safe version**

> **Every person on this page agreed to be named here.**
>
> If it's a role, tell me the company and the number I'd be carrying. If it's consulting, tell me
> what's stuck.

**For.** The first line is the strongest closing sentence the page can make and it costs nothing,
because it is now simply true of all six. It also quietly answers the question a careful reader has
been holding since the first document: *do these people know this is on the internet?* The second line
is the contact copy already in use across the site, unchanged.

**Against.** Consent to be named is not consent to be telephoned, and a reader may hear it as an
offer of reference calls. The line stops short deliberately.

### **Do not use without a second consent** — the strong version

> **Every person on this page agreed to be named here.** If you would rather hear it in a phone call
> than read it on a website, tell me which one and I will ask them.

**Why it is held.** It is the best line available and it is not Brad's to write yet. What Lowry,
Walker and Lemon have consented to is publication. Committing six people to fielding calls from
strangers is a separate ask and needs a separate yes. *"I will ask them"* rather than *"I will make
the introduction"* is the honest construction even after consent, because it does not promise the
other person's answer.

**Unblocks on:** a second, explicit consent from all six covering reference calls. Cheap to ask for at
the same time as anything else.

## 7. What the page must not say

Ten documents exist in the archive. Six are published. The four that are not — Jessep, MySimpleSiteMan,
95bFM and the CanWest sales sheet — are absent for four different reasons, and none of them are the
reader's business. **The page therefore makes no completeness claim of any kind.**

Banned on this page:

- *Everything anyone has written about me*
- *All of them*, *the complete file*, *the full archive*, *here they all are*
- Any construction implying the six are the total, rather than six named people who put it in writing

*"Six people put it in writing"* is a count of what is shown. It is exact, and it stays exact only as
long as nothing nearby implies it is also a count of what exists.

### The disclosure that was considered and rejected

A page arguing *these people trust me* could plausibly go further and name what it withheld: *there are
two more references I have chosen not to publish — one because the referee shares my surname, and one
because it puts six former colleagues' sales figures on the internet.* On a site built on disclosure
that is a coherent move, and the second half of it is genuinely creditable.

**Rejected, for one reason.** The first half discloses that a relative wrote Brad a reference, which is
worse for the reader than silence and is the exact suspicion the other six exist to prevent. Splitting
the two — disclosing the sales sheet and not the surname — is a selective disclosure dressed as a
candid one, which is a worse failure than either. Withholding quietly and claiming nothing is the
honest position, and it is what §7 above enforces.
