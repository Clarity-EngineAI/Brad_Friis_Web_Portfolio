## Answer 2 — how the Visitor Guide account grew, in Brad's own words

**Given 14 August 2026, verbatim (free-text override):**

> "more advertisers, more categories and price increases over the 3 years as the guide became more
> popular and valuable as a resource for Visitor Information Centres around NZ - now called i-Sites -
> you rarely saw a tourist in Hawke's Bay without one. Particularly with the wineries - every winery
> had to be in it year 3 or they'd miss out on visitors. It also developed as a wine trail in the
> guide. I also leveraged the visitor guide to align to the HB Tourism website - which I also managed
> all the advertisers on. So we deveoped a package deal where they could be on both, and buy premium
> spots on the tourism website for extra fees - Gold Pages, and Gold Listings we called them, versus
> normal listings (still chargeable but no control on where they were placed in the order of their
> category - Gold advertisers received premium placement."

### Three separate growth mechanisms, not one

| Mechanism | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **More advertisers** | Volume growth — new categories of business joined as the guide's reach grew |
| **More categories** | The guide's scope widened — wineries becoming a defined wine-trail section is the named instance |
| **Price increases** | A rate rise layered on top of volume growth, not instead of it |

**This is the same shape as the six-move commercial pattern already logged at 99 Corporation and the
education role** (`_index.md` "governing question" section, `3-99-corporation.md`) — volume growth
and a rate increase running together, not one or the other. **It is the earliest instance of that
pattern in the archive**, predating 99 Corporation (2013) by twelve years.

### A tiered pricing product: Gold Pages / Gold Listings

**Brad built a two-tier product across two properties (the printed Visitor Guide and the Hawke's Bay
Tourism website) and sold a bundle across both, with a premium tier on top.**

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | Created a tiered advertising product — standard listings (chargeable, unordered placement) and "Gold" listings/pages (premium placement, extra fee) — sold as a bundle across the printed Visitor Guide and the Hawke's Bay Tourism website |
| Verbatim | "we deveoped a package deal where they could be on both, and buy premium spots on the tourism website for extra fees - Gold Pages, and Gold Listings we called them" |
| Evidence status | **Vouched (Brad).** No figure for how many advertisers took the Gold tier, no separate revenue number |
| Publishable | **Yes, qualitatively.** **No invented uptake percentage or revenue figure — none was given** |

**This is a genuine pricing-tier product, not a discount or a bundle discount.** It is structurally
close to the education role's tiered service model and to 99 Corporation's "pricing would scale
accordingly" — **a repeatable instinct to build a ladder rather than sell one flat rate.**

### The wineries detail is worth keeping as a named instance

**"Every winery had to be in it year 3 or they'd miss out on visitors"** is a specific, concrete
description of category saturation — not a vague growth claim. **It also corroborates the reference
letters directly**: Hamish Lowry's letter (Hawke's Bay Tourism, 12 February 2003) describes Brad's

fees; he did not build or own a SaaS product.**

regional tourism body's own numbers, not a measured claim of his own performance, and the end-date is
unstated ("latest figures" could be any year up to now). **This is context for the region, not a
performance figure attributable to Brad.** If used at all, it belongs as scene-setting, not as
evidence of what he achieved.
