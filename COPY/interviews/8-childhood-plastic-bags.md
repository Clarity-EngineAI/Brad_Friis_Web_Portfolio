## The interview

### The scheme

> "This was a school initiative to fundraise money from the school community. The stakes were high -
> the person who sold the most would win a brand new 'scientific calculator'. As soon as I heard
> that I was both bedazzled and inspired to make sure it was mine when the sales tally was said and
> done."

**A school-run fundraiser with a single prize for the top seller.** The competition was there from
the start and it was the entire motivation. **The money was the school's; the calculator was the
point.**

> "Every student was given a sample pack of each type of bag, and a form to record which Aunty or
> Grandma had bought what and how many."

**The design of the scheme is the story's hidden joke and should not be explained in the post.** The
school handed out a form pre-shaped for selling to relatives — one sheet, family names expected.
Brad treated it as a territory.

### The product

> "The bags were sandwich bags and freexer bags. No idea what the cost was or the schools cut."

Sandwich bags and freezer bags. **No unit price, no school margin, no total raised.** Brad does not
know these and they must not be estimated.

### The bike and the route

> "I lived 6KM out of town on a farm that grew 10 acres of raspberries and a poultry arm of 30,000
> chickens."

**Home: a farm 6km out of Hastings. 10 acres of raspberries, 30,000 chickens.** Entirely new to the
project record and the best scene-setting detail in either interview.

> "I covered the bulk of the suburbs in Hastings that felt safe - looking for well mown lawns as a
> sign of kindness and safety."

**"Well mown lawns as a sign of kindness and safety."** An eleven-year-old running a qualification
heuristic on his territory. **This is the single strongest line in either interview** — it is
prospecting, it is a child's logic, and it is funny and slightly sad at once. Do not explain it.

> "After schjool every day I hit the streets on my bike and then the weekends too. At the end of the
> sales window each day, I would have to ride 6-7Km home, depending on where my last house call had
> landed."

**A 6–7km ride home at the end of every selling day**, distance varying by where he finished.

> "I had a Raleigh ten speed bike, light blue and silver."

**Raleigh ten speed, light blue and silver.**

> "Samples were in my school bag, along with my order taking book. I had to use a book to store all
> the sales as the single sheet distributed by the school was never going to cut it. I wanted that
> calculator."

**He outgrew the school's form and moved to his own book.** The decision was made *in advance* —
"was never going to cut it" — not after filling the sheet. **This is the second-strongest detail in
the file** and it is the moment the story stops being about a school fundraiser.

### The daily shape

> "after school every day and weekends. I don't think my parents were fully aware as to the extent
> of my drive and ambition to secure the calculator."

**Every weekday after school, plus weekends, over roughly 2.5 months** (duration from the prior
record, not re-confirmed this interview — see open questions). **Parents not fully aware of the
extent.** Alone, on a bike, across a town, at eleven.

### Repeat customers — the four-beat arc does not apply

> "No it was a single sale product. Once a signature was colected I promptly moved on to find more
> buyers."

**Direct answer: no repeat business. One sale per door, then move on.**

**This kills the assumption carried in `NEXT-SESSION-PROMPT.md:104-107`**, which held that the
four-beat mythic arc (entry → repetition → naming → belonging) fitted the plastic bag story via "a
route, repeat customers, becoming the bag kid." **There was no route and there were no repeat
customers.** The repetition beat is the ride home, not the customer.

**Consequence for drafting:** the arc's repetition beat has to be carried by the daily 6–7km ride
and the qualification of streets, not by returning to buyers. The belonging beat is the assembly.
**Do not manufacture repeat customers to fit the structure.**

### The no's

> "Getting a no had no impact on me whatsoever, and I knew it was only a matter of time before I got
> a yes. So I just kept going, and don't recall anyone ever being an ass to a young boy trying to
> get ahead in life."

**No door-slam material exists.** Brad recalls no hostility at all. **Do not invent a rejection
scene** — it is the obvious dramatic temptation in this story and there is nothing behind it.

### The numbers — see "The figure problem" below

> "There were approximately 280 students and when you account for those that never sold any, likely
> around half, that would mean over 100 sales were made by me."

### Being the bag kid

> "There was an awards ceremony at a whole school assembly. I went up to get my prize, with parents
> in the audience watching."

**A whole-school assembly, parents present.** No nickname, no wall tally, no teacher comment
recalled — the "did anyone call you something" question returned nothing.

### The calculator

> "Casio - and to be honest, I don't think I ever got the real value out of it. I was far more
> interested in the casio calculator that had a space invaders game built into it. I think the
> scientific calculator sat on my shelf and got very little use, at that stage I had no idea what a
> cosine was, or why I needed to square a number.."

**A Casio. He wanted the other Casio — the one with Space Invaders in it.** It sat on a shelf,
barely used. He did not know what a cosine was.

**Assessment: this is the ending.** 2.5 months, every day after school, a 6–7km ride home in the
dark, for a prize he had no use for and did not especially want once he had it. **The story's
subject is the wanting, not the calculator, and the post must not say so.** The bare fact of the
unused calculator on the shelf carries it — the same move as the *Fort Commander* badge in Brad's
own style example, inverted.

### Parents

> "I think by that stage of my life, my parents had learned to just hold on, and hope for the best!"

Offered as a joke. **Usable as the narrator's aside; it is the one place a dry line fits.**

## The figure problem — unresolved, blocks the number only

**Two incompatible claims are now in the record.**

| Source | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| `design/05-younger-years-edit-prompt.md:58` | "just shy of selling more than the entire school combined sales" |
| This interview, answer 15 | "over 100 sales were made by me" |

**These are not the same claim** and neither is currently publishable.

**Why the 100 fails.** It is not a remembered figure. Brad's own working: ~280 students, assume
about half sold nothing, therefore he made over 100 sales. That is **a headcount multiplied by an
assumed participation rate to produce a sales figure** — precisely the derivation barred by the
standing rule (*never combine, divide or compound two separately-given figures into a third*) and by
`design/09-blog-voice.md` §4.2.3. The 280 is a remembered number; the 100 is arithmetic performed in
the interview, and the arithmetic does not even support the conclusion — it estimates *other
students' non-participation*, which says nothing about Brad's own tally.

**Why the comparative also fails as written.** "Just shy of selling more than the entire school
combined" is an extraordinary claim with no source. It may well be what was announced at the
assembly, in which case it is a real memory of a real statement — but the interview did not confirm
that and Brad did not repeat it.

### RESOLVED — 15 August 2026

Brad, on being pressed for the source:

> "the school principal told my Mum I nearly outsold the entire school."

**The claim is sourced, attributed, and publishable.** It is not Brad's assessment of his own
performance — it is **a statement by the school principal, made to Brad's mother.**

**Why this resolves the block completely:**

1. **It is attributed to a third party in a position to know.** The principal held the tally; Brad
   did not. §7's *let the customers do the praising* now applies in Brad's favour — the most
   extraordinary claim in either childhood story is the one he is not making himself.
2. **It explains the gap that made the figure suspect.** Brad could not produce his own tally
   because **he was never given one** — he heard the outcome secondhand, as a reported remark. The
   "over 100" reconstruction in answer 15 was him trying to rebuild a number he never held. That
   inconsistency is now explained rather than merely noted.
3. **It is categorically not derived.** No arithmetic, no combination of figures. A remembered
   sentence.

**Approved form of the claim:** the principal's statement, attributed as such. **Not** as a bare
assertion of sales volume in Brad's own voice.

**Still barred:** the "over 100 sales" reconstruction (derived — see above), any unit price, the
school's cut, and any total raised. **Attributing the comparative does not license quantifying it.**

**Drafting note.** Brad heard this as a child, secondhand, in an adult conversation about him —
not as a figure he was handed. **The register should carry it as a scene rather than a statistic**:
the principal telling his mother, Brad present. Confirm the setting with Brad before drafting — the
assembly, the school gate, or the phone are three different scenes and only he knows which.

**Precedent applied:** [[verify-dictated-figures-before-locking]] — the disagreement between "just
shy of the whole school" and "over 100" was a misspeak in the second, not a correction to the first,
and pressing once produced a source that neither dictated version contained.
