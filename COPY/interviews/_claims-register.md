# Claims register

**Every claim from the Phase 2 interviews, one row each.** This replaces the `deep-interview` skill's
`_summary.md`, deliberately — a prose synthesis is where drafted copy sneaks in and where a sentence
starts getting flattered. Rows, not paragraphs.

**Updated:** Friday 14 August 2026. **Now covers the whole career.** Tiers A/B/C and the D-actions
cover the blocker round and the 2015–2025 education role. **Tier E, added 14 August 2026, covers the
four pre-education roles** — 99 Corporation, Xplore, Adplus/Tracta and Canwest Media — which were
interviewed after the original register was written.

**Evidence status vocabulary** is defined in `_index.md`. **Publishable** is a separate axis from
accuracy: a claim can be true, evidenced and still unpublishable.

**Why Tier E is separate rather than merged into A/B/C.** The A-numbers are referenced by number
across a dozen files and in `_open-questions.md`; renumbering them to interleave four roles
chronologically would break every one of those references for no gain. **Tier E carries its own
publishable/not-publishable column instead of being split across three tiers** — the pre-education
material is smaller, and splitting forty rows across three tables would scatter each role. **The bars
are the point, and they are in the same table as the claims they bar.**

### Visual evidence — the bars, written before the content arrives

**Added 14 August 2026 (D18).** Brad has offered *"other evidence of design wotk and visuals. I have
some content saved."* **This is a new evidence category and the archive has no rule covering it.
Every discipline this project has built is about words** — verbatim quotes, hedges that must survive,
figures that may not be combined. **None of it transfers automatically to an image, and images make
claims faster than sentences do.**

**The four bars, in the order they are most likely to be breached:**

1. **An image asserts authorship unless it says otherwise.** A visual on a personal site reads as *I
   made this* by default. **Where Brad directed rather than designed — E5 and every equivalent — the
   credit must be on the page, not in the archive.** "Design: Coast Design" is four words and closes
   it. **Without the credit, the image makes a claim the register bars in text**, which is the
   loophole to watch for.
2. **Third-party marks are not Brad's to publish.** 99 Corporation's brand, a client's logo, a
   website he built for someone else — **these belong to businesses that have not agreed to appear.**
   §1.9's naming ruling governs *names*; **it does not extend to reproducing a company's visual
   identity**, which is a further step and needs its own permission. **Curda's consent does not cover
   99 Corporation's marks.**
3. **Proportion, and this is the one most likely to go wrong quietly.**
   `references-not-the-focus` is a standing rule of this project, and **visuals break it more easily
   than text because they win attention without asking.** **The argument of this site is
   land-keep-grow, evidenced by retention.** A gallery of design work argues something else
   entirely — that Brad is a creative practitioner — and **the target role is account management, not
   design.** **Visuals appear in service of a numbered row or they do not appear.**
4. **A "before" is worth more than an "after".** A finished artefact is a portfolio piece and proves
   nothing about Brad's judgement. **The same artefact against what preceded it is evidence of a
   decision he made.** **Where only the after exists, weight it down accordingly** — and never let a
   single-state image carry a claim about improvement.

**One further check before any visual ships:** the education employer is never named in site output,
**and that constraint applies to logos, screenshots and document letterheads exactly as it applies to
text.** Anything from the 2015–2025 decade needs checking pixel by pixel, not skimmed.
