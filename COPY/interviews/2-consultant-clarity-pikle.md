## What these answers change

**Four of the five answers reduce what this role may claim. None expand it.** That is the correct
outcome for a currency proof, but it must be written down plainly so Phase 3 does not re-inflate it.

### 1. Sweat equity is not consultancy, and the CV currently says it is

`bradfriisresumemaster20260806.md` line 10 titles this **"Independent Consultant · Clarity Engine
AI"** and line 11 opens **"Consultancy work for Pikle"**. **Sweat equity is unpaid work in exchange
for a stake.** A reader takes "consultancy work for" to mean a paid engagement with a client.

**Ruling: the word "client" is barred for Pikle.** So is any phrasing implying Pikle retained,
engaged or paid him.

**This is not a reason to hide it.** Sweat equity in a startup is honest and common, and taking
equity rather than fees is a *stronger* signal than a small invoice — it says he backed his own
judgement. **The honest sentence is also the better one**, the same finding as contradiction 4.3.
**But it must be Brad's call whether to say "sweat equity" on the page**, because it also discloses
he was not being paid. Flagged, not decided.

### 2. The plural claim is now disproven, not merely unevidenced

CV line 11: *"alongside positioning and customer experience engagements with early-stage New Zealand
software companies"* — **plural, and it is not true.** Brad's answer: the only other work was his
own.

**Ruling: that clause must be cut from the CV, not softened.** This is a stronger finding than the
usual unevidenced-plural: the interview did not fail to find instances, it established there are
none. **One company. Say one company.**

**Logged as a new contradiction — the first found in a CV claim about the present.** All prior
contradictions concerned events over a decade old.

### 3. "Ramp up for go to market" — the work was pre-launch, and that caps it permanently

Brad's own framing: **startup mode, ramping up to go to market.** So the positioning, pricing,
lead channels, commercial plan and console audit were all built **before there was a market result
to measure.**

**Ruling: this role can never supply outcome evidence, and the constraint is now factual rather
than merely a depth ruling.** No adoption metric, no revenue, no retention. **Anything on the site
that implies this work produced a commercial result is invented.**

**"Helping deliver" is Brad's own hedge and survives verbatim.** Not "led", not "drove", not
"owned". He was one contributor to a go-to-market effort, not its author.

### 4. Clarity Engine AI is a brand, not an entity

**Not registered.** So it may not carry a company-shaped description, and Brad may not be given a
title *at* it — no "Founder, Clarity Engine AI", no "Director".

**The "AI" in the name is now the single largest misreading risk on the site.** An unregistered
brand with AI in its name, on a hire-me page, in a year when every reader assumes an AI product:
**the default read is that he has built and shipped one. He has not.**

**Ruling: the copy must actively prevent that read, not merely avoid asserting it.** Avoiding the
claim is insufficient when the name itself makes the claim. **Standing constraint, now with a named
mechanism.**

### 5. Paused, not running — and the site must not imply otherwise

**One paying client. Founder has a personal issue. Paused 4–6 months from August 2026** — so
roughly **December 2026 to February 2027**, and possibly not resuming.

**This is the answer that undermines the role's entire purpose.** It was kept on the page to prove
Brad is *currently* active. **He is not currently active in it.** A "September 2025 to present"
date range on a paused project is not false, but it implies ongoing work that has stopped.

**Two things are barred outright:**
- **Any present-tense claim of live engagement.** "Currently consulting", "working with", "advising".
- **Naming or characterising the founder's personal issue.** It is a third party's private
  circumstance, disclosed to Brad in confidence. **It never reaches the site in any form, including
  as an unattributed reason for a pause.** Not Brad's information to publish.

**The pause itself need not be published either.** Silence is honest; a false present tense is not.

### The "one paying client" ambiguity — unresolved

Answer 5 says **Pikle has one paying client**. Read the other way, it could mean **Brad has one
paying client**. These are opposite facts: the first is Pikle's traction, the second would mean
Brad has paid consulting work he has not otherwise mentioned — and would contradict answer 1.

**Reading adopted: Pikle has one paying customer.** It fits "startup mode… ramp up for go to
market" and it fits sweat equity. **Not published either way until Brad confirms.** If it is
Pikle's customer, **it is Pikle's fact and not Brad's to claim as a result.**

### The constraint this creates, and it is severe

**This is now the highest-risk material in the archive for the no-AI-product rule.**

The facts are: an unregistered brand called **Clarity Engine AI**; eight months immersed in AI;
custom GPTs built and released to businesses. **A reader assembles that into "he has an AI
company with shipped products" without any single sentence claiming it.**

**What is true:** he taught himself AI tooling and delivered two working builds to two small
businesses.
**What is not true and must be actively prevented:** that he founded an AI company, shipped a
product, or has commercial AI revenue.

**Never publish these together as a portfolio under the Clarity Engine AI name.** The brand plus
the builds implies a company with a product line. **They are self-directed work and two client
builds, and the copy must keep them that shape.**
