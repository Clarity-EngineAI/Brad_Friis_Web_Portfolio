### The sixth move — and it is a different class from the other five

**Brad licensed the farming database to other telemarketing companies and businesses, including
Nielsen** — firms that wanted the rural data and did not have it.

**Note what it also is: selling to competitors.** The buyers were other telemarketing firms. **That
is a commercial judgement most sales managers would not make** — it requires seeing the database as
an asset with a market rather than as a competitive moat.

**Ruling: this belongs alongside the five moves, not beneath them.** It strengthens the structural
finding, and it is now **six moves, one role.**

## Answer 6 — the two figures, and they supersede "more than doubled"

**Given 13 August 2026, verbatim:**

> "at 99 Corporation it is very safe to say I increased the customer base by 70% and income by 120% -
> easily."

### These are better claims than the one they replace, for a specific reason

**"More than doubled" was a single business-level outcome with no attribution.** These are **two
figures, and they separate customer count from revenue** — which is exactly the separation the target
role turns on.

| Figure | What it measures | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer base +70%** | **How many customers** | The acquisition half |
| **Income +120%** | **How much revenue** | The value half |

**The two numbers do not match, and the gap is the finding.** Customers rose 70 per cent; income rose
120 per cent. **Revenue grew at roughly 1.7× the rate of the customer count** — meaning the average
customer became substantially more valuable during his tenure.

**That is upsell, expressed in arithmetic, and it is the single most direct evidence for the target
role in the entire archive.** `brad-target-role-account-management` records that Brad wants retention
plus upsell commission and that the copy must argue land-keep-grow. **The six commercial moves
explain the mechanism; this gap is the result.**

**The relationship is consistent with everything else in the file.** Set-up costs, script-writing
charges, higher hourly rates, volume-scaled pricing and the data licensing all raise revenue per
customer without adding customers. **Six moves that grow account value, and a revenue line growing
faster than the customer count. The story is internally coherent** — which is itself a mark in favour
of the figures being real recollection rather than reconstruction.

**Do not compute the per-customer uplift.** 120 ÷ 70 invites "average customer value up ~29 per
cent", and that is an invented figure of exactly the class disclaimed three times in this project.
**Brad gave two figures. Publish two figures. The reader can see the gap without being handed a
third number.**

### Status of "more than doubled"

**Superseded, but not contradicted.** "More than doubled" and "income up 120 per cent" are the same
claim at different resolutions — 120 per cent *is* more than double.

**Ruling: publish the 120 per cent, retire "more than doubled" as a phrasing.** It was a hedge
offered when the 400 per cent collapsed; Brad has now given the number he actually stands behind.
**Do not publish both — a reader who sees them together will wonder why the vaguer one was needed.**

### Evidence status — and the hedge that must survive

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | Increased the customer base by 70 per cent and income by 120 per cent during his tenure |
| Verbatim | "very safe to say I increased the customer base by 70% and income by 120% - easily" |
| Evidence status | **Vouched (Brad), hedged twice — "very safe to say" and "easily".** **Not measured.** No document is claimed and none has been produced |
| Publishable | **Yes.** Two figures, separately. **Never combined, never divided, never compounded** |

**"Very safe to say" and "easily" are Brad's own hedges and they are doing real work.** They signal a
conservative floor rather than a precise measurement. **The honest publishable form keeps that shape
— "increased the customer base by around 70 per cent and income by around 120 per cent" — and never
hardens it to "by exactly" or inflates it to "by over".**

**Note the direction of the hedging, because it is unusual and it matters.** Brad is describing these
as *floors* — the numbers he is certain he can defend, not his best estimate. **This is the
`press-once-when-brad-understates` pattern in its clearest form yet.** He disclaimed a 400 per cent
figure he could not source, then volunteered two he can. **That sequence makes these more credible,
not less.**

### The attribution question — and this time Brad claimed it himself

**Every prior figure in this role was a business outcome: "the business more than doubled."** This one
is stated in the first person — **"I increased the customer base… and income."**

**That is a stronger claim and it needs a correspondingly clear bar.** Brad managed sales and
marketing for the business and made six commercial changes. **He is entitled to claim the outcome of
the function he ran.** He is not entitled to claim the whole business's performance, and the copy
must not drift from *the customer base and income of the business he ran sales and marketing for*
into *the company's growth*.

**Retrieval route worth one question, no more.** Customer counts and revenue growth over a
2013–2015 period would sit in the company's records, which Brad no longer has access to. **These
publish as vouched, and that is sufficient** — the project's standard has never required documentary
proof for a first-person recollection Brad will stand behind.

## Answer 2 — retention. The question the whole sweep exists to settle

**Given 13 August 2026, verbatim:**

> "Yes I kept the national accounts as the person who delivered the billing and contract
> negotiations, and repeat business."

### Why this is the most consequential answer of the session

**The working assumption going in — stated at the top of this sweep — was that the pre-education
career was business development, i.e. new client acquisition, which is the opposite motion to the
target role.** That assumption is now wrong.

Brad did not hand the accounts on after winning them. **He held them, and he held them through the
three functions that define account management:**

| Function | Brad's words | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Billing** | "delivered the billing" | Ongoing commercial ownership, not a handover |
| **Contract negotiations** | "contract negotiations" | Renewal and terms — the retention motion itself |
| **Repeat business** | "repeat business" | The customer bought again |

**Ruling: the pre-education career is not business development. It is land-keep-grow, and it always
was.** Combined with the five pricing moves above, 99 Corporation is a complete account-management
role that both of Brad's public documents describe as a sales job.

**This extends the land-keep-grow story from ten years to at least twelve, and probably further
back** — the same question must now be put to Xplore, Adplus/Tracta and Canwest, where the same
assumption may be equally wrong. **`_index.md` records this as the sweep's governing question.**

### The contradiction it creates against Brad's own CV

`bradfriisresumemaster20260806.md` line 27 titles this **"National Sales Manager"** and gives it a
single bullet: *"Won and managed national accounts, scoping business requirements and delivering
end-to-end solutions."*

**"Won and managed" buries the finding.** It reads as *won, then administered*. What actually
happened was **won, priced, contracted, renewed and resold** — and the CV bullet gives that four
words in a sentence mostly about winning.

**LinkedIn is worse.** Line 63 leads with *"national positioning and client acquisition
strategies"* — acquisition, the wrong motion entirely — and line 65 reduces the deposit and volume
pricing restructure to *"innovative billing and invoicing systems to mitigate business risks."*

**Logged as contradiction 4.15. Both documents understate the role in the same direction, and it is
the direction that matters most for the target role.**

### What may be claimed

**Publishable:**
- Held the national accounts he won, owning billing, contract negotiation and repeat business.
- Restructured how the business charged: priced the unpriced, raised rates, secured cash up front,

**Barred:**
- **Any retention figure.** No rate, no count, no duration. **He said he kept them; he did not say

- **Any implication he was sole owner of those relationships.** He said he was "the person who
  delivered" those functions — that is what is claimable.
- **"Repeat business" turned into "grew the accounts."** Repeat business is a customer buying

### The follow-ups this answer earns

1. **Which accounts specifically, and for how long?** LexisNexis for two years is a materially

2. **Did any account grow in value under him?** Distinct from repeat business. **This is the upsell

3. **Did he negotiate a renewal against a competitor or a budget cut?** The education role has two

| **Ethical discomfort with some clients represented** | **Yes — and it is the strongest of the three** | See below |

### The ethical objection is the valuable one, and it corroborates the whole positioning

**Brad left a job partly because he did not want to sell for clients whose products ran chemicals
into New Zealand rivers.** He was not the manufacturer, not the polluter, and not obliged to care —
he ran the lead generation. **He objected anyway, and left.**

**This matters beyond the anecdote, because it independently corroborates the positioning the
project has been building from the other end.** The archive already establishes that Brad's method
is reading what a customer actually needs and building a solution to it — `every-school-is-different.md`,
the 108 interviews, the SIS integration built to remove a customer's worst job. **This is the same
disposition stated negatively: he will not sell something he does not believe serves the buyer.**

**A hirer for an account management role is buying exactly that.** Retention depends on a customer
believing they are not being sold to against their interest. **A candidate who once left a job over
it is making a credible claim rather than an aspirational one.**

**It also explains the shape of his career, which is otherwise a puzzle.** He moved from outbound
direct marketing straight into a decade of consultative education work — and stayed. **The move now
reads as a deliberate correction, not a drift.**

### Handling rules — this is the most easily mishandled material in the archive

**Never name the clients or the industry participants.** Brad named no company and must not be led
to. The claim is about the *kind* of work, not about identifiable businesses.

**Never state it as an accusation.** *"Some of the businesses we represented were not as ethical as
I would like"* is Brad's own careful phrasing and it is careful for a reason — these are former
clients of a former employer. **His hedge survives verbatim. Do not sharpen it into "sold
pollutants" or "environmentally damaging".**

**Never make it a virtue boast.** *"I left a job on principle"* as a headline is exactly the
self-assessment class that produced six unpublishable lines in the education interview under §7.
**It publishes as a fact in a career narrative, if at all — never as a character claim about
himself.**

**Never pair it with the achilles injury.** Combining them invites the read that the principle was
retrofitted to a medical exit. **Brad gave the injury first and the objection second, unprompted —
but the site does not owe a reader his full reasoning.**

**The safest publishable form is the career-shape one:** he moved out of outbound direct marketing
into consultative work, and stayed there for a decade. **That sentence is true, carries the meaning,
names nobody, and boasts about nothing.**

### What it does not do
