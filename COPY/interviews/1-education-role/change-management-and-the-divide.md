# Change management, and the leadership/faculty divide

**In master resume:** **no.** The resume describes activities — workshops, onboarding, adoption. It
never names the problem those activities existed to solve.
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026. This answer came unprompted, in response to a request for
think-tank specifics, and it is the most important thing he has said this session.

---

## Key points

**This is not another capability. It is the frame every other capability in this role sits inside.**

Asked repeatedly for figures and instances, Brad instead described the two problems his decade was
actually about. Both are stated plainly and neither is on any document in this project:

1. **Change management with a workforce that was not ready for digital**, much of it near the end of
   long careers.
2. **Standing between leadership teams and their faculties**, in schools where the two were divided.

**Why this reframes the session.** Every artefact collected so far — the 108 interviews, the tiered
course library, the 600-teacher network, the conference sponsorship, the week in Queensland, the
principal think tanks — has been recorded as an adoption or commercial mechanism. **They are all
answers to these two problems.** The programmes are the *how*; this is the *why*, and the site
currently publishes neither.

**It also supplies the missing sentence in the site's argument.** `design/01-positioning-brief.md`
§11.2 committed the site to leading with *what Brad knows*. Until now this session has produced things
Brad *did*. This is the first thing he has articulated that is genuinely knowledge — a diagnosis of a
sector, held for ten years, that explains why he built what he built.

---

## Problem one — teaching the teachers to teach differently

> "We're dealing with a workforce of various ages, but a lot of them at the top end near the ends of
> their careers. They've learned to teach with the chalkboard, and digital was rolling in faster than
> they could handle."

> "A lot of my job was in teaching teachers how to teach in a digital capacity and drive engagement and
> genuine personalised learning for students, as the landscape changed in the education sector."

**The job was not software training. It was pedagogy transition, with software underneath it.** A
teacher at the end of a thirty-year career being asked to move to digital is not solving a software
problem — they are being asked to change how they do the thing they have been good at for decades, in
front of students, in public, with no guarantee they will be as good at the new version.

**This explains the resistance the Bergh letter describes**, and it explains why Brad's answer to it
was 108 one-to-one conversations rather than a training session. **You cannot do that in a room.**

**How he did it, in one sentence of his own:**

> "…providing evidence of the value of moving to digital and using it instead of a 'this is how we've
> always done it' attitude."

**Evidence, against habit.** That is the whole method, and it is the same instinct as everything else
in this role: he did not argue, he did not mandate, he demonstrated value until the habit stopped
being defensible. **It is also the same standard this project holds Brad's own copy to** — claims
carry evidence or they do not publish. The consistency is real and worth noting.

### The register warning

**"This is how we've always done it" is Brad quoting the resistance, and it is excellent.** It is
specific, recognisable to anyone who has worked in an institution, and it does the work without any
adjective.

But the surrounding material is the most abstract-sounding of the session — *change management*,
*driving engagement*, *genuine personalised learning*. **Those are sector-standard phrases and they
will read as filler on a site that bans exactly this register.** §7 bans "any sentence a hundred other
candidates could have written", and "change management was a large part of my job" is one of them.

**The rescue is the concrete detail he already gave: the chalkboard.** *Teachers who learned to teach
with a chalkboard, being asked to go digital faster than they could handle* is a picture. *Change
management* is a category. Phase 3 must use the first and avoid the second.

## Problem two — the divide between leadership and faculty

> "Another part of my job was intersecting between leadership teams and their faculties, the teachers.
> Quite often in schools, there was a major divide between leadership and the teachers in the
> classrooms. And part of my job was to work between the two of them and try to get everyone on the
> same page."

**This is the most commercially valuable observation Brad has made, and he made it last and quietly.**

**Why it matters more than it sounds.** In a school, leadership buys and teachers use. When those two
groups are divided, the vendor is exposed on both sides at once: leadership has signed for something
its staff resent, and the staff resent a decision made above them. **That is the standard shape of a
renewal that fails** — not a product failure, an alignment failure.

**Brad positioned himself in the gap.** Not as leadership's supplier, and not as the teachers'
advocate, but as the party working between them. Three consequences, none of which he stated:

1. **It is why the accounts stayed.** An account where the buyer and the users are aligned does not
   churn on a change of principal or a tight budget year.
2. **It is a structural explanation for the retention record**, which the site currently asserts and
   evidences only by outcome. This is the mechanism.
3. **It is transferable, and that is what a hiring manager needs.** Every enterprise account has this
   divide — the executive who signs and the team who use it. **Schools are just an unusually visible
   version.** A candidate who can name that dynamic and say what he does about it is describing
   account management at a level most CVs never reach.

**This is the strongest available answer to the hunter-or-farmer objection** at §3.1. The objection is
that accounts may have stayed because switching a student management system is painful. The answer:
they stayed because the people who signed and the people who used it were brought onto the same page,
deliberately, by someone whose job he defined as doing exactly that.

## The empathy answer, and what it is worth

**Added later in the same session, 13 August 2026.**

> "I had to carry a deep sense of empathy and compassion for the nontechnical and the older teachers
> because the digital revolution was so new to them and so challenging and difficult. So teaching them
> to use the tools effectively was one of my greatest achievements because ultimately it led to student
> achievement and happy classrooms."

**Two halves, and they publish very differently.**

**The unpublishable half.** *I had to carry a deep sense of empathy and compassion* is a self-adjective,
and *one of my greatest achievements* is a self-assessment. Both are the §7 banned class, and the
second is specifically the "let the customers do the praising" rule. **Brad has now supplied five
self-assessments today** — "hugely successful", "performing outstandingly", "brilliant branding",
"became really good at", and this. **All five are the same instinct and all five are unpublishable.**
Recorded together so Phase 3 recognises the pattern rather than re-litigating each one.

**The publishable half, and it is a real gain.** *It led to student achievement and happy classrooms*
names the outcome Brad was actually working toward, and it is the first time this session that he has
named an end state beyond the commercial one. It also matches Bergh's published letter, which carries
the 95 per cent pass rate — **an outcome measured in student results, not in licences.**

**Why this matters for the target role.** A candidate for a retention-and-growth seat who can say what
the customer's customer got is arguing at a level above product usage. The chain is: teachers adopted
→ classrooms worked → students achieved → the school renewed. **The site currently publishes only the
last link.**

**The empathy claim itself is not lost, it just cannot be self-stated.** Bergh's published sentence —
*"One of Brad's most remarkable qualities is his ability to support teachers, particularly those
resistant to change"* — is the same claim in a third party's words, which is where it belongs.

## What this changes for Phase 3

**Recorded as structure, not as copy.** Per the standing instruction, no lines are drafted here.

1. **The role has a stated problem for the first time.** Every version of this decade on the site is a
   list of things Brad did. None of them says what he was up against. A capability panel that opens
   with the chalkboard and the divide has somewhere to put every mechanism collected this session.
2. **The buyer/user divide generalises off the education sector**, which matters because Brad has twice
   rejected copy for fencing him into edtech or SaaS. **This is sector-independent by nature** — the
   signer and the users are divided in every enterprise account. It may be the frame that lets the
   decade argue for a role outside education without losing its specifics.
3. **It supplies the "what I know" the knowledge-led architecture has been short of.** §11.6 left open
   whether the ramp method leads the homepage. This is a second candidate, and it is inside one role.

## Open questions

1. **A named instance of the divide.** The claim is general and Brad's first answers are consistently
   general. **Which school, what was the disagreement, what did he actually do to close it?** Per the
   standing rule: ask for the occasion, not the capability. **Highest-value follow-up on this page.**
2. **Did closing a divide ever save or grow an account?** If one of the retained schools was retained
   *because* leadership and faculty were realigned, this stops being a description of a job and
   becomes a retention mechanism with an outcome attached.
3. **Was this recognised by anyone at the time?** A principal or a leadership member who would say "he
   was the person who got us and our staff onto the same page" would be an unusually strong reference
   line, and different in kind from the two already published.
4. **Did the vendor know he was doing this?** It is well outside a reseller's contracted scope, and if
   the vendor valued it, that connects to the Australian territory award.
