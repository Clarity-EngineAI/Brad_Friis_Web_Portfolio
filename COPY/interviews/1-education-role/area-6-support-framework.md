## Key points

**The framework has two dimensions, and the brief's single word "triaged" only covers one of them.**

| Dimension | What it is | What it governs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Investment tier** | **Between 2 and 6 hours per month** | **How much** support a school bought |
| **Escalation** | By **urgency and type of issue** | **How fast** the support team had to respond |

**These are separable and Brad named both in one sentence without separating them.** Hours per month is
a *volume* commitment sold to the customer. Escalation by urgency and issue type is a *response-time
obligation* placed on the delivery team. A school could exhaust its hours and still be entitled to a
fast response on a critical issue; a school on the top tier did not get everything answered urgently.
**One is what the customer bought, the other is what the business owed.**

**It is the inside of the managed services agreement, not a second product.** The 2–6 hours per month is
what the $3,240–$6,480 band at A32 purchased. Area 6 and area 3 are one thing seen from two sides —
area 3 is the commercial face, area 6 is the delivery face. **They must never be published as two
achievements, and never counted as two revenue lines.** See C19.

**Brad designed it and implemented it through other people.**

> "yes I designed it and then worked with our technicians and their manager to implement it"

**That sentence is the most carefully bounded origination claim Brad has made in the archive, and he
made it without being pressed.** It states the design as his and the implementation as collaborative, in
the same breath, and it names whose collaboration — the technicians and their manager. **It is the exact
distinction the SIS integration required a footnote to enforce** (A29: originator, never "Brad built").
Here Brad drew it himself.

## The detail

Three answers, verbatim, in the order given:

> "yes it was based on investment (hours per month) and escalation based on urgency and type of issue"

> "between 2 and 6 hours per month"

> "escalation changed how quickly the support team were required to respond"

> "yes I designed it and then worked with our technicians and their manager to implement it"

### The band is 2 to 6 hours and the price band is $3,240 to $6,480 — do not compute the rate

The arithmetic is trivial and **it is barred**. $3,240 ÷ 24 hours and $6,480 ÷ 72 hours give two
different hourly rates, which would be a derived figure of exactly the class this project has disclaimed
twice, and it would also expose the vendor's cost of delivery. **Brad did not state an hourly rate, the
two bands were given in separate sessions answering different questions, and it has not been established
that the bottom of one band corresponds to the bottom of the other.** See C20.

**What may be said is that the agreement bought a defined monthly allocation of support hours, banded by
school size, and that the framework specified response times by urgency.** That carries the whole
commercial point with no invented number in it.

### "Escalation changed how quickly the support team were required to respond" — the word is *required*

**Brad's verb is the claim.** He did not say escalation meant urgent issues were *handled faster*, which
would be a description of effort. He said the team were **required** to respond within different times.
That is a **contractual or internal service obligation** — a commitment the business made and then had
to staff against.

**This is the second service-level mechanism in the archive**, after the SIS integration's 24-hour
refresh cycle (A29). Both are Brad specifying a time obligation and then making the business meet it.
**Neither is a sales claim.** They are operating claims, and a hiring manager reads them differently and
better.

### What is not known, and is not worth chasing

**The response times themselves.** How fast a top-urgency issue had to be answered — four hours, one
day, next business day — is not established. **Do not ask for it and do not estimate it.** The mechanism
publishes without the number, and an invented service level would be worse than no service level. It is
recorded as an open question against the retrieval route, not as a gap in the claim.

**The tier count.** Whether 2–6 hours was two bands, three or a continuum is not established. **Publish
the range as Brad gave it — "between two and six hours a month" — never as "three tiers" or any other
count.** Same discipline as A8's course structure, which publishes as structure and never as a course
count (C8).

**Whether it survived his departure.** Not asked. Adjacent to the settlement gag, and the handoff
instruction was explicit. **Do not ask this in any future session.**

scope, term, what the schools got, who delivered it?* and recorded it as *the original question, still

| --- | --- |

| Scope | **Support**, unbundled from the licence — the same activity that had been given away free |
| Term | **Still unanswered.** Monthly structure, annual prepayment (A22) is the billing shape, but the contract term is not established |

**Phase 3 can now describe the managed services product**, which it could not before. That was the
blocker area 3 recorded against itself.

**Area 3's open question 5 is also resolved.** It flagged that *"staggered billing" may be a separate
mechanism entirely, or the brief's term may be wrong*, noting annual-upfront billing is arguably the
opposite of staggered. **The brief's "staggered" almost certainly refers to the banded pricing** — three
different schools paying three different amounts on the same product — **not to a payment schedule.**
Recorded as a reading, not a confirmation; it does not need asking, because nothing publishes on the
word "staggered" either way.
