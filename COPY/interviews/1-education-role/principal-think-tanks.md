# The principal think tanks

**In master resume:** **no.** Absent entirely, from the resume, from LinkedIn, and from the site.
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026.

---

## Key points

> "I also coordinated a series of think tanks with leading high school principals in New Zealand to
> understand the education landscape and what was needed to drive change."

**This is the highest-altitude thing Brad has described so far, and it is on no document anywhere.**
Everything else in this role is Brad working on an account, a teacher, a conference or a campus. This
is Brad convening the leaders of a sector to work out where the sector is going.

**Three distinctions that make it a different class of evidence:**

1. **He convened, he did not attend.** "Coordinated a series" — he built the forum. Getting leading
   principals into a room repeatedly is access, and access at that level is not something a channel
   partner's GM is given. It is earned.
2. **The purpose was to understand, not to sell.** "To understand the education landscape and what was
   needed to drive change." No product in the sentence. **A vendor who convenes a sector's leadership
   to ask what the sector needs is doing category positioning**, and the goodwill and intelligence flow
   back regardless of what any individual principal buys.
3. **A series, not an event.** Repeated, which means the principals kept coming back. One think tank is
   an initiative; a series is a standing forum, and people at that level do not return to something
   that wastes their time.

**The pattern is now at five instances, and this one sits above the rest.** Course library, workshops,
conference sponsorship, the week in Queensland — each is *lead with their problem, let the product
follow*, applied to a customer or an audience. This applies it to **the whole sector's leadership**.

## Why this matters to the target role, stated plainly

Brad is arguing for an account management seat paying on retention and expansion. The standing risk in
that argument — the hunter-or-farmer objection at `design/01-positioning-brief.md` §3.1 — is that
retention might mean *pleasant and available* rather than *commercial*.

**Convening a sector's principals is neither pleasant nor available. It is strategic.** It is the
activity of someone operating on the market rather than inside a pipeline, and it is the kind of thing
a hiring manager reads as evidence of seniority rather than of service.

**It is also demand generation at the top of the funnel**, done in a form that never looks like selling
— which is the same move as the NASDAP sponsorship and the pedagogy-not-product workshops, and it is
now unmistakably a deliberate approach rather than a series of coincidences.

## The risk, recorded so Phase 3 does not walk into it

**This claim is the easiest thing in the whole interview to overstate, and the most damaging if
overstated.** "Think tanks with leading principals" is exactly the register of sentence that a sceptical
hiring manager tests, and the education sector in New Zealand is small enough that a reader may know
the people involved.

**It must publish with its specifics or not at all.** A vague version — *convened sector leaders to
shape the future of education* — is the banned class at §7: a sentence a hundred other candidates could
have written, performing seniority rather than evidencing it. **The specifics are the only thing that
makes it credible, and Brad has not supplied them yet.**

## Open questions

1. **How many sessions, and over what period?** "A series" needs a number.
2. **Roughly how many principals, and from where?** Nationwide or regional. Names are not needed for
   publication, but the scale is.
3. **What came out of them?** This is the question that decides whether it publishes. A think tank with
   no output is a meeting. If it produced a document, a product change, a vendor briefing, a piece of
   sector thinking — that is the artefact, and it may still exist.
4. **Did the vendor act on any of it?** Brad's LinkedIn line 43 claims he "analysed market trends to
   identify opportunities, contributing to product development and localisation". **If the think tanks
   fed that, the two claims lock together and each makes the other credible.** Currently they sit in
   separate documents with no stated connection.
5. **Would any principal vouch for it?** One named principal confirming the forum existed and was worth
   attending would move this from Unevidenced to Vouched in a single sentence. Note that Andrew Bergh
   is a high school principal who has already written a reference — **was he one of them?**
6. **Whose idea was it, and who paid?** Ownership evidence, same as the sponsorship.
