# Implementation fees, and the scaling principle behind the pricing

**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026, volunteered as "the last revenue stream that I generated" —
and carrying an unprompted correction to `area-3-managed-services.md`.

**The sixth revenue line of the session, and it arrives with the most precise figures Brad has given
about anything.**

---

## Key points

**Two prices, and they behave differently. Brad corrected the record twice in quick succession to get
this right — the final version is below and supersedes everything said before it.**

| Line | Price | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Implementation** | **$3,750** | **Flat. The standard fee for any school, regardless of size** |
| **Managed services** | **$3,240 – $6,480 per year** | **Scaled to the size of the school** |

**The correction, in his words:**

> "The three-seven-five-oh was the standard implementation fee for any school. The managed services
> agreements were between three-two-four-oh and six-four-eight-oh relative to the size of the school."

**Why the shape matters more than either number.** Getting a school onto the platform costs the same
work whatever its size, so it is priced flat. Supporting a school scales with the school, so it is
priced on a range. **The pricing follows the cost and value of the work rather than being applied
uniformly**, and that is a designed model rather than a rate someone picked.

**This also means the recurring line is the one that scales — the right way round.** A one-off fee that
grew with size would be worth little; a *recurring* line that grows with the customer compounds for as
long as the account is held. Brad held accounts for eight years and more.

### The correction changes what area 3 is

`area-3-managed-services.md` records the managed services vertical as a flat annual line worth
$40–50k. **That file did not know the agreements were size-scaled**, and the difference is not
cosmetic:

| | Flat pricing | Size-scaled pricing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it shows | A price was set | **A pricing model was designed** |
| Revenue behaviour | Fixed per customer | **Grows automatically as the customer grows** |
| Commercial class | A rate | **A structure** |

**Size-scaled pricing means the revenue line expanded on its own as schools grew** — and Brad has
already given the archive multiple accounts that grew substantially (Rototuna 600 → ~1,200, Elim's
second and third campuses, the five-campus account's sixth). **Those growth events were not only
licence expansion. They were managed services expansion too, automatically.**

**This is the strongest structural pricing evidence in the archive**, and it retro-fits the
land-keep-grow argument with a mechanism: he built the commercial model so that keeping an account and
growing an account were the same activity.

### The range corroborates the $40–50k, and that is worth noting

`area-3-managed-services.md` records the managed services vertical as worth **around $40,000–$50,000 a
year**. The per-school range is now **$3,240–$6,480**.

**The two figures are consistent with roughly eight to twelve schools on managed services agreements**,
depending on the size mix. **That is a plausible number and it was arrived at from two answers given
separately, hours apart, without Brad reconciling them.** Internal consistency of this kind is weak
evidence, but it is evidence — the figures were not constructed to agree.

**Do not publish the derived school count.** It is arithmetic on two hedged figures ("around forty to
fifty", "between three-two-four-oh and six-four-eight-oh"), and the size mix is unknown. **Ask Brad the
attach rate instead** — it is question 7 in the handoff.

### The pricing model, assembled

**Six revenue lines have now been recorded this session. Read together they are not a list — they are a
deliberate commercial architecture**, and this answer is the piece that reveals the shape:

| Line | Type | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licence | Per student, **rising annually** | Recurring |
| **Implementation** | **One-off, $3,750 flat** | **At the front** |
| **Managed services** | **$3,240–$6,480 a year, scaled by size, billed a year upfront** | Recurring |
| SIS integration | Service charge, ~$30k p.a. across the book | Recurring |
| Course materials + on-site training | Per sale | Ad hoc |
| Government-funded training | Third-party funded | Ad hoc |

**Every stage of a school's life with the product has a price attached to it** — onboarding, running,
data, training, growth. **That is a monetisation model, not a set of upsells**, and no document in the
project has ever described Brad as someone who builds one.

**Do not overstate the deliberateness.** Brad described these across six separate answers and did not
present them as a system. **Whether he designed the architecture or accumulated it is an open question**
— see `sis-integration-product.md` open question 5, which now applies to all six lines.

## The detail

> "And the last revenue stream that I generated was a one-off implementation cost of three thousand
> seven hundred and fifty dollars per school. For larger schools, it could go up to six thousand eight
> hundred dollars. And you've just reminded me that the managed services agreements were scaled to the
> size of the school. So the larger the school, the more they paid."

**Corrected by Brad immediately afterwards, and the correction governs:**

> "Correction. The three-seven-five-oh was the standard implementation fee for any school. The managed
> services agreements were between three-two-four-oh and six-four-eight-oh relative to the size of the
> school."

**The first version attached the upper figure to the wrong line.** Implementation is flat at $3,750; the
$3,240–$6,480 range belongs to managed services. **Brad volunteered the correction unprompted, which is
the second time this session he has tightened his own figures rather than letting a generous version
stand.** Recorded because it bears on how much weight his unverified figures can carry: **he corrects
downward and toward precision, unasked.**

### Why these figures are publishable when $10 and $23 are not

**A real distinction, and it should be applied deliberately rather than assumed.**

| Figure | Nature | Ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $10 / $23 per student | **What a specific school paid**, and different schools paid differently | **Barred** (C10). Customer-identifying |
| $3,750 implementation; $3,240–$6,480 managed services | **A rate card** — standard prices offered to the market, one flat and one banded by size | **Publishable in principle.** No individual school's spend is disclosed |

**A price list is not confidential in the way a negotiated contract is.** The implementation fee was
what any school of that size paid, which is exactly why it identifies none of them.

**But it is still the former employer's commercial information**, so it falls under the same ruling as
the $40–50k and the $30k — **D8, Brad's call, flagged rather than assumed.**

### What must not be done with them

**Do not multiply.** $3,750 across a book of schools produces an implementation-revenue total Brad did
not state. **The same bar as C14**, and the temptation is stronger here because the arithmetic is easy
and the number of schools is roughly knowable.

**Do not present $6,480 as the managed services price.** It is the top of a band. **And do not attach
either end of that band to the implementation fee** — that was Brad's own first-pass error, which he
corrected, and it must not be reintroduced.

**Do not imply these are current prices.** Historical, at a business he has left.

## Open questions

1. **Who set these prices?** **The same authority question as C13**, now with sharper figures attached.
   A rate card someone else wrote and Brad applied is a different claim from one he designed. **This
   question has now arisen against five separate claims and needs asking once, plainly.**
2. **What defined the size bands** for managed services — roll, campuses, negotiated case by case? And
   how many bands were there between $3,240 and $6,480?
3. **Was the implementation fee ever waived or discounted to win or hold an account?** If it was, that
   is a commercial negotiation instance and worth more than the fee itself.
4. **Why was implementation flat when managed services scaled?** If Brad can articulate the reasoning —
   fixed onboarding effort versus support load that grows with the school — **that is the clearest
   available evidence that he designed the model rather than applied it**, and it answers question 1 of
   the handoff directly.
5. **Was the scaling principle applied to the SIS integration charge as well?** If yes, the whole
   monetisation model scaled with the customer, which is a materially stronger single statement.
