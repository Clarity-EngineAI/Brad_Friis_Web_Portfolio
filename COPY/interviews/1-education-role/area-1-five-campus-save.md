# Capability area 1 — the five-campus save

**In master resume:** yes, line 21 — "Retained a five-campus account that was actively evaluating a
move to a competitor, then converted it into an expanded investment. Written reference available."
**On the site:** the outcome is published. **Brad's account of how he did it has never been recorded
anywhere until now.** That gap is why this area exists.
**The customer:** The Industry School, Queensland. Adrian Pilgrim, Teaching and Learning Manager, wrote
the 2025 reference.
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026.

---

## Key points

**The method has three moves, and the order is the whole story.**

1. **Go there.** Travelled to Queensland and spent **a week** across the different campuses.
2. **Work with both tiers before proposing anything.** Teachers *and* senior leadership teams —
   the users and the buyers, in the same visit.
3. **Leave, build, and come back to deliver it.** Built the strategy after the visit, then **returned
   to Queensland a second time** to deliver and teach it to everybody.

**Two trips, not one.** This is the detail that makes it a method rather than a rescue mission, and it
is the detail most likely to be lost in compression. He did not diagnose and pitch in the same week.
He diagnosed, went away, built, and came back. **The gap between the trips is where the credibility
is** — it is physical proof of *won't pitch before understanding the operation*, which
`design/01-positioning-brief.md` §11.3 question 9 records as one of Brad's two stated points of view.

**The site currently proves this refusal with a quote. It could prove it with an itinerary.**

## The detail

### What he did

> "The way that I managed to keep them on board was traveling over to Queensland and spending a week
> across the different campuses, working with the teachers and senior leadership teams to then build a
> strategy that I then went back to Queensland once I'd built it to deliver and teach everybody."

**A week, across five campuses, in a foreign market, for one account that had not yet decided to
stay.** That is a substantial commitment of time and travel budget made *before* the outcome was
known, and it was made for an account that was actively evaluating a competitor.

**The commercial reading, which Brad did not make.** A competitor in an evaluation can demo. A
competitor cannot spend a week inside five campuses learning how the customer actually operates, then
return with something built specifically for them. **The method is not persuasion — it is making the
switching decision look uninformed by comparison.** The incumbent who has been on site for a week
knows things the challenger cannot know from a proposal.

### Why this matches the letter exactly

Adrian Pilgrim's reference, already published: *"custom strategies and workarounds that fit our
operations"* — cited at `design/01-positioning-brief.md` §11.2 as the evidence for *pitch after you
understand the operation*.

**Brad has now supplied the activity behind the customer's sentence, and the two independently
describe the same thing.** Pilgrim says the strategies fit their operations. Brad says he spent a week
in their operations before writing the strategy. **Neither was written with reference to the other, and
they corroborate.** That is the strongest evidential structure on the site: a customer's written claim
and the vendor's account of the work, matching without having been reconciled.

The homepage quote — *"At a time when we were seriously considering moving away from Schoology, Brad
helped us see the platform in a new light"* — now also has a mechanism underneath it. "A new light" was
a week on their campuses.

### The pattern, now at four instances

This is the fourth independent instance of the same instinct, recorded across three files:

| Instance | The move |
| --- | --- |
| Course library | Built *on* the platform, teaching the platform — the product demonstrates itself |
| Conference workshops | Topics were personalised learning and student engagement, not the product |
| NASDAP sponsorship | The product underpinned the conference rather than exhibiting at it |
| **The five-campus save** | **A week inside the operation before a strategy was written** |

**Four instances is a method, not a temperament.** §11.1 correction 3 made exactly this argument about
sales enablement — *a ramp derived from one company is an anecdote; one derived from four businesses is
a method* — and the same logic applies here with more force, because these four sit inside a single
role and need no explaining across employers.

## The commercial close — answered 13 August 2026

**This was the highest-value open question on the page and in the whole project.** It is now answered.
Asked what the account bought *after* they decided to stay, Brad named four things.

> "What they bought from me next was paid consultancy and a managed services agreement for the year.
> And, of course, they renewed and opened another campus and included that in the mix."

| # | What they bought | Class of revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Paid consultancy** | Services revenue, new line — the account was previously licence-only |
| 2 | **A managed services agreement for the year** | **Recurring services revenue, annual term** |
| 3 | **A renewal** | Retained licence revenue |
| 4 | **Another campus, included in the mix** | Expanded licence revenue — a **sixth** campus, added to the single organisation-wide account |

**Four revenue events out of an account that had been evaluating an exit.** Two of them — the
consultancy and the managed services agreement — are revenue lines that did not exist at that account
before the save. That is the definition of the seat Brad is applying for: the customer stayed, and then
the account got bigger in more than one direction.

**The managed services agreement is the first named instance of capability area 3.** Area 3 was listed
as *not started*, and it carries the unattached 30 per cent uplift figure. It now has an occasion, at
the best-evidenced account in the archive. Whether this account is the *source* of the 30 per cent
figure is still open — see open question 2 below. **Do not assume it is.**

### The mechanism — what he actually built, and why it is the strongest commercial claim in the archive

Asked what the expansion was worth, Brad answered with what he built. The figure is still missing (open
question 2), but the mechanism he described is worth more to the site than the figure would be.

> "We also implemented a new strategy that linked all five campuses together sharing resources and
> groups, bringing the principals together and the instructional leaders into more close communication
> and interaction, which increased the use of the platform across all the campuses."

**Before:** five campuses buying the same platform, operating separately.
**After:** five campuses sharing resources and groups, with the principals and instructional leaders in
regular contact across campus lines, and **platform use up across all five**.

**The commercial reading, which Brad did not make.** *Superseded in part — see "The account structure"
below, which Brad volunteered immediately after this answer and which rules out the lock-in version of
this argument.* **The surviving and correct reading is value, not switching cost.**

Five campuses were already paying for the platform. They were not getting five campuses' worth of it —
each ran its own deployment, and the organisation's own leaders were not connected through the thing it
was buying. Brad changed what the product *was* for that customer: not five separate installations of
software, but the medium through which the organisation's principals and instructional leaders
coordinate. **Use went up across all five campuses**, which is the customer's own measure that the
change worked.

**He did not just retain the account. He made the product materially more valuable to the customer than
it had been when they were evaluating leaving — and then it renewed and bought more.** Retention is a
fact. Retention with a stated mechanism is a capability, and it is the one an account management
employer reads for. **This is the single strongest commercial argument produced by the interviews so
far.**

**Why the value reading beats the lock-in reading even where lock-in is available.** A hiring manager
reading *he made them hard to leave* hears a vendor tactic. Reading *he found five campuses paying for
something they were using separately, connected them through it, and usage rose everywhere* hears an
account manager who grew an account by making the customer better off. **The second is what happened
here, and it is what Phase 3 writes.**

**A second reading, equally publishable.** Increased platform use across all five campuses is the
leading indicator that made the renewal safe and the expansion natural. The sequence is causal and
Brad stated it in that order: link the campuses → use goes up → renewal, plus a sixth campus, plus two
new services lines. **That is a land-keep-grow argument told end to end, with a mechanism in the
middle**, and nothing else in the archive currently does that.

## The account structure — one organisation, one account, campuses inside it

**Volunteered by Brad, 13 August 2026, unprompted:**

> "The campuses were always paid for as one account. They were all under the same organization."

**This corrects an assumption this file made and should not have made.** The earlier reading here
treated the five campuses as separable — a competitor able to "pick off a campus". **That was wrong on
the commercials.** They were one organisation on one account throughout. A competitor was never
choosing between one campus and five; the whole organisation was the unit that could leave.

**The correction makes the risk larger, not smaller.** A five-campus organisation evaluating a move to
a competitor is a single decision that takes all five with it. There was no partial loss available and
no soft landing. **The account that was actively evaluating an exit was the entire organisation, and
Brad's week on the campuses was spent protecting all of it at once.** The site currently describes this
as "a five-campus account", which is accurate — and the thing at stake was that account entire.

**Two consequences for the argument, and they pull in opposite directions. Both must be recorded.**

**1. The cross-campus mechanism is now about value, not lock-in.** With one organisation on one
account, linking the campuses did not raise a switching cost that could otherwise have been paid
campus by campus. **The stickiness reading in the section above is weaker than it was first written.**
What the linking strategy did was make the platform more valuable to the customer — resources shared,
leaders coordinating, use up across all five. **That is the honest and still strong claim: he did not
trap the customer, he made the thing worth more to them.** For a hiring manager, that is the better
story anyway, and it is the one that survives scrutiny. **Phase 3 must write the value reading, not the
lock-in reading.**

**2. The sixth campus was an addition to an existing organisation-wide agreement**, not a new site
sale. That reduces the *pricing* question but does not remove it — it moves it. The question is no
longer "did they buy new licences at rate" but **what happened to the organisation's contract value
when a sixth campus was folded into it.** Open question 3 is rewritten accordingly.

**"Five-campus account" stays the correct description at the moment of the save**, and the sixth campus
belongs to the expansion. No contradiction against the published record — the count was right, and it
grew afterwards. Phase 3 may still choose to tell it as *five became six*, which is the stronger and
equally true sentence.

## What is still missing from this story

**The magnitude.** Asked twice what the expansion did to the account's value, Brad has answered with
composition and mechanism, not size. **Four revenue events are now named; none is sized.** Even a
multiple would do. Pressed once per the stopping rule; not pressed again.

**What the sixth campus did to the contract value.** Brad has confirmed the campuses were always one
account under one organisation, so the question is not rate-card pricing but what the organisation-wide
agreement was worth once a sixth campus was inside it. Asked in its original form, not yet answered.

**Who proposed the expansion.** Brad's phrase is "what they bought from me", which reads as his sale,
but he has not been asked directly whether he proposed the consultancy and the managed services
agreement or whether the customer requested them. **Upsell and a happy customer buying more are
different claims** and the site may only make the one that is true.

**The five campuses and the roll.** Still no figures: students, licences, or account value.

**Whether the strategy was reusable.** Unchanged from the original open question, and now more
pointed — the cross-campus linking strategy is exactly the kind of thing that becomes a template. A
strategy built for one customer is service; one reused across accounts is a product.
