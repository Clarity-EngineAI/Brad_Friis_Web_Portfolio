# Capability area 2 — onboarding, training and adoption

**In master resume:** yes, line 24 — "Built the onboarding, training and adoption programme, running
hands-on and remote workshops for non-technical staff across the customer base."
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026.

---

## Key points

**The resume line describes a service. The interview produced an act, a count and an outcome.** That
is the whole difference between what the site says now and what it could say.

| | Resume version | Interview version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What | "hands-on and remote workshops" | 108 one-to-one interviews with teachers |
| When | unstated | across the COVID-19 lockdown period |
| Outcome | none stated | naysayers converted to advocates, some to classroom champions |

**The conversion is the claim, not the count.** The 108 is impressive and it is the thing a reader can
verify the shape of. But 108 interviews is an input — it measures Brad's effort, not the customer's
change. *"Numerous teachers who until that point had been naysayers"* becoming advocates is an
outcome, and outcomes are what the site is short of.

**Adoption ran on three mechanisms, not one.** The 108 interviews are the individual layer. Two more
emerged unprompted and are recorded below: a **tiered course library** he built across the platform's
functional areas, and a **teacher community of around 600**. Together these are a system — one-to-one
for the resistant, self-serve for the willing, peer-to-peer for the converted — and the system is a
better argument than any single part of it.

## The detail

### The programme

108 one-to-one interviews with teachers, conducted across the COVID-19 lockdown period, across
multiple schools. Purpose, in Brad's words: to support them to use the tools more effectively, and to
support their students who were in lockdown.

**One-to-one, not group.** This is the detail that separates it from the resume's "workshops". A
workshop scales; 108 individual conversations do not. It is a deliberately expensive method, and the
expense is the point — the people who needed converting were not going to be converted in a room.

### The outcome

> "I managed to convert numerous teachers across different schools who until that point had been
> naysayers and quite negative into advocates of the platform, and some of them ended up becoming
> champions of the platform and performing outstandingly in the classroom with it."

**Three tiers in one sentence, and the tiering is real rather than rhetorical:**

1. **Naysayers, quite negative** — the starting state. Not neutral users; actively against.
2. **Advocates** — converted. The many.
3. **Champions, performing outstandingly in the classroom** — the few, who went past use into
   demonstrating it to others.

**Why this matters commercially, and it needs saying plainly because Brad did not say it.** A negative
teacher inside a school is a renewal risk with a voice. Every one converted to an advocate removes a
reason for that school not to renew and adds an internal person arguing to keep it. This is not a
training story — **it is the retention mechanism, described from the inside.** The site currently
argues retention from outcomes (the save, the ten years). This explains *how*.

**It also directly evidences the existing Bergh quote.** `COPY-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md` §6 publishes:
*"One of Brad's most remarkable qualities is his ability to support teachers, particularly those
resistant to change."* Until now that sentiment had no activity beneath it on the site. It does now,
and the activity has a number: 108.

### The course library — the self-serve layer

Built "countless courses" across the different strands of the platform. Named areas:

- curriculum management
- student engagement
- managing rubrics
- Gradebook outcomes
- different pedagogical approaches

**And every course was built at three levels — beginner, intermediate, advanced.**

> "Every time I would build a beginner, intermediate, and advanced level of the courses."

**The three-tier structure is the evidence, not the course count.** "Countless" is unusable as a
figure. But *every subject, at three levels, every time* is a design decision, repeated, and a
repeated design decision is a method. It says the same thing the 108 says — that Brad's answer to
adoption is to meet the user where they are rather than to run one session and call it delivered.

**Note what these courses were built in.** They were built *on the platform he was selling*, teaching
people to use that platform. The product demonstrates itself. That is worth stating in Phase 3 because
it is the kind of detail that reads as someone who actually did the work.

**This is also sales enablement evidence, and it strengthens correction 3.**
`design/01-positioning-brief.md` §11.1 correction 3 restored sales enablement as a claim on the basis
of four named instances, one of which is "trained on the vendor's enablement programme as the local
partner, then rebuilt it for New Zealand and Australia". **This is what that rebuild consisted of** —
until now that instance was an assertion with no contents.

### The Schoology New Zealand and Schoology Australasia group — the peer layer

A teacher network Brad created, spanning both territories. **Around 600 teachers at its peak.**

**This is the strongest single artefact in the answer, and it is the one most likely to be
undervalued.** Reasons:

1. **It is a number attached to something Brad built rather than something he did.** 108 interviews
   measures his labour. 600 teachers measures a thing that existed and had members.
2. **It is the customer base talking to each other.** A vendor's user community that reaches 600
   members is an adoption asset that keeps working when the account manager is not in the room, and
   it is a switching cost — a school leaving the platform leaves the network too.
3. **It spans both territories**, which quietly evidences the NZ→AU expansion working at the user
   level, not just the contract level.
4. **It is checkable.** Unlike almost everything else in this file, a group with 600 members leaves
   traces. See open questions.

**Two territories, two groups or one?** Brad said "a network, Schoology New Zealand and Schoology
Australasia group" — ambiguous as to whether that is one group covering both or two separate ones,
and whether the ~600 is combined or one of them. Confirm before publishing the figure.

## The three-layer system — the argument Phase 3 should consider leading with

Stated once, because it is the shape of the answer rather than a claim inside it.

| Layer | Mechanism | Who it reaches | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One-to-one | 108 interviews through lockdown | The resistant, the negative | 108, and Bergh's letter |
| Self-serve | Course library, three levels per subject, built on the platform itself | The willing, at their own pace | The tiering; named subject areas |
| Peer-to-peer | Schoology NZ / Australasia network | The converted, teaching each other | ~600 teachers at peak, across two territories |

**Why this is worth more than the sum of the three.** `design/01-positioning-brief.md` §11.2 committed
the site to leading with *what Brad knows*, each thing carrying its evidence in the same viewport, and
§11.6 left open whether the ramp method leads the homepage. This is a fully-formed method with three
mechanisms, a number under each, and a letter behind the first — and unlike the ramp, it is entirely
inside one role and needs no explaining across four employers.

**It also answers the hunter-or-farmer objection at §3.1 from a new direction.** The objection is that
customers may have stayed because switching a student management system is painful. The answer here is
that Brad built the thing that made staying attractive rather than merely difficult.

**Not a copy proposal.** Recorded as a structure Phase 3 may use, per the standing instruction that
this session does not draft copy.

## Open questions

1. **How many of the 108 were the naysayers?** Asked, not yet answered. "Numerous" is doing
   load-bearing work in a sentence that otherwise has a precise number in it. Even a rough
   proportion turns this from a warm claim into a measured one. **Still the highest-value follow-up.**
2. **Does the Schoology NZ / Australasia group still exist, and is there a trace of it?** A member
   count in a screenshot, an export, a mention in a vendor report. Of everything in this file this is
   the most likely to be independently checkable, which would make it the only adoption claim on the
   site with a source a reader could reach.
3. **One group or two?** And is ~600 the combined figure or one territory's?
4. **Where is the 108 recorded?** A schedule, a calendar, a report to the vendor, an invoice.
5. **Would any of the converted teachers vouch for it in writing?** One named teacher saying "I was
   against this and Brad changed my mind" would be the strongest adoption document in the archive,
   and the only one written by an end user rather than a manager.
6. **Did the schools whose teachers were converted renew?** The commercial close on this story is
   currently missing. If the conversion mapped to retained accounts, this stops being a training
   story and becomes a retention one.
7. **Was the course library Brad's design or the vendor's?** §11.1 correction 3 records that he
   rebuilt PowerSchool's enablement programme for NZ/AU. If the library *is* that rebuild, correction
   3 gains its missing contents and the enablement claim gets much stronger.
8. **Do the courses still exist anywhere?** They were built on a platform owned by a vendor, for an
   employer he has left. Likely inaccessible, but worth one question — a course library is a portfolio
   artefact if any of it can be shown.
