# The authority question — answered, and the revenue model as a designed system

**In master resume:** no. **On the site:** no. **In the brief:** no.
**Source:** Brad, 13 August 2026, in answer to the question held open across five separate claims.

**Status before this session:** the single most important unanswered question in the project, per
`NEXT-SESSION-PROMPT.md`. **Now answered, and it resolves seven blocked items at once.**

---

## The question, and why it was held

The authority question had arisen against five claims individually — the price increases, the managed
services vertical, the SIS integration, the course materials, and the implementation rate card — and had
been deferred each time rather than asked five ways. Two earlier claims in this archive had already had
to be corrected downward for exactly this reason: the Australian territory award and the price rises
both came through in the first person and turned out, on inspection, to be company achievements
narrated personally.

**It was asked once, plainly, in two parts.**

---

## The answer

**Part one — was the revenue model designed, or did it accumulate?**

> "yes I designed and delivered the revenue models."

**Part two — whose idea, who approved, and on what authority?** Pressed once, per the standing rule.

> "It was all my own work/ideas and I approved them, I was the senior leader charged with set up and
> driving the education sector of the business."

---

## Key points

**The second sentence is worth more than the first, and it is the half Brad did not think he was
giving.**

"I designed the revenue models" is an assertion of credit. **"I was the senior leader charged with set
up and driving the education sector of the business" is a description of a position** — and a position
either existed or it did not. It is checkable, it is the kind of thing a former colleague confirms in
one sentence, and it explains the first-person pronouns across the entire decade rather than merely
defending them.

| What was ambiguous | What the answer resolves it to |
| --- | --- |
| "I lifted their price each year" vs "we were sitting at about twenty-three" | The pronouns move because he **led the sector and worked in a team**. Both are true |
| "a vertical that I created" | Created by the person whose job was to create it |
| "we built an integration in my business" | **"My business"** is now explained — see the ruling below |
| Six revenue lines described separately | **Designed as a model by one person with the mandate to design it** |

**He approved his own commercial decisions.** That is the load-bearing clause. It means the
implementation rate card, the managed services pricing, the unbundling of support and the integration
charge were not proposals someone else signed off — they were decisions inside his remit. **This is
the difference the project has been trying to establish since the commercial half opened**, and it
lands on the strategic side of the line.

### C15 — "my business" still may not be published as ownership

**The answer strengthens the reasoning rather than overturning the rule.** Senior leader charged with
setting up and driving a sector is precisely the role in which a person says "my business" colloquially
and means *the business I ran the education side of*. It is not a claim of equity. **Publishing it as
ownership remains a factual error**, and now demonstrably a foreseeable one.

### C13 — closed, with the wording that survives

Copy may now say Brad **set the pricing for the education sector**. It may **not** say he set the
vendor's list price or controlled pricing across the whole business — his mandate was the education
sector, and the answer says so precisely. **The scope of the authority is part of the claim.**

## The finding this produces

**Six revenue lines, one designer, one mandate.** The monetisation model recorded across six separate
answers in the previous session — implementation, licence, integration, managed services, course
materials, government-funded training — may now be described as **a model that was designed**, not one
that accumulated.

| Stage of a school's life | The line | Who designed it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | Implementation, $3,750 flat | **Brad** |
| Running | Licence, per student, rising annually | **Brad** (education sector) |
| Data | SIS integration, ~$30k p.a. | **Brad** — originator, not engineer |
| Support | Managed services, $3,240–$6,480, billed a year upfront | **Brad** |
| Training | Localised course materials + on-site training | **Brad** — localisation, not authorship |
| Training | Government-funded bulk hours | **Brad**, via the Digital Circus partnership |

**Every stage of the customer lifecycle has a price attached, two lines scale automatically as the
account grows, two are funded by someone other than the school — and one person designed all six with
the mandate to do it.**

**This is the strongest single claim in the archive**, and it is stronger than any of the six lines
individually. It is also the claim most exposed if the authority is ever questioned, which is why the
position — senior leader for the sector — travels with it in any published form. **Never publish the
model without the mandate.**

## What may and may not be published

- **May:** that Brad designed the commercial model for the education sector, and that he held the
  mandate to do so as its senior leader.
- **May:** that he set pricing for the sector.
- **May not:** that he set pricing for the business as a whole. The sector is the boundary.
- **May not:** "my business" as ownership. C15 stands.
- **May not:** authorship of the US course materials. C16 stands, untouched by this answer.
- **May not:** any combined revenue total. **C14 stands and is now more tempting, not less** — a
  designed six-line model invites a sum. The lines may still have overlapped, no period is established,
  and Brad has still never made the combined claim.
- **Sole source is Brad.** The position itself is **Retrievable** — an org chart, a contract, a job
  title, a former colleague. **Worth retrieving, because it underwrites seven other claims.**

## Open questions

1. **The exact title of the position.** "Senior leader charged with set up and driving the education
   sector" is a description, not a title. **A government-status rule applies by analogy (D9): publish
   the real title or describe the remit, never an approximation dressed as a title.** Also bears on the
   promotion sequence, which still has no home on the site (§4.8).
2. **Was the sector set up from nothing, or inherited and grown?** "Set up" suggests the former. If the
   education sector did not exist before Brad, that is a materially larger claim than designing its
   pricing — and it is not yet made. **Ask; do not assume "set up" means founded.**
3. **Did anyone push back on unbundling support?** Not answered. Support had been free to schools;
   pricing it was a decision someone could have argued. **A named person who objected and was convinced
   is corroboration in the same class as D7.** Still worth one question.
4. **Retrieve the position.** See evidence status above. Highest-value retrieval in the archive now,
   because it underwrites seven claims rather than one.
