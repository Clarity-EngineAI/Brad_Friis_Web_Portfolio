**In the brief:** listed as a capability area, and **corrected down at `design/01-positioning-brief.md`

## Why this answer outranks its own capability area

§11.1 corrected the reseller story from *three territories originated* to *one agreement, one product,
NZ and AU*, and recorded that what it evidenced was "contracts, pricing matrices and international
negotiation" — with **no named term Brad had argued for and won.** The claim had a category and no
contents.

**It now has contents, and they are better than the claim that was voided.** The corrected-down version
said Brad managed an agreement. **The evidenced version is that he lost a contract to a force outside
his control, replaced the product line inside a year, and then renegotiated the returning vendor's
agreement across four dimensions — buy price, terms, licensing model and billing relationship.**

**Nothing about this contradicts §11.1.** One agreement, one product, NZ and AU remains correct. What is
added is what was inside the agreement and how it got there.

## The sequence

**Recorded as sequence because the sequence is the argument.** Each event is unremarkable alone; in
order, they are a negotiating position built out of a disaster.

| # | Event | Commercial significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **COVID-19 hits. US servers fail under home-learning load** | Outside Brad's control. Establishes the cause was not performance |
| 2 | **The channel partner contract is temporarily cancelled** | The business loses its product line |
| 3 | **Brad forms a partnership with itslearning, a Norwegian LMS** | **Replaced the product line inside the gap.** Initiative under loss, not recovery after it |
| 4 | **A year later PowerSchool — having acquired Schoology — returns, apologises, asks to resume and to add products** | **The vendor came back.** Brad's negotiating position is now structurally different |
| 5 | **Brad negotiates the entire new reseller agreement** | Buy price, terms, licensing framework, and the billing relationship |

**Step 3 is what makes step 5 possible and Brad did not present it that way.** He described forming the
itslearning partnership as taking initiative, which it was. Its commercial consequence is larger: when
PowerSchool returned, **the business was not desperate.** It had a working LMS in market. That is why
the terms he then won were as good as they were, and **any published version that gives the terms
without the year in between is telling the smaller story.**

## The terms Brad negotiated

> "I negotiated the entire reseller agreement with them, the buy price, the terms, and also the
> licensing framework."

**Five dimensions, three of them described in detail.** Two — licensing and billing — came in the main
answer; the minimum licence count was volunteered separately minutes later, which is the same pattern
as the managed services size-scaling correction: **Brad gives the mechanism first and the term that
made it work afterwards, without connecting them.**

| # | Term | What it changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Buy-as-needed, no upfront cost, fixed markup price** | The reseller's working capital and write-off risk |
| 2 | **Direct local-currency invoicing** | Who owned the customer relationship |
| 3 | **No minimum licence count** | **Who could be sold to at all** |
| 4 | Buy price | Named, not described |
| 5 | "The terms" | Named, not described |

**Terms 1 and 2 improved the economics of the business. Term 3 expanded its addressable market.** They
are different kinds of win and should not be published as a single undifferentiated list of things Brad
negotiated.

### 1 — Licensing: from buy-upfront-or-lose-it, to buy-as-needed at a fixed price

> "Previously, we had been forced to buy licenses upfront. And if they weren't sold, they would expire.
> In the new reseller agreement, I negotiated for us to buy as needed with no upfront cost at a fixed
> price that we could mark up."

**This is the most commercially significant term in the archive**, and it belongs to the same family as
the annual-prepayment decision at A22 — both are cash-cycle decisions rather than sales ones.

| | Old terms | New terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Payment** | Licences bought upfront | **No upfront cost** |
| **Risk of unsold stock** | **Expired — a total write-off** | **None. Buy as needed** |
| **Margin** | — | **Fixed buy price, marked up by the business** |

**Three separate risks removed in one term.** Working capital freed, inventory write-off eliminated, and
margin made predictable. **A reseller carrying expiring pre-bought inventory is exposed on all three.**
Brad removed the exposure and kept the markup.

### 2 — Billing and the customer relationship

> "the Americans were invoicing the customers on any sales made in American dollars, and I knew that was
> a pain for schools to have to pay in American dollars. And also, it meant that we didn't own the
> relationship. So I negotiated to change that"

**Two reasons given, and Brad ordered them customer-first.** The USD burden on schools came first; the
relationship ownership came second, introduced with "and also". **The order is worth preserving** — it
is the clearest instance in the archive of a commercial term argued from the customer's problem, which
is the whole thesis of the site.

**Owning the invoice is owning the account.** A reseller whose vendor bills the end customer directly
does not hold the commercial relationship, cannot control the renewal conversation, and is one vendor
decision away from disintermediation. **This term is what makes every retention and expansion claim in
this archive structurally possible** — the accounts were his to keep because he made them his to bill.

### 3 — No minimum licence count

**Volunteered separately, minutes after the rest.** Consistent with the session pattern: the mechanism
first, the term that made it work afterwards.

> "historically, [the vendor] would insist on a minimum of five hundred licenses, which did not align
> with the New Zealand education market in particular. Several of the schools in New Zealand don't even
> have five hundred students, so it made no sense. So I held firm and negotiated no minimum license
> count for a sale. As a result, we even had a school with only fifty licenses on the books."

**Transcription note.** The bracketed `[the vendor]` replaces **"Paris School"** as heard — almost
certainly voice transcription of **PowerSchool**. **The bracket is an edit, not Brad's words**, and is
marked so no future session mistakes it for verbatim. **Confirm before publishing**, and note it also
governs *which* vendor's minimum this was: PowerSchool post-acquisition, or Schoology before it. The
word "historically" suggests the term predates the renegotiation — see open question 9.

**This is the term that connects the contract to the market, and it is the argument the other four
terms do not make.** Buy-as-needed and local invoicing improved the reseller's economics. **Removing the
minimum changed who could be sold to at all.**

A 500-licence floor set by a US vendor is not a hard commercial position — it is a default carried over
from a market where schools are large. **Brad's argument was not that the minimum was unfair; it was
that it did not fit the market**, and he had the specific fact to prove it: New Zealand schools that do
not have 500 students. **The floor excluded customers who were not too small to be profitable, only too
small to meet a number written somewhere else.**

**The fifty-licence school is the proof and must always travel with the term.** Without it the claim is
a contract detail. With it, it is a term that opened a segment — one tenth of the old minimum, on the
books, because the floor was gone.

**"I held firm" is the archive's only instance of Brad describing himself holding a position against
the vendor**, and it survives §7 where C3–C7 did not. It reports conduct in a negotiation rather than
assessing his own quality. **Keep it.**

**Never render this as a volume win.** It is the exact opposite: he removed a volume requirement. The
claim is market access, not scale.

### 4 and 5 — buy price, and "the terms"

**Named, not detailed.** Brad listed both and described neither. **Do not infer.** See open questions.

## The uniqueness claim — corrected by Brad, 13 August 2026

**Brad's correction, given immediately after this file was first written:**

> "i did not say we are the only reseller in the world. I said we are the only channel partner that
> manages the billing directly with their clients."

**The correction is accepted and this section was rewritten to it.** The claim on record is now:

> **The only channel partner that manages the billing directly with their clients.**

**Recording what happened, because the pattern is instructive.** The spoken sentence transcribed as
*"we are the and we are the only reseller in the world that works under that arrangement where we
invoice the client and manage the relationship directly"* — a false start, then the phrase "in the
world". **This file's first draft treated that as the claim and barred it.** Brad's correction tightens
it to a bounded population, which is **the fourth time in two sessions he has narrowed his own claim
unprompted** — after the managed services size-scaling, the implementation fee split, and the price-rise
concession. **That pattern is now strong enough to be evidence in itself** about how his figures should
be read: his corrections consistently move toward accuracy, not toward flattery.

### Why the correction materially changes the ruling

| | As transcribed | As corrected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population | **Every reseller in the world**, all products, all vendors | **This vendor's channel partners** |
| Measurable by Brad? | No | **Plausibly yes** — a channel partner knows the other partners in the programme |
| Refutable by the vendor in one sentence? | **Yes** | Less easily; it is a claim about their own programme |
| Claim type | Global superlative | **A term he holds that his peers do not** |

**"The only channel partner" is a claim a channel partner is actually positioned to know.** Partner
programmes have finite, known membership; partners meet at vendor events; terms are compared. **This is
a different epistemic situation from asserting a global fact**, and it moves the claim from barred to
conditionally publishable.

### Source of the knowledge — answered, 13 August 2026

**Asked whether the vendor told him or he concluded it:**

> "I know it to be true from Powerschool. I developed a very close working relationship with the team at
> Powerschool over the years."

**The clause publishes.** The uniqueness is the vendor's own statement about its own partner programme,
which is the strongest available provenance for a fact of this kind — PowerSchool is the only party who
can definitively know it, and they are the party who said it.

**One constraint remains: tense.** "We **are** the only channel partner" asserts a present fact about a
business Brad left in September 2025. **Any published form is past tense**, scoped to his time there.

**The publishable form:**

> Negotiated an arrangement under which the business invoiced its own schools in New Zealand dollars and
> held the customer relationship directly — **the only channel partner in the vendor's programme
> operating that way.**

### The second claim inside the answer, which Brad did not present as one

> "I developed a very close working relationship with the team at Powerschool over the years."

**This is capability area 7 — the ten-year vendor channel relationship — arriving unannounced inside an
answer about a contract term**, and it is the mechanism the area has been missing. `_index.md` records
area 7 as *partial*, evidenced only by the Australian territory award and its four stated reasons.

**The relationship is what the whole of area 4 rests on**, and the sequence now reads as one argument:

| | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| The relationship existed | "a very close working relationship with the team at PowerSchool over the years" |
| It survived a contract cancellation | The vendor returned after a year and asked to resume |
| It produced commercial terms | Buy-as-needed, direct invoicing, no minimum licence count |
| It produced privileged information | **The vendor told him where he stood against their other partners** |

**The last row is the least obvious and the most telling.** A vendor does not tell an ordinary channel
partner how their terms compare to other partners' terms. **That Brad knew it, from them, is evidence
of the relationship's quality that is independent of his describing it as close** — which matters,
because "very close working relationship" is otherwise a self-assessment of the C3–C7 class.

**Publish the evidence, not the adjective.** *A vendor relationship close enough that they told him he
was the only partner billing directly* makes the point without Brad characterising it. **"Very close"
on its own is §7-banned; the fact underneath it is not.**

**Recorded to area 7 as well** — see open question 10.

## What may and may not be published

- **May:** the sequence — contract cancelled by a force outside his control, replacement partnership
  formed inside the gap, vendor returned, agreement renegotiated.
- **May:** all four negotiated dimensions, and the licensing and billing terms in detail.
- **May:** that the vendor approached the business to resume.
- **May not:** **"the only reseller in the world"** — **Brad corrected this on 13 August and never
  claimed it.** The claim is *the only channel partner managing billing directly with clients*,
  publishable subject to open question 3 and always in the past tense.
- **May not:** present tense on any of it. He left in September 2025.
- **May not:** **"begging"**. Brad's word for the vendor's approach. It is characterisation of a third
  party, it reads as gloating, and *approached us to resume, with an apology* carries the same fact.
- **May not:** any buy price, margin percentage or markup figure. **None was given.** Do not derive one.
- **May not:** the no-minimum term rendered as a volume or scale achievement. It removed a volume
  requirement. **The claim is market access.**
- **May not:** the fifty-licence school as a record, a smallest-ever, or a minimum. Brad said "we even
  had a school with only fifty" — **an instance, offered as proof the floor was gone.** Publish it as
  that and nothing more.
- **May:** **"I held firm."** It reports conduct in a negotiation, not a self-assessment, and so sits
  outside the §7 class that closed C3–C7. **The only instance in the archive of Brad describing himself
  holding a position against the vendor.**
- **Care:** **itslearning is a named third-party company**, as is PowerSchool. The naming rule (D4)
  governs schools; **vendors and partners are a separate question Brad has not been asked.** The site
  already names PowerSchool and Schoology by §11.1's own replacement framing, so itslearning is likely
  consistent — **but it is an inference and is flagged, not assumed.**
- **Spelling:** **itslearning** is the company's own styling — one word, lowercase. Brad said "It's
  Learning". **Confirm before publishing.**

## Open questions

1. **The buy price and "the terms" — named but not described.** What did the buy price move from and
   to, and what were the other terms? **Ask once; do not press if no figure exists**, and note that a
   vendor's buy price may be confidential in the same way C10's per-student pricing was.
2. **Was the new agreement exclusive?** The master resume says "exclusive reseller agreements", plural.
   §11.1 corrected the plural. **Exclusivity is a separate term from everything described here and has
   never been confirmed.** Do not publish "exclusive" until it is.
3. **"The only channel partner that manages the billing directly" — was Brad told this by the vendor,
   or did he conclude it?** **The only remaining gate on the uniqueness clause**, now that the
   population is bounded to the vendor's partner programme. Told → publishable as the vendor's own
   distinction. Concluded → *the only channel partner we knew of*, or drop the clause. **Either way the
   three negotiated terms stand without it.**
4. **Which additional products were picked up**, and is this the same event as §11.1 correction 5's two
   further distribution agreements and two overseas trips? **Ask; do not merge.** The pattern of
   assuming two descriptions are the same thing has already been wrong once this project.
5. **What happened to itslearning when PowerSchool returned?** Run both, or drop one? A business that
   carried two LMS products is a different commercial picture from one that switched back. **Neutral
   question, no implied answer.**
6. **Confirm the spelling "itslearning"** and confirm PowerSchool's acquisition of Schoology is being
   described in the right order and year. **Publicly checkable — the acquisition is a matter of record.**
7. **May third-party vendors and partners be named on the site?** D4 covers schools. PowerSchool and
   Schoology are already named by §11.1. **itslearning and Digital Circus need the same ruling.**
8. **How many schools came onto the book below the old 500 minimum?** The fifty-licence school proves
   the floor was gone. **A count would establish the segment it opened**, which is a materially larger
   claim than one anecdote — and it is the same attach-rate question outstanding against three other
   revenue lines. **Ask once; if no figure exists it is Retrievable, not dead.**
9. **Was the 500 minimum the old contract's term, or the vendor's opening position in the new
   negotiation?** Brad said "historically… would insist", which reads as the former. **The distinction
   matters:** removing a term that had constrained the business for years is a larger claim than
   declining one that was proposed. **Ask; the answer is probably the stronger one, which is why it
   should not be assumed.**
