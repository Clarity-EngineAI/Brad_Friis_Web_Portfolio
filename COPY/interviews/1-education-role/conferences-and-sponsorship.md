# Conferences, workshops and the association sponsorship

**In master resume:** partially. Line 25 ends "…and conference sponsorship and presentation" — six
words at the tail of a bullet about the full sales cycle. **That line is carrying one of the better
commercial stories on the CV and it is carrying it as an afterthought.**
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026.

---

## Key points

**Three separate things arrived in one answer and they are not the same capability.**

1. **Speaking** at numerous conferences — personal profile and category authority.
2. **Presenting workshops** to teachers on personalised learning and student engagement — the
   adoption method, delivered at scale to non-customers as well as customers.
3. **Securing major sponsorship of a national association's conference**, where the product underpinned
   the conference's own communication and materials — **this is the commercially significant one, and
   it is a different discipline from the other two.**

**Item 3 is not marketing spend. It is a distribution channel bought once and used on a whole
profession at a time.** The sponsorship put the product in the hands of every teacher at a national
conference *as the thing they used to attend the conference*, rather than as a stand at the back of
the room. That is a materially cleverer move than a logo on a lanyard, and nothing on the site
currently says he can do it.

---

## The detail

### The association

**NASDAP** — National Association of Secondary Assistant Principals, for New Zealand high schools.

*Brad self-corrected mid-sentence from "Nasdaq" to "NASDAP". The acronym as spoken expands to National
Association of Secondary Deputy/Assistant Principals. **Exact name and acronym need confirming before
publication** — a wrong association name in front of an education-sector reader is worse than no name
at all.*

**Why the association matters more than the conference.** The buyers of a student management platform
are not teachers; they are senior leadership. Assistant and deputy principals are exactly that tier —
the people who sit on the purchasing decision without being the signature on it. **Sponsoring their
national conference is buying access to the decision-influencer layer of the entire country's
secondary sector in one transaction.**

### What the sponsorship actually bought

> "Our product was used to underpin all of the communication and all of the workshops and materials
> shared in the workshops at the conference."

**This is the detail that makes the story.** The platform was not exhibited at the conference. It was
the infrastructure *of* the conference — the channel through which delegates received communications
and through which workshop materials were distributed.

**Three things that does, and only the first is obvious:**

1. **Exposure.** Every delegate saw the product. Brad's own words: "brilliant branding and exposed all
   of the teachers there to using the product."
2. **Forced hands-on use.** A delegate collecting their workshop materials *used the platform to do
   it*. That is a product trial disguised as conference logistics, and it reaches the sceptics who
   would never visit a vendor stand.
3. **Implied endorsement by the association.** A national body running its own conference on a product
   is a reference of a kind no letter can match, and it costs nothing extra once the sponsorship is
   signed.

**Brad did not make any of these three arguments.** He described what happened and called the branding
brilliant. The commercial reading above is this session's, and Phase 3 should treat the reasoning as
inference from his facts rather than as his claim.

### Speaking and workshops

Spoke at "numerous conferences" and presented workshops to teachers on:

- how to deliver personalised learning
- how to drive student engagement

**Note what these workshops are not about.** Neither topic is the product. They are pedagogy topics
that the product happens to serve — which is the same move as the course library being built *on* the
platform, and the same move as `design/01-positioning-brief.md` §11.3 question 9, *won't pitch before
understanding the operation*. **Three independent instances of the same instinct: lead with the
customer's problem, let the product follow.** That consistency is worth more than any one of them.

"Numerous" is not a figure and must not become one.

## What this changes about the site

**The master resume buries this.** Line 25 reads: "Ran the full cycle from prospecting to close: demos,
written proposals, pipeline management in Pipedrive, and conference sponsorship and presentation." The
sponsorship is the last four words of a six-item list about *sales process*. It is not a sales-process
item. It is a channel-marketing decision with a national footprint, and it belongs somewhere it can be
seen. Flagged for Phase 3.

**It also adds a fourth adoption layer.** `area-2-onboarding-adoption.md` records three mechanisms —
one-to-one, self-serve, peer-to-peer. This is a fourth and it sits *above* all of them: **sector-wide**,
reaching people who were not yet customers. The system is wider than that file currently states, and
this one should be read alongside it.

## Open questions

1. **The association's exact name and acronym.** NASDAP as spoken; needs confirming. Also which years
   the sponsorship ran, and whether it was one conference or several.
2. **Is the sponsorship traceable?** A conference programme, a sponsor page, an archived website, a
   photograph. This is the most likely retrieval route on the page and it would move the claim from
   Vouched to Measured.
3. **What did the sponsorship cost, and what did it produce?** Brad has not said whether any account
   traced back to it. If even one school can be attributed, this becomes a demand-generation story
   with an outcome rather than a branding story.
4. **Who signed off the spend?** Sponsorship of a national conference is a budget decision. If it was
   Brad's call, that is commercial ownership evidence — one of the four deal-breakers at §11.3
   question 4 is "no ownership of the relationship", so ownership evidence is load-bearing for the
   positioning.
5. **Named conferences for the speaking.** One named event with a topic beats "numerous conferences"
   entirely.
