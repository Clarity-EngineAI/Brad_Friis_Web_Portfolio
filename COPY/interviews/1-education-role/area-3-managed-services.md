# Capability area 3 — managed services and staggered billing

**In master resume:** no. **On the site:** no. **In the brief:** listed as a capability area carrying an
unattached "30 per cent uplift" figure — see the ruling below, which **detaches that figure from this
area entirely.**
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026.

**Status before this session:** *not started*. **This area is now the second-strongest commercial
material in the archive**, behind Sancta Maria.

---

## Key points

**Brad did not sell a managed services agreement. He created the product.** When he began in the role
there was no charge for support — it was rolled into the licence cost. He identified it as a separable
line, built a monthly managed services agreement, and **billed it annually in advance**.

**Three distinct commercial moves in one decision, and Brad named all three without noticing:**

| # | The move | Why it counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Unbundled support from the licence** | Found revenue the business was already giving away |
| 2 | **Priced and packaged it as a new vertical** | Product creation, not selling an existing product |
| 3 | **Structured it monthly, billed it annually upfront** | **A working-capital decision.** Cash in the door a year ahead of delivery |

**Worth around $40,000–$50,000 a year to the business, at near-zero marginal cost**, on work that was
already being performed.

**The third move is the rarest and Brad passed over it fastest.** Unbundling and pricing are standard
commercial thinking. Deliberately structuring an agreement monthly and then collecting the year upfront
is a cash-cycle decision most salespeople never make and most cannot explain. **It is the most
CFO-legible fact in the whole archive.**

## The detail

> "The managed services agreement was a vertical that I created when I first began in the role. There
> was no charge for support. It was all rolled into the license cost, whereas I could see that this was
> a vertical where we could earn some money. So I created a monthly managed services agreement. I had
> the schools pay annually, so we got the money upfront, and worked out to be around forty to fifty
> thousand dollars per year extra income for the business for doing very little, to be honest."

### Correction, 13 August 2026 — the agreements were size-scaled

**Volunteered by Brad later the same session**, prompted by describing the implementation fee:

> "You've just reminded me that the managed services agreements were scaled to the size of the school.
> So the larger the school, the more they paid."

**This file was written treating the vertical as a flat annual line. It was not.** The distinction is
material: a flat price is a rate; a size-scaled price is **a pricing model that expands on its own as
the customer grows.** Given how many accounts in this archive grew substantially — Rototuna 600 →
~1,200, Elim's second and third campuses, the five-campus account's sixth — **the managed services line
grew with them without a new sale each time.**

**Recorded in full at `implementation-fee-and-scaled-pricing.md`.** The $40–50k figure below is
unaffected; what changes is what the vertical demonstrates.

### "For doing very little" is the claim, not a caveat

Brad said it apologetically and it is the strongest half of the sentence. **The work was already being
delivered.** Support was being given to schools at no charge because it sat inside the licence. Brad
did not add cost, headcount or delivery obligation — he attached a price to an existing activity.

**That is margin, not revenue**, and the distinction is the point. $40–50k of new sales carries a cost
of sale. $40–50k against work already being done is close to all of it falling through. **Any published
version must keep the near-zero marginal cost, because without it the figure is merely a number; with
it, the figure is a demonstration of commercial thinking.**

**Never publish "for doing very little" in Brad's words.** It undersells the identification, which was
the hard part, and it reads as coasting. The publishable form is that the service was already being
delivered unpriced.

### Timing — "when I first began in the role"

**This was done at the start, not accumulated over the decade.** That matters for two reasons and
neither is obvious:

1. **It is evidence of what Brad does on arriving somewhere new** — which is the single most useful
   thing a hiring manager can know, because it is a prediction rather than a history. A candidate who
   found an unpriced revenue line in their first months at their last job is making an implicit offer.
2. **It compounds.** A vertical created at the start of a ten-year role and worth $40–50k a year is a
   materially larger cumulative number, and **that number must not be computed or published** — see the
   ruling below.

the class already disclaimed three times. The two facts sit side by side; they never multiply.

**"90 per cent" is Brad's number and is not to be rounded to "almost all" or hardened to "nine in
ten of every client".** Publish it as given.

## What may and may not be published

**The $40–50k is a different confidentiality problem from the per-student pricing, and it resolves the
other way.**

| Figure | Whose information | Ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $10 / $23 per student | **The customer's.** What a named school pays | **Barred** — C10 |
| $40–50k per year | **The former employer's** aggregate services revenue | **Publishable in principle.** No school's spend is reconstructable from it, and the employer is never named on the site. **Brad's call, flagged not assumed** |

**Never compute a cumulative or lifetime figure.** $40–50k a year across a ten-year role invites a
"half a million dollars" sentence. Brad did not say it, the vertical's revenue across the decade has
not been established, and it would be an invented figure of exactly the class the project has already
disclaimed twice.

**Never imply the vertical still exists or that the figure is current.** It is a decade-old annual
revenue line at a business Brad has left.

**Never publish this area and area 6 as two achievements** (C19, added 13 August 2026). **They are one
product.** The 2–6 hours a month at `area-6-support-framework.md` is what the $3,240–$6,480 band bought.
This file is the commercial face, area 6 the delivery face. **Counting them as two revenue lines is
C14's error in a new costume**, and the seam is the first place an interviewer would push.

**Never divide the price band by the hours band** (C20). $3,240 ÷ 24 and $6,480 ÷ 72 give two different
hourly rates. Brad stated no rate, the two bands came from separate sessions, and it is not established
that the bottom of one maps to the bottom of the other.

**The hedge is Brad's and survives:** "around forty to fifty thousand". Not "$50,000". Not "over
$40,000".

## Open questions

1. **"A vertical that I created" — was it Brad's idea, and did he build it alone?** The claim is
   product creation inside someone else's business, and it currently rests on one sentence with no
   corroboration. **The third instance of the same ambiguity this session** — the Australian territory
   award ("we"), the price increases ("I lifted" / "we were sitting at"), and now this. **Ask directly
   whether he proposed it, who approved it, and whether anyone else built it with him.** If it was his
   proposal, this is the best origination evidence in the archive now that the reseller claim has been
   corrected down.
2. **Is Brad content to publish a former employer's revenue figure?** See the table above. His call.
3. **What was actually in the agreement** — scope, term, what the schools got, who delivered it?
   **ANSWERED 13 August 2026, except the term — see `area-6-support-framework.md`.** The schools got
   **2–6 hours of support a month, banded by school size, with response times set by issue urgency and
   type.** It was delivered by **the business's own technicians under their manager**, to a framework
   **Brad designed**. Scope is support, unbundled from the licence. **The contract term is still
   unanswered.** Phase 3 can now describe the product, which it could not before.
4. **How many schools took it, out of how many in the book?** Attach rate. If most of the book bought
   it, that is a far stronger claim than the dollar figure. If few did, the figure needs care.
   **Still open, and now the strongest unclaimed fact about this product.**
5. **Staggered billing — what problem was it solving?** ~~Asked twice, not yet answered.~~ **Resolved as
   a reading, 13 August 2026.** The brief's "staggered" almost certainly refers to **the banded
   pricing** — different schools paying different amounts on the same product — **not to a payment
   schedule**, which would contradict the annual prepayment. **Recorded as a reading, not a
   confirmation, and it does not need asking: nothing publishes on the word "staggered" either way.**
6. **Where did the 30 per cent uplift figure come from?** See the ruling above.
7. **Did the vertical survive after Brad left?** A product that outlived its creator is a stronger
   claim. **Ask carefully** — this is adjacent to how the role ended, which is under a settlement gag.
   If the answer trends there, stop.
