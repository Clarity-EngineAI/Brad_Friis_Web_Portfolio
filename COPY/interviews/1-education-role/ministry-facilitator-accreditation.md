# Ministry of Education facilitator accreditation — a funded training channel

**In master resume:** no. **On the site:** no. **In the brief:** no. **In any document in the project:**
no.
**Source:** Brad, spoken, 13 August 2026, volunteered unprompted while describing revenue he generated.

**This is the third revenue line Brad created inside the education role**, after the managed services
vertical and the annual repricing of the licence book. **It is the most strategically significant of
the three**, and it is the only one that answers a structural problem rather than finding money on the
table.

---

## Key points

**Brad became a Ministry of Education approved facilitator**, which opened the business up to access
**government funds for training**. Schools he worked with could then **apply for bulk hours, paid for
by the government**, to deliver training that upskilled their teachers.

**He could not become a facilitator alone.** He partnered with **Digital Circus**, another company, who
"took me under their wing" and helped him through the accreditation.

### Why this is the strongest strategic move in the archive

**It solves the exact problem that was costing him accounts.** In the immediately preceding answer Brad
explained that the occasional school was lost on overall cost, because Google Classroom and Microsoft
365 are subsidised by the New Zealand government and Schoology is expensive by comparison.

**The subsidy was the structural disadvantage. Brad went and got accredited into the funding system.**

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitor | Government-subsidised, free at point of use | Unchanged |
| Brad's product | Paid, unsubsidised | Unchanged |
| **Brad's training** | **A cost the school had to fund** | **Fundable by government bulk hours** |

He did not lower the price or argue with the subsidy. **He made a part of his own offer government-
funded**, which narrows the cost gap that was losing him schools and does it without discounting.

**This is a different class of commercial thinking from everything else recorded this session.**
Unbundling support (area 3) is finding revenue already being given away. Annual repricing (area 5) is
holding value against pressure. **Accreditation is changing the terms of the market you sell in.** It
required an external partnership, a qualification process, and a bet that the funded hours would
follow.

### Two things it evidences that nothing else in the archive does

1. **Partnership to acquire a capability the business did not have.** Brad did not have standing to
   become a facilitator, so he found a company that did and worked through them. **The archive's only
   other partnership evidence is the reseller agreement**, which §11.1 corrected down to one agreement,
   one product, two territories. This is a second, different, and unpolluted instance.
2. **Selling into a funding system rather than a budget.** Every other retention claim on the site is
   about persuading a school to spend its own money. This is about routing a third party's money to the
   school so it could buy training. **A hiring manager in any enterprise or public-sector-adjacent
   market reads that immediately.**

## The detail

> "Another area where I generated revenue was becoming a Ministry of Education approved facilitator,
> which then opened our business up to access government funds for training. In order to become a
> facilitator, I partnered with another company called Digital Circus, who took me under their wing so
> that I could become a facilitator and helped me. Then each school that we worked with were able to
> apply for bulk hours paid for by the government to then deliver training to upskill their teachers."

### The pronoun, for the third time this session — and this one reads as his

The pattern flagged at the Australian territory award and again at the price increases recurs, but
**resolves differently here.** Brad says **"I partnered"**, **"I could become a facilitator"**, **"me"**
— first person singular, three times, for the acquisition of the accreditation. The "our" and "we"
appear only for **who benefited**: "opened *our business* up", "each school that *we* worked with".

**The division is clean and it is the honest reading: the accreditation was Brad's, the benefit was the
company's.** This is the opposite structure to the territory award, where the achievement was the
company's and only the narration was personal.

**Still to confirm** — whether the accreditation attached to Brad personally or to the business through
him. See open question 1. **It materially changes the claim**, because a personal professional
accreditation is portable and is the kind of thing that belongs on a CV in its own right.

## What is missing, and it is the same gap as everywhere else

**No magnitude.** Brad introduced this as "another area where I generated revenue" and then described
the mechanism without saying what it earned. **Three revenue lines have now been described this session
and only one carries a figure** (managed services, $40–50k a year).

**How many schools took up the funded hours, and what the channel was worth, are both unknown.** This
is the fourth time in one session that Brad has answered a commercial question with a mechanism. The
pattern is now consistent enough to be worth naming in the handoff: **he explains how something worked
rather than what it produced**, and the figure has to be asked for separately every time.

## Open questions

1. **Was the accreditation Brad's personally, or the business's through him?** **Highest-value question
   here.** A personal accreditation is portable, belongs on the CV, and survives the employer never
   being named. An organisational one does not travel with him.
2. **What was the funded training channel worth?** Per school, per year, or in total. Any magnitude.
3. **How many schools took up the bulk hours**, out of how many in the book? Attach rate again.
4. **The exact name of the status.** "Ministry of Education approved facilitator" as spoken — the
   official term may differ, and the official term is what makes it checkable. **Do not publish an
   approximation of a government accreditation's name.**
5. **Confirm "Digital Circus"** — spelling, and whether the company still exists.
6. **When did this happen** in the decade? Before or after the schools started being lost on overall
   cost? **If it was a response to that pressure, the two answers become one argument** and it is a much
   stronger story than either alone. **Do not assume the sequence.**
7. **Did Brad deliver the funded training himself, or did the schools buy it from the business?** Who
   was paid, and for what.
8. **Is the accreditation still current?** Relevant to whether it is a live asset or a historical one.
