## The interview

### The can

> "sliced beetroot can, slightly stained with the purple juice of it's past contents... no sharp
> edges to cut generous fingers as they place their support inside of it"

**A sliced beetroot tin.** Purple staining from the contents. No sharp edges — Brad's own framing is
that the safety of the rim mattered *to the donor*, not to him.

### The location and the route

> "We were living at 2 Arthur Avenue, Manningham in Adelaide, South Australia. I worked my way down
> one side of the street and back up the other."

**2 Arthur Avenue, Manningham, Adelaide, South Australia.** One street. Down one side, back up the
other. No roads crossed.

**Note for publication:** this is a childhood address and it is Brad's own. Naming the street is his
call — see open questions below.

### The pitch — Brad's reconstruction, not verbatim from memory

> "I would like to ask you to please help me get a cubs uniform because I want to go to cubs and Mum
> said we have no money for the uniform because my Dad's business got stolen by his business
> partner."

**Flagged: this is a reconstruction**, offered in answer to a request for the actual words. It is
almost certainly the *substance* of what a seven-year-old said rather than the exact sentence.
**Usable in a row-four post as reconstructed dialogue; not usable as a quotation of record.**

**SUPERSEDED IN PART — 15 August 2026.** The original reconstruction contained a third-party
allegation (that the father's business partner stole the business). **Brad has replaced it**, on
being asked how much could be said and in whose voice:

> "The fathers business can simply be 'Mum said we couldnt afford it - so I thought I was genuinely
> helping!"

**This is the approved version of the pitch and the allegation does not publish in any form.**

**Assessment: the replacement is stronger than what it replaces**, not merely safer. It relocates
the story from an adult grievance to a child's misunderstanding — *I thought I was genuinely
helping* — which is where the story's engine already was. The father's business is now background
the reader never needs. **Constraint 1 below is closed by this.**

### Who answered

> "There were multiple middle class Australian house wives who answered. I collected money from
> multiple houses on the street."

No individual door recalled. **No named person, no reported speech, and no recollection of a
refusal** — the "who said no" question returned nothing for this story.

### The money and the duration

> "It was one afternoon after school, and I raised enough for the uniform."

**One afternoon.** Amount raised: not recalled as a figure, only as sufficiency — enough for the
uniform. **No dollar amount exists for this story and none should be introduced.**

### The mother — the strongest material in the file

> "What the bloody hell have you been doing! You little bugger, you'll return this money to every
> house!"

**Flagged as Brad's recall of the actual words**, though at nearly fifty years' distance treat as
reconstruction for accuracy purposes while using as dialogue in the post.

> "Then she kicked me up the bum and walked with me to every house I went to and made me say sorry
> and give them their money back."

**She walked the return with him.** Door by door, an apology at each, money back at each. This is
new — the existing précis says only that she made him return it.

How she found out:

> "She found out because I proudly told her and Dad I was able to go to cubs now becaudse we have
> the money, I earned it."

**He told on himself, proudly.** This detail is the engine of the piece and is absent from every
prior version of the story.

### The uniform

> "I don't recall the cost of the uniform"

**No cost figure. Do not introduce one.**

> "yes, I did get to go to cubs, the money was miraculously found for the uniform."

**"Miraculously found"** — Brad's own words, and his own implied reading of what his parents did.
The post should not explain this. It is the strongest unexplained fact in the story.

> "I recall putting th euniform on and pulling the leather bobble up my scarf round my neck and
> looking in the mirror and marvelling at the clean and importance of such an outfit. As each badge
> was won and sewn onto it by my dedicated and supportive mother, my sense of achievement grew."

**The leather bobble. The scarf. The mirror.** And **the mother who sewed on each badge** — the same
woman who marched him back down the street. That pairing is doing the emotional work of the whole
piece and neither half should be stated as a lesson.

### The reversal — new, and it changes what the story is about

> "Though it never grew so much as to influence me to want to wear a unifrom later in life. In fact
> I have a real aversion to wearing business branded clothign and have refused to this day to do
> so."

**A lifelong aversion to branded clothing, still held.** This did not exist anywhere in the project
record before 15 August 2026.

**Assessment:** this is the ending. It is a reversal — a boy who marvels at an earned uniform in a
mirror becomes a man who refuses to wear one — and it is structurally what the *Fort Commander*
badge ending does in Brad's own tabled style example (`COPY/blog/Nobunga writing example 2`:
"I did not ask for this rank. I will not give it back."). **It also makes the existing stated moral
redundant**, because the story is no longer about how to ask. See `design/09-blog-voice.md` §6.

### What he was wearing

> "I was wearign stubbies and a striped t-shirt and thongs (Australian for jandals)"

**Stubbies, a striped t-shirt, thongs.** Note for a New Zealand audience: *thongs* is the Australian
term and will misread badly on a NZ site without the gloss. Brad supplied the gloss himself.

## The ending — Brad's instruction, 15 August 2026

> "with the cubs story I also want to end it with a realisation in later life that it's the skills
> and results that matter - not the badge."

**The intent is settled and is better than the ending proposed to him** (the branded-clothing
aversion alone). It says something about how Brad works rather than only what he will not wear.

**The unresolved part is execution, not intent.** Stated in those words it is a stated moral, which
`design/09-blog-voice.md` §3.1 bans in row four — and it is structurally the same sentence as the
About Me paragraph's *"asking is not the embarrassing part. Asking badly is."* Both of Brad's own
tabled examples reach their point without declaring it.

**Three routes put to Brad, all landing on his stated point:**

1. **Badge and refusal, adjacent and unexplained.** The mother sews on each badge; the mirror; then,
   flat and much later, the lifelong refusal to wear branded clothing. The reader concludes *he
   stopped needing the badge* unaided.
2. **The badge outlives its meaning.** The uniform ends up in a drawer or given away. The results
   did not. Which mattered is never stated.
3. **The skill outlasts the uniform.** The uniform was outgrown within a year; what persisted was
   the thing done with the beetroot tin — walking up to a stranger's door and asking.
   **Recommended, combined with a touch of 1.** It names the skill by showing it persist rather than
   by declaring a lesson, which is closest to Brad's stated intent.

### RESOLVED — 15 August 2026

Brad, on both questions:

> "1 - lets try a combo of yours and mine.
> 2 - the branded clothing aversion arrived later in life."

**(a) The ending is a combination**: route 3 carries the realisation, Brad's own thought supplies its
substance. The uniform was outgrown; what persisted was the thing done with the beetroot tin.

**(b) The aversion is chronologically and causally separate, and this is a material constraint on
the draft.** It arrived later in life, independently. **It is therefore not evidence for the
realisation and must not be presented as its consequence.**

**Why this matters more than it looks.** The tidy version writes itself — *the badge taught me not
to need badges, which is why I have never worn a branded uniform since* — and it is false. Brad's
memory does not contain that causal line; it contains two facts that happen to share a uniform.
Asserting the causation would be **invented biography**, which is a worse failure than any style
breach in this document, because every other claim on this site is defensible and this one would not
be.

**Approved treatment: the two facts rhyme, they do not connect.** The aversion may sit at the end as
an unexplained coda — the register permits adjacency and trusts the reader to feel the echo — but
**no *therefore*, no *which is why*, and no causal verb between them.** If the coda cannot be made
to work without implying cause, cut it; the realisation stands without it.

### The direction correction — 15 August 2026, and it reframes the whole story

Three endings were put to Brad. He chose A, with a correction:

> "i think A - biut there needs to be a spin on it - because I've spent my career walking up to
> strangers askign if I can help them - and in exchange they give me soemthign in return"

**The draft line had the motion backwards.** "Walking up to strangers and asking them for something"
describes the tin can — take first. **Brad's actual career motion is the inverse: offer help, and the
exchange follows.**

**This is corroborated by the entire pre-education interview sweep** and is not merely Brad's
self-description. All four pre-education roles returned **retention, not acquisition**
(`COPY/interviews/_index.md`, "The governing question of the pre-education sweep"). Land-keep-grow is
help-then-exchange. The record already argues this; the ending simply has to match it.

**The consequence is structural, not cosmetic.** If the career motion is help-first, then **the
seven-year-old with the tin is not an early version of Brad — he is the inverse of one.** He took
money and gave nothing back. **The correction of that direction is the mother walking him to every
door to apologise and return every dollar.**

**Therefore: the tin can is not the origin of the sales instinct. The walk back is.**

This is consistent with Brad's own approved pitch line — *"I thought I was genuinely helping!"* The
child believed he was in the help-first business and was not yet. The gap between what he believed
he was doing and what he was actually doing is the story, and the mother closes it.

**Effect on the About Me paragraph (`design/09-blog-voice.md` §6):** this makes the existing moral —
*"asking is not the embarrassing part. Asking badly is."* — not just a stated moral but **arguably
the wrong reading of the event.** The paragraph frames the story as being about how to ask. On this
reading it is about which direction the asking runs. **Strengthens the case for retiring the
paragraph, but the decision remains Brad's and remains open.**

**Effect on the mother:** she moves from comic relief in the middle of the piece to **the figure who
sets the direction of the whole working life.** The draft must give her that weight without stating
it.

### Approved ending — as corrected

> The uniform lasted a year, maybe two, before I grew out of it.
>
> The walk back lasted longer. I have spent thirty years knocking on doors and asking people whether
> I can help them, and what comes back comes back after. That is the whole trade. It took a beetroot
> tin and one furious woman marching me up my own street to teach me which way round it goes.
>
> I still do not own a branded shirt.

**Open at draft time:** *"That is the whole trade"* is close to a stated moral and the piece is
stronger without it. Cut unless Brad wants the point unmissable.

**Constraint retained:** the branded-shirt line is a coda that rhymes. **No causal verb joining it to
anything above it.**

**Drafting is unblocked.**
