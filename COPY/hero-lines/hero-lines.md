## Round three — written 11 August 2026 after Brad's archive decisions

The brief for this round: keep the *structure* of option G — a third-party fact at the top of the
page, obeying the site's governing rule at the very first line — while making a claim about span
rather than solicitation. Round three concludes that the structure should be kept and **moved one
line down**, into the support paragraph. The reasoning is in J and K below.

### J — The proposition, with the evidence behind it · **Recommendation suspended, 12 August 2026**

> **Restatused 12 August 2026 after the strategy interview.** Recommended → **held, support
> paragraph pending a re-cut.** Two reasons, both from the interview record at
> `01-positioning-brief.md` §11:
>
> **1. The support paragraph is achievement-led and the site is not any more.** Brad: *"I want users
> to learn more about what I know than what I've done — the CV will show the roles I've had."* Every
> sentence below the display line is a thing Brad did. Under the knowledge-led architecture the
> support paragraph leads with what he knows and carries its evidence beside it.
>
> **2. It exceeds the new density ceiling.** The homepage budget is under 250 words and two of
> Brad's three repellent references failed on wordiness. Sixty-two words of support above the fold
> spends a quarter of the page before the reader has learned anything.
>
> The display line itself is not restatused. It is a knowledge claim already, and it survives the
> re-test or it does not on its own merits — that is next session's first job, along with whether
> *keeps* survives as the proposition's verb at all.

> ### I have kept the same customers for ten years at a time.
>
> Brad Friis — account management, customer success and channel partnerships, Auckland. Twenty-five
> years in software, media and agency work, ten of them running a United States vendor's New Zealand
> and Australian business as its only local partner. The licensed base went from 2,800 to 24,500,
> and an account that was actively evaluating a competitor stayed and expanded. Six people have put
> it in writing; the oldest is from February 2003.

**For.** It states the proposition the whole site exists to prove — `01-positioning-brief.md` §3 —
in the reader's first three seconds, and there is nothing in it to object to. Warm, unhurried,
senior, no wordplay, and impossible to write unless it is true. The hiring manager has the seat by
the end of the first sentence of support and the number by the end of the second, which is the
forty-second test passed in order of what they actually need. The letters arrive last, as
corroboration rather than as the pitch, which is where `01-positioning-brief.md` §4 ranks them —
and the closing date does the arithmetic instead of handing over a count that is about to move.

**Against.** Quiet. No turn, no tension, and a scanner may read *kept* as maintenance rather than
growth. Brad has now turned down two lines built on the same verb — A on exactly this ground, and C
for register — so recommending a third is a real risk and it is named here rather than buried. The
display line is also the longest in the file at nine words.

**Provenance.** The display line is round two's option I, untouched. It was already the strongest
surviving candidate in this file, because it makes no claim about solicitation and needs no rewriting
when the count changes. **The support paragraph is new.** Option I's version claimed *"the last
decade ran on renewals rather than new logos"*, which is not supported — the CV records full-cycle
prospecting to close throughout the channel-partner decade, and a licensed base going from 2,800 to 24,500 in a
small market almost certainly includes new schools. That clause is void; see the restatus under I.

**Design consequence.** The support paragraph names six documents, so the governing rule requires the
names in the same viewport. See the attribution line in
`COPY/section-headings/section-headings.md`, restatused this session from three names to six.

### K — Letters first, the option G shape made true · **Held, 12 August 2026. Support paragraph pending a re-cut**

> **Restatused 12 August 2026, same grounds as J.** Available → **held.** The support paragraph is
> the same achievement-led sentence set and the same sixty-word weight, and it fails the sub-250-word
> homepage budget for the same reason. The display line is untouched by the restatus.

> ### Six people put it in writing. The oldest is from February 2003.
>
> Brad Friis — account management, customer success and channel partnerships, Auckland. Twenty-five
> years in software, media and agency work, ten of them running a United States vendor's New Zealand
> and Australian business as its only local partner. The licensed base went from 2,800 to 24,500,
> and an account that was actively evaluating a competitor stayed and expanded.

**For.** It preserves what was good about option G exactly: the most unusual fact on the site, stated
third-party rather than self-claimed, obeying the evidence rule at the first line and setting the
terms for everything below. It makes `/letters` the obvious destination and *Read all six* the
obvious primary action, which is the only hero here that earns the nav's boldest label before the
reader reaches it. Two sentences, no industry count to pick at, and the date lets the reader do the
arithmetic.

**Against, and it is why J is recommended instead.** Three things.

**1. Volume of praise is not a commercial outcome.** This was G's original objection and Brad's
decisions did not touch it. The support paragraph still has to carry the seat, the scale and the save
on its own, and the reader learns what Brad does second rather than first.

**2. The count is in the display line and the count is moving.** Brad is requesting a replacement
reference from Jessep. The moment it arrives, the largest type on the site is wrong until it is
reset, and it will be typeset into three Stage 3 directions and a Stage 4 homepage by then.

**3. The site would say the same thing twice in its two loudest slots.** *Six people put it in
writing* is the recommended `/letters` page headline, and `Who says so` is the nav item pointing at
it. Spending the hero on the same sentence leaves the top of the homepage making one argument three
times.

**When to prefer it.** If Brad wants the site to lead with the thing no competitor has rather than
with the seat he is applying for. That is a legitimate choice and it is not the recommendation.

### L — Both sides of the desk · **Rejected 11 August 2026 — good fact, wrong slot**

> ### Two of my bosses and four of my customers wrote it down.

**For.** The most interesting structural fact in the archive, and it answers the recruiter's sharper
doubt — customers like a good salesman, so what did the people who managed him think? Both employers
volunteered in writing that the resignation was Brad's own.

**Against.** *Wrote it down* is vague at display size — wrote *what* down? The line needs a second
sentence to mean anything, which makes it a standfirst rather than a hero. And a hero built on an
employer-versus-customer split makes the site's opening argument a rebuttal to an objection the
reader has not made yet.

**Where it went.** The standfirst of the `/letters` page: *Two bosses and four customers, 2003 to
2025.* See `COPY/letters/letters-page.md` §1. Nothing is wasted.

## Round two — written 10 August 2026 after A, C and E were turned down

### G — Evidence first · **Rejected 11 August 2026 — factually untrue**

> ### Three customers wrote references I never asked for.
>
> Twenty-five years in software, media and agency work. Ten of them running a United States
> vendor's New Zealand and Australian business as its only local partner, where the licensed base
> went from 2,800 to 24,500 and an account that was actively evaluating a competitor stayed and
> expanded.

**For.** The most unusual fact on the site, and it is third-party rather than a claim Brad makes
about himself. It obeys the site's own governing rule — no claim without its evidence — at the
very top of the page, which sets the terms for everything below it. It makes `/proof` the
destination rather than a footnote, and `Read the references` becomes the obvious primary action.

**Against.** Volume of praise is not a commercial outcome. The support paragraph has to carry the
seat, the scale and the save on its own, which makes it the hardest-working sentence on the site.
The reader learns what Brad does second rather than first.

**Design consequence.** The claim names three documents, so three names have to be visible in the
same viewport or the hero breaks the rule it is demonstrating. Every Stage 3 direction carries an
attribution line directly under the support paragraph:

> Adrian Pilgrim, The Industry School · Dr Andrew Bergh, high school principal · Pranesh Lad,
> Rototuna High Schools

**Consent consequence.** Three real names now sit above the fold rather than three screens down.
Written consent moves from important to structural. *Consent was granted by all three on 11 August
2026 — that part held.*

**Why it died, 11 August 2026.** Not taste. Fact. Brad asked Bergh and Pilgrim for their references,
so the line is untrue of two of the three documents it names, and it would have appeared in public
above those two people's signatures. No variant of it is recoverable. What is worth keeping is the
*structure* of the option — a third-party fact at the top of the page, obeying the site's governing
rule at the very first line — and the replacement should keep that shape while making a claim about
span rather than solicitation.
