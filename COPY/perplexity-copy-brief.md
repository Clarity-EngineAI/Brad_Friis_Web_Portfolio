# Copy generation brief — for Perplexity

**Purpose:** paste this whole file into Perplexity at the start of a copy session, then add the
one section block you want written. It carries the character budgets from the live layout, the
voice rules, and the constraints that have already killed copy on this project.

**Updated:** Sunday 16 August 2026. Character budgets measured against the built CSS, not estimated.

---

## 1. The role you are writing for

Brad Friis. Auckland, New Zealand. Twenty-five years across software sales, account management,
media, agency and web strategy. He is looking for a commercial role — account management with
retention and expansion responsibility, not net-new hunting.

The site is a personal positioning site, not a company site. First person where a voice is needed,
never second person. The reader is never addressed as "you".

## 2. Who reads it

**Primary: a hiring manager or an internal recruiter.** Forty seconds on the page, one question in
their head: *is this person going to hit a retention and expansion number.*

This has consequences that have already been learned the hard way:

- **Do not write to a business buying Brad's services.** A whole round of copy was rejected for
  addressing a prospective client rather than a prospective employer.
- **No second person.** "I help you win better-fit customers" is wrong for this site. "I find out
  what the customer needs before I sell anything" is right.
- **Value proposition, not advertisement.** Brad's exact words: *"you are writing as if it is an
  advertisement instead of a value proposition."* When thirty options were put in front of him he
  named the plainest one as the best. Plain declarative sentences about the work beat slogans every
  time here.

## 3. Voice

- Plain declarative sentences, decodable in one beat.
- NZ English spelling: colour, organise, recognise, centre, behaviour, favourite.
- No emojis. No em-dash-heavy rhythm. No rhetorical questions.
- Present tense for a capability claim. Past tense when stating a record or a role held.
- Concrete nouns from the actual work — pipeline, proposals, renewals, onboarding, adoption,
  contract terms, billing — rather than abstractions like "synergy", "passion", "journey".
- No device. No two-part rhythm with a withheld second clause, no reversal, no aphorism, no
  colon-then-punchline. These read as advertising and get rejected.
- **Sentence case everywhere. Never Title Case.** Every heading, card title, career row and pill on
  this site is sentence case — "Kept the national account for three years", not "Kept the National
  Account for Three Years". The CSS applies no capitalisation transform, so whatever you write is
  what renders. (The eyebrow pill is the one exception: it is uppercased by CSS, so write it in
  sentence case and it will render uppercase.)
- **Vary the sentence opening across a set.** When writing four cards or five section headings that
  sit down one page, do not start more than two of them with the same word. A round came back with
  eleven of sixteen lines opening "I" — read down a page that is a chant, and a repeated
  grammatical shape is a device just as much as a slogan is. The section headings are centred in
  the layout, which makes a repeated opening word visually obvious.

**The value test.** Cover the company name, the dates and the job title. Does the sentence still
tell a buyer what they get? If it only works because a logo is attached, rewrite it.

**The CV test.** If a line could be the top bullet of a CV, it fails. Sell the value, not the
record.

## 4. Hard constraints — these have all killed copy before

1. **No universal quantifiers and no unfalsifiable claims** — *every*, *always*, *never*, *all*.
2. **No comparison to other people**, predecessors, competitors or colleagues. No dismissal of any
   part of the work, his own or anyone else's. This killed four options in one round and a line on
   13 August for reading as arrogant.
3. **No industry noun in display type** — not *software*, not *edtech*, not *SaaS*, in a headline
   or a section heading. (Body copy may name a sector where it is factual.)
4. **Answerable in an interview from the published CV.** If he would be caught out being asked to
   expand on it, it is wrong.
5. **No count of references and no number that can move.** Six references may become seven.
6. **Cite or die.** Every factual clause must trace to something in the evidence register. Do not
   invent a figure, a client name, a date or an outcome. If you need a number and do not have one,
   write the sentence without a number and flag it.
7. **Never combine, divide or compound two separately-given figures into a third.** No annualising,
   no percentages derived from two other percentages, no totals built from separate line items.
8. **Do not use *build* as Brad's verb.** His words: *"it suggests I build software or apps — I
   don't."* Use *originate*, *shape*, *get it made*, *get it to market*, *make it happen*.
9. **The relationship must be present.** Brad: *"relationships is a key part of sales — that's
   nowhere."* But state the relationship as a consequence, never as a self-assessment. "A very
   close working relationship" is banned. The fact underneath it is not — for example, a vendor
   telling a partner how their terms compare with other partners' terms *demonstrates* the
   relationship without claiming it. **Publish the evidence, never the adjective.**
10. **Concessions travel with their claims.** If a retention claim is qualified — mixed across a
    book of accounts, one flagship held personally — the qualification stays in the same sentence
    or the next one. Do not strip it for punchiness.
11. **Hedges survive verbatim.** "around 70", "around 120", "between two and six hours". Do not
    round them, tighten them or drop the hedge.
12. **itslearning is always lower case.**
13. **Never write "four industries."**
14. **Never name the education employer** (2015–2025). The role is described by what it was, not by
    who it was for. The dispute that ended it is under a settlement gag — do not go near it.
15. **Never publish these names:** Deane Jessep, 95bFM, or any Canwest sales figures. The "400 per
    cent" figure is dead and does not come back.
16. **Nothing implying an AI product has shipped.** The AI work is consulting and systems, not a
    launched product.
17. **MySimpleSiteMan is the one failure story** — singular, human, not a pattern. It is not
    evidence of resilience-as-a-trait and must not be framed that way.

## 5. Character budgets — measured from the built layout

**These are the important part of this brief.** Every one is derived from the real CSS measure and
type size on the live page. Copy that lands inside the band lays out cleanly at every screen width
from 390px to 1440px. Copy outside the band forces a layout change or breaks badly on a phone.

Count characters **including spaces and punctuation**. Give a character count beside every option
you produce.

| Where it goes | Ideal | Hard ceiling | Renders as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hero headline** (h1, 26ch measure) | 55–75 | 80 | 3 lines desktop, 3 lines phone |
| **Hero support paragraph** (55ch measure) | 150–230 | 260 | 3 lines desktop, 5 lines phone |
| **Eyebrow pill** (uppercase, 0.12em tracking) | 20–34 | 46 | one line, must not wrap |
| **Section heading** (h2, 24ch measure) | 30–48 | 55 | 2 lines |
| **Section lead** (55ch measure) | 90–150 | 180 | 2 lines |
| **Capability card heading** (h3, 30ch measure) | 25–45 | 55 | 1–2 lines |
| **Capability card summary** (small grey line) | 25–45 | 55 | 1 line |
| **Capability card body** (62ch measure) | 140–220 | 260 | 3–4 lines |
| **Capability card evidence** (62ch, small, boxed) | 70–130 | 160 | 1–2 lines |
| **Work cell heading** (22ch measure) | 25–42 | 50 | 2 lines |
| **Work cell body** (in a half-width cell) | 130–200 | 240 | 4–5 lines |
| **Career row title** (lead size, 1 line wanted) | 28–48 | 58 | 1 line desktop |
| **Career row detail** (62ch measure) | 100–260 | 300 | 2–4 lines |
| **Standfirst** (55ch measure) | 150–240 | 280 | 3 lines |

**Uppercase text costs more.** The eyebrow pill is uppercase with wide letter-spacing, so its
budget is much tighter than its font size suggests. 34 characters is a comfortable pill; 46 is the
point where it wraps to two lines inside its border and looks like a broken component.

## 6. Line-break rules — the layout fault to write around

Brad's standing instruction: **no sentence may break so that a single word sits alone on the line
below.** A stranded word at the end of a paragraph is the specific fault to avoid.

You cannot control where lines break, but you can write lines that break well:

- **Avoid hyphenated compounds** near the end of a line-length unit. A browser will break at the
  hyphen. "long-term", "full-cycle", "go-to-market", "better-fit" are all break opportunities.
  If a compound is genuinely the right word, use it and flag it — the build inserts a
  non-breaking hyphen, but it has to be told which word.
- **Avoid slashes.** "UI/UX", "sales/delivery" are break opportunities for the same reason.
- **Prefer a short final clause over a long one.** A sentence ending "...and grow the account" rags
  better than one ending "...and grow the account relationship over time".
- **Do not end a paragraph with a one- or two-word clause.** That is exactly what strands.
- **Aim for the middle of the band, not the ceiling.** Copy at the ceiling has no room to move when
  the type scales down at 640px.

## 7. What to give back

For each section requested, produce **8–12 options**. For every option:

1. The line itself.
2. The character count in brackets.
3. One line on which constraint or argument it satisfies.

Do not produce a recommendation or a "strongest option" ranking unless asked. Brad chooses.

Flag anything you were unsure about rather than smoothing over it — particularly any place you
wanted a number and did not have one, and any compound word or slash you had to use.

## 8. Section blocks — paste ONE of these under the brief

### 8a. Eyebrow pill (above the hero)

Currently reads "Account management · retention & growth". That is too narrow now: the hero
headline is *"Helping SaaS and digital businesses turn customer needs into growth"* and the support
line covers sales, account management, delivery, UI/UX and web strategy. The pill fences the page
to one of those five.

**Job:** a short uppercase label that frames the breadth without contradicting the headline, or
that names the target role plainly. It sits directly above the headline and is read first.

**Budget:** 20–34 characters ideal, 46 hard ceiling. Uppercase. A middot (·) is available as a
separator. It must not wrap.

### 8b. The four capability cards

Four cards, currently labelled *Sales Growth · Product Adoption · AI & Product · Product & UX*.
Those are topic labels. They need to become four arguments — a claim a hiring manager can act on.

The four arguments, in order:

1. **Finding out what the customer actually needs before selling anything.** This is the
   differentiator and it is currently absent from the site entirely.
2. **The accounts kept are the accounts that grow.** Retention and expansion as one motion. The
   qualification travels with it: results were mixed across a book of accounts, with the flagship
   account held personally.
3. **Originating the thing the customer buys next.** Spotting the gap, shaping the offer, getting
   it made and to market. Cards 2 and 3 sit next to each other because 3 is the mechanism behind 2.
4. **Negotiating the terms that make the rest possible.** The load-bearing fact: a reseller whose
   vendor bills the end customer does not own the renewal conversation. The accounts were his to
   keep because he made them his to bill.

**Each card needs four pieces:**

- Heading — 25–45 characters, ceiling 55. This is the argument in short form. Past tense if it
  states a record, present tense if it states a capability.
- Summary line — 25–45 characters, ceiling 55. Small grey line under the heading. A compression, not
  a repetition.
- Body — 140–220 characters, ceiling 260. What the work actually is, in concrete nouns.
- Evidence line — 70–130 characters, ceiling 160. One specific, checkable thing. This sits in a box
  and its job is to make the claim above it believable.

### 8c. Section headings and leads

Five sections down the page, each with the same three-part header: an uppercase eyebrow pill, a
statement heading, and a grey lead line under it. They are the spine of the page — a reader
skimming in forty seconds reads these five headings and nothing else, so each one has to carry
its section's argument on its own.

**Budget for every one of them:** pill 20–34 (ceiling 46, uppercase, must not wrap), heading 30–48
(ceiling 55, two lines), lead 90–150 (ceiling 180, two lines).

Request one section at a time, or all five, but keep them consistent in register — they are read
as a set. Here is what is live now and what each section actually contains.

**1. About Me — the capability section**

- Pill: `About Me`
- Heading: `Sell it, then make it stick.`
- Lead: `Pipeline, proposals, adoption strategy, AI-enabled systems. All of it starts with
  understanding the customer.`

*What the section holds:* the four capability cards from §8b — finding out what the customer needs,
keeping accounts that grow, originating what they buy next, negotiating the terms. *Problem with
the current copy:* "Sell it, then make it stick" is a two-part device with a punchline, which is
the advertisement register that gets rejected. "About Me" is also a weak pill for a section that is
actually about capability, not biography. Both are open.

**2. Work**

- Pill: `Work`
- Heading: `The system is the proof.`
- Lead: `Demand, pipeline, proposals, adoption. Then AI, product and UX. The whole revenue path.`

*What the section holds:* four work cells — ten years selling and growing a SaaS platform across NZ
and Australia (licensed users 2,800 to 24,500); current AI product consulting; a sales operations
role that included the process behind a fourteen-person telemarketing team and the billing
structure; and the earlier media and web growth years. *Problem:* "The system is the proof" is
abstract and does not say what a reader gets. The lead is a list of nouns with no verb.

**3. The proof — reference letters**

- Pill: `The proof`
- Heading: `The customers who nearly left said it themselves.`
- Lead: `Both wrote in 2025. Both describe keeping an account that had a reason to go.`

*What the section holds:* two verbatim quotes from 2025 reference letters, both from customers who
stayed. There are six letters in total, published on a separate page. *Constraint:* do not count the
references in copy — six may become seven. *This one is closest to working already* — it is plain,
it is specific, and the heading states a fact rather than a slogan. Options should not drift far
from it.

**4. Career**

- Pill: `Career`
- Heading: `The range came one job at a time.`
- Lead: `Generate demand. Shape the offer. Close the work. Enable users. Then grow the account.`
- Standfirst under the lead: `I kept the bulk of the accounts I won. Not all of them, and not
  everywhere — but the billing, the contracts, the renewals and the relationship were mine at every
  job I have had since 2001.`

*What the section holds:* the seven career rows from §8d, plus one paragraph about a failed product.
*Note:* this section has a fourth element the others do not — the standfirst, at 150–240 characters
(ceiling 280). It carries the retention claim with its concession attached, and it is doing real
work. If you rewrite it, the concession ("not all of them, and not everywhere") must survive.

**5. Blog**

- Pill: `Blog`
- Heading: `I write about the work, and occasionally about a beetroot tin.`
- Lead: `Web strategy, communications, AI, and the odd true story. Two of them are already up.`

*What the section holds:* the two most recent blog posts, pulled automatically. *Two problems:* the
heading is 61 characters, over the 55 ceiling. And "Two of them are already up" goes stale the
moment a third post publishes — the replacement lead must not state a count.

### 8d. Career row titles

Seven rows down the career section, one per role. Each row is two lines: a **title** in dark text,
and underneath it a grey **meta** line carrying the year, the job title and the employer.

**The device:** the title is not the job title. It is **the hireable capability that job proves** —
what a reader would want from someone doing it. The job title is already in the meta line
underneath, so repeating it wastes the strongest line on the row.

**Tense:** past tense for roles that are finished, because they state a record. The two most recent
rows are present tense because the capability is current. This has reverted once and been caught
once, so hold it.

**Budget:** 28–48 characters, ceiling 58. Must hold one line at desktop width.

Here are all seven as they stand. The meta line is fixed fact and does not change; only the title
is open.

| # | Current title | Meta line (fixed) |

| 2 | Held the flagship account myself | 2001 · Business development, Adplus Advertising (later Tracta) |
| 3 | Kept a national account for the whole tenure | 2003 · Sales, Canwest Media |

**What sits underneath each one, so the title is true:**

1. **2000AD, 1999.** Sold an event that did not exist yet — sponsorship and participation committed
   before there was anything to see.
2. **Adplus, 2001.** Held the agency's flagship account personally rather than passing it to an

3. **Canwest, 2003.** Held the Harvey Norman account for the full three years of the tenure. *Do

capability lands without the implied judgement is the specific job on that row.
