## The draft

### The Territory

The school was raising money and the prize for selling the most was a scientific calculator.

I was eleven. I heard the word *calculator* and something in me went very quiet and very focused, the
way it does before you decide a thing is already yours and you are simply waiting for everyone else
to find out.

They gave every student a sample pack — sandwich bags and freezer bags — and a form to write the
orders on. One sheet of paper. It had columns for the name and the quantity, and the sheet was clearly
built on the assumption that you would sell to an aunty, and possibly a grandmother, and then stop.

I looked at the sheet and I understood it was not going to be adequate.

So before I sold a single bag, I got a book.

---

We lived six kilometres out of Hastings, on a farm — ten acres of raspberries and thirty thousand
chickens. I had a Raleigh ten speed, light blue and silver. The samples went in my school bag with
the order book, and after school every day I rode into town and worked the suburbs.

Akina. Frimley. I did not do the whole town. I did the parts that felt safe, and the way I decided
what was safe was the lawns. A well mown lawn meant a kind person lived there. That was the entire
system. I do not know where I got it from and I ran it for the whole campaign without ever once
questioning it, and I still could not tell you today whether it was a good system or whether I simply
never tested it against anything.

Knock. Sample pack. Sandwich or freezer. Signature in the book.

Then move on, because there was no second sale to be had at that door — a person buys their bags once
— and standing about being liked was not what I was there for.

Nobody was ever unkind to me. Not once. I have gone looking through that whole period for the door
that got slammed and there is not one in there. A no meant nothing at all to me. A no was a house
that was simply not the house, and the next one was thirty seconds away, and one of them was going to
be a yes.

Then the weekends, as well. It ran the whole of term three — two and a half months of it — and by
the second week it did not feel like a fundraiser any more, it felt like a job.

And at the end of every day, whenever the light started going and wherever my last house had put me,
I got back on the bike and rode the six or seven kilometres home. It depended where I finished. That
was the only part of the arrangement I would call work.

I do not think my parents ever fully understood the extent of it.

I think, by that stage, they had learned to just hold on and hope for the best.

---

There was an assembly at the end. The whole school in the hall, parents down the back, and I went up
in front of all of them and collected the calculator.

Afterwards the principal spoke to my mother, and she told me later what he had said, which was that I
had very nearly outsold the entire school combined.

I was not there for that part. It reached me the way most true things reach a child — secondhand,
from your mother, in the kitchen, some time after the fact and slightly flattened by the trip
across.

I did not have a number of my own. Nobody ever gave me one. I had a book full of signatures and a
principal's sentence arriving via my mother, and to this day that is the whole of the evidence.

---

The calculator was a Casio.

I want to be honest about the calculator. It was not the Casio I wanted. The Casio I wanted was the
one with Space Invaders built into it, which a boy could actually use.

I did not know what a cosine was. I had no reason to square anything.

It sat on my shelf.

## Structural decisions, with the argument against each

### The repetition beat is the ride home, not the customers

**Required by the interview.** Brad: *"it was a single sale product. Once a signature was colected I
promptly moved on to find more buyers."* There were no repeat customers and there was no route.

`NEXT-SESSION-PROMPT.md:104-107` had assumed the opposite and built the four-beat arc on it. **The
draft does not manufacture repeat business to fill the structural slot** — the recurrence the reader
feels is the six-or-seven kilometre ride at the end of every day, and the paragraph is placed so that
it lands after the knocking rather than among it.

### The duration is in the post

**RESOLVED — Brad, 15 August 2026: "Change it to one term, which is two and a half months — started
at the beginning of term 3."** The 2.5 months had come from the older
`design/05-younger-years-edit-prompt.md` précis and was unconfirmed at first draft; Brad has now
confirmed it directly, with the added detail that it was term three specifically.

**Added at "Then the weekends, as well"** — the paragraph the earlier draft note flagged as built to
take it — as *"It ran the whole of term three — two and a half months of it — and by the second week
it did not feel like a fundraiser any more, it felt like a job."* The added sentence carries the
figure and one confirmed texture detail (the shift from fundraiser to job) rather than the bare
number alone.

**This is now the post's one figure that is a genuine quantity claim** rather than biographical
scene-setting, and it is Brad's own confirmed number, given twice, consistently. §4.2 re-run
required before publish — see review section below.

### The principal's line is reported speech and Brad is not in the room

**Required by Brad's answer, 15 August 2026:** *"at the assembly... Mum told me later."*

The tempting version has the boy overhearing an adult say something about him. **That version is
barred** — he did not hear it. So the draft states plainly that he was not there and that it reached
him through his mother.

**Against:** the overheard version is a better scene, and the draft gives it up.

**For, and it is the stronger argument:** the claim is the biggest in either childhood story, and
having it arrive secondhand is what makes it survivable. `design/09-blog-voice.md` §2's *let the
customers do the praising* applies exactly here — **the most extraordinary claim in either post is
the one Brad is not making.** The sentence *"I did not have a number of my own"* is the draft doing
that work openly, and it is also simply true.

### No figure attaches to the comparative

The principal's statement publishes **attributed and unquantified.** The interview file is explicit:
*"attributing the comparative does not license quantifying it."*

**Barred and absent from the draft:** the "over 100 sales" reconstruction (derived in-interview from
280 students × an assumed participation rate), the unit price, the school's cut, and any total
raised. The 280 student figure is also absent, because its only function in the record was as an
input to the barred arithmetic.

### No rejection scene

**Required by the interview.** Brad recalls no hostility whatsoever. The door-slam is the obvious
dramatic temptation in a door-knocking story and **there is nothing behind it.**

The draft handles this by making the absence itself the material — *"I have gone looking through that
whole period for the door that got slammed and there is not one in there"* — which is honest about
the memory rather than filling it in.

### The order book is placed before the selling starts

The interview's second-strongest detail, and the decision was made in advance: *"the single sheet
distributed by the school was never going to cut it."* Not after filling the sheet. Before.

**The draft ends the first section on it** — *"So before I sold a single bag, I got a book"* — because
that is the moment the story stops being about a school fundraiser. The school's form is described in
enough detail for the reader to see what it assumed, and the assumption is never named.

### The lawns heuristic is not explained

§3.1's hardest rule and the interview's own instruction: *do not explain it.* The draft states the
system, admits he does not know where it came from, and stops — including the admission that he never
tested it. **That admission is not an explanation.** It is the narrator being accurate about the
limits of his own evidence, which is the register, and it happens to be the same move the AI category
requires of him thirty years later.

### The ending is the shelf

The unused calculator, stated flat, with the wanting left entirely alone. **The story's subject is
the wanting and the post must not say so.**

The last four lines are deliberately short and set apart. *"It sat on my shelf"* is the object; the
refusal is the whole preceding campaign, which the reader is holding while they read it. Same
structure as FORT COMMANDER, inverted — Brad's example ends on a badge he will not give back, this
one ends on a prize he had no use for.

**Against:** it is an anticlimax by construction, and a reader who wants the story to pay off in
triumph will feel cheated. **That is the intended effect** and softening it — a line about what the
effort was really worth — would be the stated moral.

**Self-praise check, expanded**, because this post is the harder of the two for §4.3.3: the draft
contains *"something in me went very quiet and very focused"* and *"standing about being liked was not
what I was there for."* Both are **statements about the eleven-year-old's conduct at the time, not
claims about the man writing.** §2's rule is *claim about the work, not about the self*, and a
narrator reporting his own childhood behaviour is reporting work. **Passes, but it is the sentence a
stricter reviewer would query**, so it is recorded here rather than left for someone to find.
