## The draft

### The Tin

I was seven years old and I needed a Cubs uniform.

Mum said we could not afford it. She said it plainly, the way you tell a child a fact rather than a
disappointment, and I accepted the fact and went looking for a solution. I did not think I was doing
anything unusual. I thought I was being helpful.

We lived in Manningham, South Australia. It is a street like every other street in that part of the
world — one side, then the other side, and nothing in between worth crossing for.

I found a tin in the kitchen. Sliced beetroot. It still had the purple on the inside from what it
used to be, and I checked the rim with my thumb to make sure there was nothing sharp on it, because
people were going to be putting their fingers in there and it seemed rude to cut them.

Then I went to work.

I started at one end and I went down one side of the street, and then I came back up the other side.
I do not remember crossing a road. I do not think I was allowed to.

At every door I said the same thing.

I would like to ask you to please help me get a Cubs uniform, because I want to go to Cubs, and Mum
said we cannot afford it.

They were middle class Australian housewives, mostly, answering their own front doors in the middle
of an afternoon. I do not remember a single one of them saying no. I do not remember any of their
faces either. I remember the sound the coins made going into a tin that used to have beetroot in it,
which is a specific sound, and which I liked.

It took one afternoon. By the end of it I had enough.

I was wearing stubbies, a striped t-shirt, and thongs, which is what Australians call jandals.

---

I went home and I told them.

That is the part I would change, if I were allowed to change it, and I am not. I did not hide the
tin. I did not put it under my bed and produce the money later with a story attached to it. I walked
in the door and I told my mother and my father that it was all sorted, that I could go to Cubs now,
because we had the money.

I had earned it.

I was very proud.

My mother looked at the tin.

"What on earth have you been doing! You little bugger, you'll return this money to every
house!"

Then she kicked me up the bum, and she put her shoes on, and she walked me back out the front door.

We did the whole street again. Both sides. Every house I had been to, in the same order I had been to
them, and at each one I stood on the step and said sorry and gave the money back, and she stood
behind me while I did it.

It took considerably longer than the first pass had.

---

I got the uniform.

The money was miraculously found, which is the phrase I have always used for it and I have never
examined it too closely.

I remember putting it on. I remember pulling the leather bobble up the scarf, tight to my neck, and
standing in front of the mirror and being amazed at how clean it was. How important a thing it was to
own. Every badge I won after that, my mother sewed onto it herself.

The uniform lasted a year, maybe two, before I grew out of it.

The walk back lasted longer. I have spent thirty years knocking on doors and asking people whether I
can help them, and what comes back comes back after. It took a beetroot tin and one furious woman
marching me up my own street to teach me which way round it goes.

I still do not own a branded shirt.

## Decisions taken in this draft, with the argument against each

### The street is named as suburb and state only

**RESOLVED — Brad, 15 August 2026: "Manningham, South Australia."** No street name, no house number,
no Adelaide. The draft published "Arthur Avenue, in Manningham, in Adelaide" and Brad narrowed it
himself. **Applied.**

### The mother's line is softened

**RESOLVED — Brad, 15 August 2026: "Soften it."** "What the bloody hell" is replaced with "What on
earth"; "you little bugger" is kept. The interview file's recommendation was to publish the profanity
as given; Brad overruled it. **Applied — recorded as an argument-against per the standing
convention.**

**Argument against the change, for the record:** the line was the most alive sentence in the entire
project record and softening it moves the mother slightly toward device rather than person. Brad's
call stands regardless.

### "That is the whole trade" is cut

The interview file's approved ending contained it and flagged it: *"close to a stated moral and the
piece is stronger without it. Cut unless Brad wants the point unmissable."*

**RESOLVED — Brad, 15 August 2026: "Leave it cut."** With it, the paragraph explains what it has just
shown, which is the exact failure §3.1 exists to prevent. **Confirmed final — not reopening.**

### The branded-shirt line has no causal verb attaching it to anything

This is the hardest constraint in the file and the reason the last line sits alone with white space
above it. Brad confirmed the aversion **arrived later in life, independently**. The tidy version —
*the badge taught me not to need badges, which is why I have never worn a branded uniform since* — is
**invented biography** and would be the only undefensible claim on the site.

So: no *therefore*, no *which is why*, no *since*. Two facts that rhyme, with a rule between them.
**If a reader draws the causal line themselves, that is the register working. If the draft draws it,
that is a fabrication.**

### The father's business does not appear

Closed by the interview file. The approved pitch is *"Mum said we couldn't afford it — so I thought I
was genuinely helping."* The third-party allegation does not publish in any form and the business is
not background in this draft — it is absent.

### "I thought I was being helpful" is placed early, not at the end

Brad's approved pitch line carries the story's engine — the gap between what the child believed he
was doing and what he was actually doing. **Placing it in the third paragraph rather than the last
means the reader carries the gap through the whole piece** rather than having it explained to them at
the close. It is the same information doing more work.

**Against:** it slightly softens the mother's entrance, because the reader has already been told he
meant well and is therefore less shocked on his behalf.

### No figures anywhere

Constraint 2. There is no amount raised and no uniform cost, because neither exists. **Sufficiency is
the only quantity in the story** — "enough" and "one afternoon" — and both are Brad's own words.
