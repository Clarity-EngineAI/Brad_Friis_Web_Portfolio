# Prompt for exploring presentation formats (paste into another LLM)

Status: ready to use. Copy everything in the fenced block below into another LLM
(ChatGPT, Gemini, etc). Not for pasting into the site — this is a creative-exploration
prompt only.

---

```
I'm building a personal portfolio website (bradfriis.com) and want your help thinking
through how to visually present one section of it. I need creative options, not a
build spec — I'll pick a direction afterward and have someone else implement it.

CONTEXT

I spent roughly 10 years in a senior, end-to-end operating role at an education
technology company that resold and supported a K-12 learning management system
platform (the underlying product was Schoology, distributed in my market through a
partnership with the vendor PowerSchool — I can name PowerSchool and Schoology
specifically, but not my employer's name, which I need to keep out of this entirely
for legal reasons unrelated to the work itself). The role ended in September 2025.

Over that decade I was effectively the whole go-to-market and operations function for
that platform in my region — not a narrow specialist. Here is the real scope, in the
categories I think in:

1. Sales — strategy and delivery: prospecting, sales assets (presentations, demos,
   phone scripts, email automation sequences), lead generation, proposals, EULAs,
   financial projections and modelling.

2. Implementation strategy — technical delivery of the platform into new customer
   environments.

3. Customer onboarding: across leadership, staff, students, parents and support
   staff, often bespoke per customer. Training design and material development —
   video, self-paced courses, workshops (both generic and bespoke), webinars,
   designed and delivered end to end. Plus generating supporting marketing assets as
   needed, often one-pagers promoting new features or tools.

4. Marketing: designing and building exhibition space at conferences, conference
   engagement and lead-generation strategy, presenting workshops at conferences,
   negotiating major sponsorship of several national conferences in exchange for
   software access to help run the conference itself. Website design and
   development. Email marketing and video production. Built and maintained a
   nationwide network of ~650 teachers and school leaders for sharing resources and
   ideas.

5. Support: designed and developed all support materials, response-time standards,
   and the MSA (master service agreement) framework.

6. Financial strategy: billing design and development, accounts and payments, SLAs,
   invoicing design, annual renewal strategy and management of renewing customers,
   and a referral program I designed and ran, including managing reciprocal referral
   relationships.

7. Accredited government facilitator: I was an accredited facilitator for a
   government ministry, delivering ministry-funded training and strategic guidance
   to schools. I also provided technical support for, and helped design, an
   integration tool that saved schools a minimum of 8 days of database work each.

8. Staff management: both internal staff and external contractors.

9. Regular board reporting.

10. International reseller relationship management: partnership contract
    negotiation and renewal, licensing and reseller terms, and liaising across every
    stakeholder group on both sides — sales, marketing, technical, support, renewals,
    leadership.

That's likely not exhaustive — treat it as close to complete but not certainly so.

THE PROBLEM I'M TRYING TO SOLVE

My site currently communicates my career mostly through prose — headline claims on
cards, paragraphs of copy. But a huge part of what I'm actually good at is web
design, visual communication, and marketing production. A wall of text undersells
that; it doesn't show the reader that I can also design and build things visually. I
need this particular page to demonstrate visual/design competence through its own
presentation, not just describe it in words.

My working instinct was a hub-and-spoke diagram: me in the centre, categories like
"Sales" or "Marketing" arranged around me, each expanding on hover/tap into the list
of responsibilities within it. I deliberately do NOT want you to treat that as the
brief. I want you to set it aside and think freely.

WHAT I WANT FROM YOU

Propose THREE genuinely different visual/structural approaches for presenting this
scope of work on a webpage. Not three variations on a hub-and-spoke wheel — three
different underlying metaphors or structures. For each one:

- Describe the core visual/interaction idea in a few sentences.
- Explain why it suits "breadth and depth of an end-to-end operator," specifically —
  what does this format prove about me that a list of bullet points doesn't?
- Address mobile explicitly. A desktop-only hover-reveal idea is not acceptable on
  its own — tell me how each concept degrades or adapts on a phone screen (tap
  instead of hover is not a sufficient answer by itself; think about layout,
  not just interaction swap).
- Flag the build complexity honestly (e.g., "this is a CSS/SVG layout," vs "this
  needs a canvas/WebGL library," vs "this is achievable with well-structured HTML
  and a bit of JS").
- Note any risk that the format could overwhelm or bury the content rather than
  clarify it — I'd rather know that upfront than find out after it's built.

Do not write actual page copy or category labels beyond what's needed to illustrate
each concept. This is a format/structure exploration, not a copy draft.

One more open question, which you can factor into your recommendation but don't need
to resolve definitively: I haven't yet decided whether this page is aimed at
landing me short-term/fractional consulting work with edtech companies, or a
permanent senior operating role, or is deliberately written to work for either. If
the visual format you're proposing works better for one of those than the other,
say so.
```
