Decision one

Operations is purple. It should not be.

Growth (sienna) and Delivery (teal) are settled and unchanged in all four. Only the Operations family moves. The test that matters is the bottom row of each card: a 3px rule is how the colour actually appears on a calendar card, and it is where the current violet was chosen to fix a clash with teal.

Decision two

The pills read as component-library default.

They are 999px lozenges with a 1px hairline on white, repeated sixteen times in two sizes. Form never varies; only size and hue do. That uniformity is the signature you are reacting to. All four below are live — click them. Operations is showing warm graphite so you are judging one decision at a time.

Current

In V3 now

Pill radius, hairline border, white fill, coloured text on selection.

The default badge of every generated UI since 2023.

Borrows a shape language the rest of the page does not use — the site is 10px corners and flat grounds.

Sixteen identical silhouettes give the eye no way to tell row one from row two except size.

A · Rules, not pills

Alternative

Border and fill removed entirely. Each control is a label on a rule that thickens and colours when selected.

The most restrained option, and the closest to the site's rule-driven manner.

Makes the filter row a literal legend for the 3px rules on the cards below.

Reads as tabs. Tabs imply one selection at a time, but this filter is additive and multi-select.

The 44px tap target is invisible, which hurts most on the phone.

B · Ledger chips

Recommended

10px corners, no border, flat tile, 3px coloured left edge — the same device the calendar cards already use.

The filter and the calendar become visibly the same material. No other option does this — compare the chips to the cards below them.

Uses only tokens the site already ships: --r-sm, --surface-alt, the 3px rule.

Selection is a solid fill, which is unmistakable at a glance and survives being small on a phone.

Denser and heavier than the current row. Twelve filled chips is a lot of colour when everything is on.

C · Underline set

Alternative

Pill silhouette kept, fill and border stripped. Colour arrives as an underline that grows on selection.

Lightest of the four. The filter bar almost disappears until used.

The grow animation makes selection feel deliberate.

Least differentiated from what is there now, so it does least about the original complaint.

Unselected chips have no edge at all, so the row reads as loose text rather than controls.

Decision three

On a phone, eight of the twelve chips cannot be found.

Measured at 390px: the chip row is 1,303px wide inside a 390px viewport, and the scrollbar is deliberately hidden. Roughly 913px of controls sit off-screen with no affordance saying so. Both options below are shown in a phone frame at real width. The three pillar buttons fit at 334px and are kept in both.

A · Sheet

Recommended

One "Areas" button opens the existing bottom sheet, listing all twelve grouped by pillar. No new pattern.

Filter 30 of 30

All twelve visible at once, grouped and labelled by pillar. Nothing hidden.

Reuses the dialog the page already ships for entry detail. No new component.

Full-width 44px rows are the easiest thing to hit one-handed.

Saves ~50px of sticky bar height, because row two collapses to one button.

Selecting takes a tap to open and a tap to close. Two taps, not one.

B · Native select

Alternative

A plain multi-select. The OS renders the picker.

Filter 30 of 30
The OS picker cannot show colour swatches, so the link between a filter and the coloured rule on a card is broken at exactly the moment the reader needs it.

Zero custom code. Free accessibility.

No colour. The whole point of the filter is that it keys the calendar's colours.

Looks like a form control on a page that has none. Renders differently on every OS.

If you want one answer

Graphite, ledger chips, sheet, both cuts.

  1. Colour: A, warm graphite. Purple was a patch for a clash. Ink is not a patch — it is the honest answer for the structural pillar, and it makes the page look more restrained rather than less considered.
  2. Pills: B, ledger chips. It is the only option that makes the filter and the calendar the same material, and it invents nothing — 10px corners and a 3px left rule are both already on the page.
  3. Mobile: A, sheet. The scrolling row hides a third of the content. The sheet reuses a dialog you already ship.
  4. Length: cuts 1 and 2. Together they put the first calendar entry at 288px, above the fold, with the H1 still on screen. Neither touches a word of copy.
