## The prompt

You are editing raw autobiographical material into short, punchy timeline copy for a personal
website's "Life's Journey" section. The material below is Brad's own writing — his facts, his
voice, his typos. Your job is compression, not composition.

**Hard rules:**

1. Do not invent, embellish, or infer any fact, number, date, or detail that isn't already in the
   source. If a sentence is ambiguous, cut toward the safest reading rather than filling the gap.
2. Fix spelling and grammar typos only (e.g. "askng" → "asking", "campaing" → "campaign"). Do not
   smooth away the voice — keep the dry understatement, the deadpan outcomes, the specific numbers.
3. NZ English spelling throughout.
4. No AI slop. Specifically banned:
   - Em-dash-stacked sentences ("He did X — and Y — before finally Z")
   - "It's not just X, it's Y" or any variant
   - Rule-of-three adjective pileups ("bold, ambitious, and unforgettable")
   - Generic uplifting wrap-up lines that aren't in the source
   - Corporate or motivational-poster vocabulary ("journey," "passion," "authentic" — unless
     Brad used the word himself)
   - Every entry forced into the identical grammatical shape. Ten entries in the same template
     reads like a mail-merge, not a life. Vary sentence rhythm and length entry to entry.
5. Each entry: keep the existing title/caption line as-is (don't touch those). Cut the body to
   1–2 sentences — just enough to land what happened and the punchline outcome. If an entry has
   two good beats, keep the sharper one and cut the other rather than keeping both at half-strength.
6. Where you're genuinely torn between two cuts of the same entry, present both and say why,
   rather than silently picking one.

**Output format:** a numbered list, Role 1 through Role 10. For each: the title line unchanged,
then the edited body, then a one-line note on what you cut and why (so Brad can check the cut
against his memory of the actual event before it goes anywhere near the live site).

**Do not** write the result back into the CSV or any other file. Output the edited copy in the
response for Brad to review first.
