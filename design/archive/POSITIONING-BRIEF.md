# Framer AI site strategy

**Written:** 13 August 2026, 11:37 AM NZST  
**Updated:** 13 August 2026, 1:17 PM NZST

## Assessment

Use the Prolens template, but do not use it as designed.

The template is a good base because its bones match the job: one-page personal brand, fast hero,
About section, proof cards, career timeline, testimonials and contact. Brad does not need a complex
site right now. He needs a credible link a recruiter, hiring manager, commercial leader or founder can
open and understand in under a minute.

The risk is that Prolens is originally an entrepreneur/investor/advisor template. Left intact, it
makes Brad look like a generic business personality with a blog, social section and vague portfolio.
That is the wrong frame. Brad's strongest asset is not "personal brand". It is checkable commercial
proof: ten years growing SaaS accounts in the education market, customer retention under competitive pressure,
written references and a warm, specific sales instinct that started early.

The public template listing confirms this read: Prolens is sold as a personal website for
entrepreneurs, investors and business leaders, with a clean structure for journey, projects and
expertise. That makes it directionally right for Brad, but only after replacing the generic
business-person frame with specific employment proof.

So the answer is yes, but only with a disciplined conversion strategy:

- Keep the template's simple scroll, generous spacing and personal tone.
- Replace the entrepreneur/investor frame with a fuller commercial-operator frame: sales growth,
  lead generation, proposal development, revenue systems, product adoption, AI workflows, UX and
  design systems.
- Make every section prove one thing: Brad can understand the customer, shape the offer, build the
  sales system, win the work, onboard the users and keep the account growing.
- Hide or delete sections that create empty obligations: blog cards, social-network blocks and a long
  life-story timeline.
- Use the Cubs story in About Me, not the career timeline. It gives warmth, vulnerability and humour
  without making the professional proof feel juvenile.
- Make references and CV access obvious. This site is not finished until a hiring manager can reach
  the CV and written letters quickly.

## Recommendation

Use Prolens for the urgent launch. Do not spend time shopping for a better template yet.

The better move is to turn Prolens into a tight proof-led personal site, test the live first screen on
mobile and desktop, then decide whether the template is limiting the message. Right now the message
is the constraint, not the layout.

## Website Measurement Brief

**Written:** 13 August 2026, 12:58 PM NZST

Use this section as the standard for judging the site while it is being built. The site is working if
a recruiter, hiring manager, founder or commercial leader can understand Brad's full commercial
range quickly, believe the proof, and feel that contacting him is the natural next step.

### Primary Goal

The website should convert the right visitor into a conversation with Brad.

That visitor should leave with a clear answer to four questions:

1. Who is Brad?
2. What can he be hired for?
3. What proof supports the claim?
4. How do I contact him, read the CV or check the references?

The site is not trying to impress everyone. It is trying to make Brad memorable and credible for
senior commercial roles where sales growth, SaaS strategy, account growth, lead generation, proposal
development, revenue systems, product adoption, AI workflows, product thinking, UI/UX judgement and
clear communication all matter.

### Overall Intention

The site should feel like a confident personal introduction backed by evidence. It should not feel
like a generic consultant site, a startup agency landing page, a life-story memoir or a loose
portfolio of unrelated ventures.

The emotional effect should be:

- Warm enough that Brad feels easy to talk to.
- Commercial enough that the reader can see where he fits.
- Specific enough that the proof feels real.
- Simple enough that the reader is never made to work.

The best version of the site says: Brad listens well, sells carefully, builds the system behind the
sale, helps people adopt the product and now applies AI/product/design judgement to the same work.
EdTech is strong evidence, but it should not read as the desired next category.

### Audience

Build for these readers first:

- Recruiters looking for senior account management or customer success talent.
- Hiring managers in software, SaaS, technology, education or commercial growth teams.
- Founders and commercial leaders who need practical revenue, adoption or account-growth help.

Secondary audiences are acceptable, but they must not take over the page. Clarity Engine, AI
consulting, founder support and website strategy can appear as applied evidence of Brad's commercial
range. They should not turn the homepage into a generic AI consultancy offer.

### Positioning Standard

Every important section should reinforce one core idea:

Brad builds the commercial system around growth: he finds the opportunity, shapes the offer, builds
the pipeline, writes the proposal, closes the work, leads product adoption and grows the account.

Use proof before personality where the visitor needs confidence. Use personality before proof where
the visitor needs warmth. The homepage needs both, but the first screen must show the breadth without
becoming a checklist.

The strongest proof points are:

- SaaS experience that began at Xplore with monthly-fee bolt-on modules and continued through later
  SaaS-driven solutions.
- Ten years of SaaS growth in the education market across New Zealand and Australia.
- Licensed users growing from 2,800 to 24,500.
- A key account retained and expanded under competitive pressure.
- Full-cycle prospecting to close, including demos, proposals, Pipedrive pipeline management,
  conference sponsorship and presentation.
- Built adoption strategy, enablement workshops and stakeholder communication for non-technical users.
- Pikle positioning, pricing posture, proposal templates, CRM stages, four lead channels, sales asset
  kit, UX audit and brand/design governance.
- 400 per cent revenue growth at 99 Corporation, if confirmed for public use.
- 178 per cent of budget at Canwest Media.
- Written references spanning multiple parts of Brad's career.

### Design Styling

The look should be clean, editorial and human. Keep the Prolens template's spacing and rhythm, but
strip away anything that feels like a generic business-influencer template.

Design should favour:

- Strong first-screen hierarchy: name, role, proof, call to action.
- Generous white space and restrained sections.
- Calm contrast, readable type and clear buttons.
- A mobile-first scan path.
- Proof-led capability cards rather than decorative portfolio tiles.
- Enough visual space for sales systems, AI/product and design capability to appear credible, not
  bolted on.
- Real imagery, neutral professional imagery or simple visual treatments instead of stock startup
  photos.

Avoid:

- Busy animations that slow down comprehension.
- Abstract founder imagery.
- Over-polished agency visuals.
- Long scrolling sections that delay the proof.
- Weak contrast, tiny type or decorative text that hurts mobile readability.

### Look And Feel

The site should feel understated, not timid. Confident, not loud. Personal, not sentimental.

A good reference point is a senior commercial operator's proof page, not a coach's sales funnel. The
reader should feel they have met a person, but also that the person can walk into a commercial team
and be useful quickly.

The tone should be:

- Direct.
- Warm.
- Specific.
- Plain-spoken.
- Commercially aware.

It should not sound like:

- A motivational speaker.
- A generic SaaS consultant.
- A personal-brand influencer.
- A corporate CV copied onto a web page.

### Content Approach

Lead with the current commercial story, then support it with older proof. The page should move from
clarity to breadth, evidence, trust and contact.

Preferred order:

1. Hero: who Brad is, what he can be hired for, and how to contact him.
2. Capability map: sales growth, SaaS strategy, revenue systems, product adoption and AI/product work.
3. Proof: numbers, references and the SaaS decade in the education market.
4. Work: four checkable proof points across the commercial system, not a random project gallery.
5. References or Letters: short quotes with a clear path to the full letters.
6. CV and Contact: easy access, low friction.

Cut or hide anything that does not help the reader trust Brad or contact him. Empty blog cards, social
blocks, vague AI consulting sections and soft life-story timelines should not compete with the
conversion path. Specific AI/product/design evidence should stay. Do not let EdTech become the
category unless Brad explicitly chooses an EdTech role.

### Copy Rules

Use New Zealand English. Write in plain, specific sentences. Let the numbers and references carry the
praise.

Use words like:

- account growth
- customer success
- adoption
- retention
- commercial growth
- sales enablement
- lead generation
- proposals
- revenue systems
- product adoption
- enablement workshops
- AI workflows
- UX audit
- design governance
- trust
- practical systems

Avoid words and phrases like:

- passionate
- results-driven
- thought leader
- transformative
- game-changing
- cutting-edge
- entrepreneur/investor/advisor framing as the main identity
- vague claims about AI products that are not shipped
- visual design claims without artefacts or screenshots

### Conversion Standard

Every page section should pass at least one of these tests:

- It makes Brad easier to understand.
- It makes Brad easier to trust.
- It proves a commercial outcome.
- It proves a commercial capability, such as revenue systems, proposals, product adoption, AI/product
  work or design systems.
- It makes contacting Brad easier.
- It helps the reader check the CV or references.

If a section does none of those things, hide it for launch.

The primary call to action should be contact-focused. The secondary proof paths should be CV and
Letters. The visitor should never wonder whether the next step is to hire Clarity Engine, read a blog,
follow a social account or contact Brad. The answer is contact Brad.

### Development Checklist

Before launch, measure the site against this checklist:

- The first screen says Brad's name, role, proof and contact path without scrolling.
- The main navigation is Home, Work, Letters, CV and Contact.
- The homepage does not position Brad primarily as Clarity Engine, but it does show applied AI and
  product/design capability.
- The homepage does not make EdTech look like the desired next industry. Education is evidence, not
  the target.
- The strongest recent SaaS proof appears before older career history.
- The page makes full-cycle sales, lead generation, proposals, revenue systems and communication visible.
- Product adoption and enablement workshops are named without making Brad look like a training-only hire.
- Pikle/Clarity Engine work appears as specific AI/product/commercial proof, not vague AI consulting.
- UI, UX, branding, sales kits or app-design claims are backed by artefacts, screenshots or clear
  resume evidence.
- References feel like evidence, not decoration.
- The CV is easy to find.
- Contact is visible, simple and low-friction.
- Mobile reads cleanly in one pass.
- No public copy names GCT Education.
- No claim uses figures that still conflict between CV, LinkedIn and site copy.
- No placeholder blog, social, testimonial or project content is visible.
- The site feels like Brad: warm, direct, commercial and grounded.
