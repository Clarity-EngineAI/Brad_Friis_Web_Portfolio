Direction B — Technical · specification

Type pairing

IBM Plex Sans — display and body, 600 at display with −0.032em tracking so the hero sets tight and square. Plex has engineered detail without the neutrality problem: it is one of the few free grotesques that does not read as Inter, Helvetica or the default.

IBM Plex Mono — every piece of metadata on the page. Section indices, navigation, buttons, table headers, image data, quote framing. One superfamily, two voices, which is what makes the system feel designed rather than assembled.

Near-monochrome with a hard budget on the signal: four appearances per screen. Here they are the wordmark square, the section index, the full-stop at the end of the hero line, and the link to the letter. Vermilion rather than blue because blue is the default of every template in this category, and the point of a signal colour is that it signals.

Scale and spacing

  • Display clamp(36px, 4.7vw, 62px), line-height 1.05, tracking −0.032em
  • Case title clamp(25px, 3vw, 38px), line-height 1.14
  • Pull-quote 17px, tracking −0.007em
  • Body 16–16.5px, line-height 1.60–1.66
  • Mono 10–11.5px, tracking +0.06 to +0.12em

A tighter overall scale than the other two directions — the largest type here is 62px against 74px in A and 128px in C. Confidence is carried by structure rather than size. Twelve-column grid on a 44px gutter, declared at the top of the page as a tick strip and enforced by the hairlines between the hero columns.

Component treatment

Square corners everywhere, no shadows, no radius. Structure is a two-weight rule system: full ink at section boundaries, #e4e4e8 hairlines everywhere else. The pull-quote is a bordered panel with its own metadata header, because in this system a quote is a record with provenance rather than a decorative flourish.

Photography

Cropped square rather than 4:5, fully desaturated, contrast up 6 percent, with a mono data block beneath instead of a caption. The greyscale is doing real work: it stops the studio warmth from breaking a near-monochrome page, and it means any future photograph drops in without a colour-matching problem.

How it handles the hero claim

This is the direction's strongest move. The claim is three customers wrote references, so the evidence is a three-row table of names, roles and links — the assertion and its receipts in one object. No other direction can make the proof this literal, and the table doubles as a second navigation route into /proof.

What it costs

It is cold, and the brief says warmth is the differentiator. Section indices, mono metadata and a greyscale portrait describe a systems engineer. Adrian Pilgrim's actual words are about warmth, patience and humour, and this system puts them in a bordered panel labelled Reference 01 of 03. That tension is real and it is the main argument against this direction.

It borrows a house style. Mono metadata, tick strips and one signal colour is the current developer-tool visual language — Linear, Vercel, Resend. It is not a template, but it is a recognisable dialect, and Brad has already rejected two things for converging on a known look.

The greyscale portrait costs the photograph. The single warmest asset on the site is the expression in that picture, and desaturating it removes about half of what it does.
