# 11 — Leveraging the interview record into value-driven copy

**Written 15 August 2026.** Brad's instruction, verbatim: *"provide the plan on how the rest of the
copy from the interviews will get leveraged - to generate a value driven website for the reader. the
copy must sell the value of what i do. not just a CV style."*

This plan governs Phase 3. It is not a rewrite brief for one page — it is the mapping from **148
evidenced rows** (98 in Tiers A–C, 50 in Tier E) onto a site that currently uses almost none of them.

---

## 1. The diagnosis, in one line

**The site describes a career. The register contains a capability.** Those are different products and
the second one is what a hiring manager pays for.

Three measurements make the gap concrete:

| Measure | Now | Should be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidenced rows used on the homepage | **~6 of 148** | The load-bearing twelve |
| Visible homepage words | **1,027** | 250 (§7.1), or a revised cap Brad sets deliberately |
| Sections that open with a customer | **0 of 6** | At least two |

The third row is the actual finding. Every section heading on the homepage opens with Brad or with
his system — *Sell it, then make it stick* · *The system is the proof* · *The range came one job at a
time*. **Not one opens with a customer's problem.** A2, A4, A6, A11 and A28 are all customer-problem
rows and none of them is on the page.

---

## 2. What "value-driven, not CV-style" means as a test

Brad's instruction needs to be testable or it will not survive contact with drafting. **The test:**

> Cover the company name, the dates and the job title. **Does the sentence still tell a buyer what
> they get?**

- *"Ten years selling, demoing, proposing, forecasting, enabling and supporting a SaaS platform"* —
  fails. Cover the dates and it is a list of verbs any candidate could write.
- *"Schools were exporting spreadsheets into the LMS by hand every year. I got that job taken off
  them entirely, and charged for it."* — passes. It names a customer's pain, the resolution and the
  commercial consequence, and it is A28 + A29 + A30 with nothing added.

**Applied as a gate:** no sentence ships on the homepage or `/work` unless it survives the cover test.
CV-style sentences are legitimate — **on `/cv`**, which is where the reader goes for chronology.

## 3. The four value arguments, and the rows that carry each

The register's 148 rows collapse into **four arguments**. Everything else is support. This is the
spine of the rebuild, because a reader can hold four things and cannot hold 148.

### Argument 1 — I find out what the customer actually needs before I sell anything

**The differentiator, and it is currently absent from the site entirely.**

| Row | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| A4, A5 | A week across five campuses before building any strategy; returned to teach it |
| A6 | 108 one-to-one teacher interviews across lockdown |
| A11 | Onboarding built per school — different ecosystem, curriculum and practice each time |
| A28 | The problem stated as the customer's pain, not Brad's activity |
| E18 | The same word — *interviewed* — used at Xplore in 2009 |
| E15 | Left a role rather than sell what he did not believe served the buyer |

**Why it leads:** it is the only argument that explains *why* the retention happened. Without it,
retention is luck or inertia. It is also the argument the reference letters independently corroborate.

### Argument 2 — The accounts I keep are the accounts that grow

**The target role, stated as a mechanism rather than a boast.**

| Row | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| A3 | Eight consecutive renewals at one school |
| A16, A17, A18 | The five-campus save → paid consultancy + managed services → renewal + sixth campus |
| A20, A24 | Renewed while the price rose annually; never lost a school on a price rise, did lose some on total cost |
| E1, E17, E30, E41 | Four-for-four retention across the pre-education career, 2001–2015 |
| E12, E51 | Customers up ~70 per cent, income up ~120 per cent; owner-validated from a second angle |

**E30's qualification travels with this argument, always** — mixed across the Adplus book, flagship
held personally. It is what makes the other three believable.

### Argument 3 — I build the thing they buy next

**The upsell half, and the register's richest seam. Six revenue lines, one designed model (A35).**

| Row | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| A21, A22 | Support was unpriced; made it a line, structured monthly, billed annually in advance |
| A29, A30 | SIS integration removed a manual job entirely, sold as a service |
| A31 | US course materials localised and sold with on-site training |
| A32, A33 | Flat implementation fee, size-scaled managed services — the line grows as the customer grows |
| A25, A26, A27 | Ministry facilitator accreditation, opening government training funds |
| E6–E11, E52 | The same instinct at 99 Corporation — set-up costs, script writing, deposits, data sales |

**A33 is the single best row in the archive and carries no dollar figure.** Size-scaled pricing means
revenue expanded as accounts grew, without a new sale. That is the commercial mechanism behind
Argument 2, and it is why 2 and 3 must appear adjacent on the page.

### Argument 4 — I negotiate the terms that make the rest possible

**The most senior argument, and the one that separates Brad from an account manager who inherits a
contract.**

| Row | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| A34, A35 | Senior leader charged with the sector; designed its revenue model |
| A36 | Replaced the product line with itslearning when the channel contract was cancelled |
| A37, A38 | Negotiated the whole new reseller agreement; buy-as-needed with no upfront cost |
| A39 | Won the right to invoice customers directly in local currency |

**A39 is load-bearing for the whole site** and is currently nowhere on it: a reseller whose vendor
bills the end customer does not own the renewal conversation. **The accounts were his to keep because
he made them his to bill.** That single sentence is the strongest thing available for `/work`.

## 4. Where each argument goes

**The homepage carries all four at headline weight and nothing more.** Depth lives one click down.
This is what resolves the word-count breach — not trimming adjectives, but moving material to pages
built to hold it.

| Surface | Carries | Rows | Words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hero** | Arguments 1 and 2, compressed | Round five, Brad's pick | ~45 |
| **Four capability cards** | One argument each, replacing the current four | Headline row per card | ~120 |
| **Proof quotes** | Corroboration only — unchanged in role | Pilgrim, Bergh | ~60 |
| **Career rows** | Span, past tense, one hireable capability per row | E1, E17, E30, E41 | ~90 |
| **`/work` case studies** | The full mechanism, one page per argument | Everything else | Unbudgeted |
| **`/cv`** | Chronology, titles, dates | CV-style is correct here | Unbudgeted |

**The capability cards are the highest-leverage change on the site.** They currently read *Sales
Growth · Product Adoption · AI & Product · Product & UX* — four skill categories any CV lists. Under
this plan they become the four arguments, each with one evidenced row visible in the same viewport,
which is the site's own governing rule applied where it is currently not applied.

## 5. The three case studies `/work` should carry

The register supports exactly three complete narratives — problem, action, commercial outcome, all
evidenced. **Three deep is worth more than eight shallow**, and eight shallow is what the current
`/work` grid is.

**1. The five-campus save, and what it bought afterwards.** A4 → A5 → A18 → A16 → A17. The only
complete arc in the archive: a week on the ground, a strategy built and taught, five campuses linked,
use rose, then consultancy and managed services — two revenue lines that had not existed at that
account — then a renewal and a sixth campus. **Pilgrim's letter covers the save; D7 would evidence the
expansion.** Until D7 lands, the expansion publishes on Brad's word and is labelled as such.

**2. The job nobody was charging for.** A28 → A29 → A30, with A21/A22 alongside. A customer pain
stated first, removed entirely, then priced. Reads as product thinking rather than sales.

**3. The contract that made the decade possible.** A36 → A37 → A38 → A39 → A40. The recovery from a
cancelled channel contract into a better one. **A36's external cause is stated every time** — pandemic
server load, not performance.

**Not a case study:** the AI/Pikle work. It is current, it is real, and it has no outcome yet
(nothing implying an AI product has shipped). It stays a capability card.

## 6. What the childhood interviews are for, and what they are not

`7-childhood-cubs-uniform.md` and `8-childhood-plastic-bags.md` are 5,175 words and have already
become two blog posts. **They do not become homepage copy.** The memory `brad-through-line-capability`

write, which is what makes the blog worth linking. **The plastic bags "over 100 sales" figure stays
barred.**

## 8. The bars that govern every step

Restated from the register because they will be under pressure at drafting time, when a sentence is
one number short of being excellent.

- **No composite figures.** C14, C20. 120 ÷ 70, $70k + $30k + $25k, an hourly rate from the hours and
  price bands, a lifetime total of the six revenue lines. All barred, all newly tempting once the
  arguments are laid out in one place.
- **Hedges survive.** "Around 70", "around 120", "between two and six hours", "around forty to
  fifty thousand". Footnotes 45 and 33.
- **Originator, never builder.** A29, A46, E5. Brad designed and directed; he did not engineer the
  integration or draw the rebrand.
- **No self-assessment.** §7, C3–C7. Every one of those six has a publishable fact next to it.
- **The employer is never named**, in text or in any image, logo or letterhead.
- **Concessions travel with their claims.** A24 with A20, E30 with the retention finding. The
  concession is what survives a reference check.
- **Brad chooses the copy.** Nothing is swapped in unasked.

## 9. What this plan does not do

It does not touch `/cv`, where CV-style is the correct register. It does not add a design gallery —
D18's proportion bar stands, and the argument of this site is land-keep-grow, not creative practice.
It does not resolve the Sanity question, which comes after the copy settles. And it does not pick the
hero: **round five presents six options with their costs, and that decision is Brad's.**
