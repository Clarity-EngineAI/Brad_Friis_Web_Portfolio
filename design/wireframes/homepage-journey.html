Homepage blueprint · 17 August 2026

Seven stops, in the order a hiring manager reads them.

The current page has nine sections and no argued order. This is the proposed order, the question each stop answers, and what changes from what is live now. Grey blocks are placeholders for real content — this shows structure and sequence, not final visual design.

The one idea

A reader arrives asking “what does this person do?” and leaves needing to have answered “what would they do for us?” Every stop between those two points exists to move them one step, and no stop should raise a question the next stop fails to answer.

The current page breaks this in one specific place: testimonials sit at position three, before any claim has been made. Praise arrives before the reader knows what it is praising, so it reads as decoration. Moving proof after the claim is the single biggest structural change here.

Second change: account management leads, the rest follows as range. The hero currently lists five disciplines. One claim, stated once, then the breadth appears later as evidence of depth rather than as a hedge.

How do they actually do it?

Unfold the tabs into a grid

Four capabilities currently sit behind a tab strip, so three quarters of the content is hidden on arrival. A reader scanning in ninety seconds sees one card and moves on.

Tabs are right when options are alternatives. These are cumulative — they describe one method in four parts, and the argument only lands if all four are visible at once.

Change Replace the tab interaction with a 2×2 grid. Delete the tab JS. Also rename the eyebrow: “About Me” describes a bio, but this section is method.

What changes, at a glance

Nine sections become seven. Nothing is deleted from the site — two things move to the footer or into a neighbouring section. No copy is rewritten by this change alone, though the hero and the four capability cards still need Brad’s words.
