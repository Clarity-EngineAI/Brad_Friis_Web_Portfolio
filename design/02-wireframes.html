Two structural rules this IA enforces

  • No claim without its evidence in the same viewport. Every testimonial pull-quote sits beside the claim it supports, and links to its complete letter at /proof#anchor. Quotes are never collected into a decorative carousel.
  • `/career` is the story, `/resume` is the document. They cover the same twenty-five years and would be redundant if that distinction blurred. `/career` gets context, scale and links out to proof; `/resume` gets dates, bullets, print styles and a PDF.

Annotations — home
  1. Header. Five links and one button. "Download resume" is a button rather than a link because it is the single most likely action for the primary audience, and it must not require a form. It serves /resume/master.pdf directly and does not navigate.
  2. Hero. Two-line display, so the line break is a design decision rather than a wrapping accident and is authored, not left to the browser. The support paragraph carries all three proof assets in one sentence, which means a reader who leaves after the hero still leaves with the argument. Two CTAs only: "Read the references" is deliberately the primary, because proof is the differentiator and sending people to /proof first is the whole strategy.
  3. Proof strip. No heading, no card borders, no icons. Four figures on a rule. It sits immediately under the hero because the hero makes four claims and this is where they get numbers. Note the third figure carries the qualifier "then expanded" — retention alone undersells it.
  4. What I'm here for. This is what replaces the lens control. Three cards that route rather than re-render, so every audience is told within one scroll that they are in the right place, and nobody is asked to classify themselves. Cards are links; no expand or accordion behaviour.
  5. The account save. The most important block on the page and it is given the most space. Hero candidate B survives as the section heading. Body copy on the left, evidence on the right, both in the same viewport — this is the rule from the brief made literal. The link is labelled with what the reader gets, never "Learn more".
  6. Adoption. Quote and copy swap sides from section 5, so the page has a rhythm rather than two identical two-column blocks in a row. Hero candidate E becomes the heading here, where the Bergh letter can back it up immediately.
  7. Career preview. Three rows, not a visual timeline. The animated timeline lives at /career and duplicating it here would give the homepage two career sections' worth of weight for one section's worth of value.
  8. Platforms and companies. Four marks. Recognition value for the education-sector and channel readers, and it quietly evidences the vendor relationship. Greyscale, no cards, no hover states.
  9. Selected writing. No cover images. The Prolens reference uses a thumbnail per post; at three posts that is three pieces of stock photography competing with the one real photograph on the page. Titles and dates only. Full images are earned at /writing.
  10. Contact. Three fields — name, email, message. Every additional field costs conversions and the phone field in the reference template earns nothing. The copy tells the reader what to include, which improves the quality of what arrives.
  11. "What I'm proud of" is cut. The reference template fills this with family and side projects. There is no material and inventing some would be the only slop on an otherwise evidenced page. Community and governance move to /about. See decision 3.
  12. The lens pill row is cut. Prolens puts four coloured pills around the hero portrait. Rejected with the mechanism behind it — Stage 1 §6. Nothing replaces it visually; the space becomes whitespace.
  13. Open: does the homepage need a one-line availability statement near the hero — "Open to account management and channel roles in Auckland or remote"? It converts, it dates quickly, and it reads differently to the consulting audience. Recommend yes, as small text under the CTAs, editable in Sanity so it can be switched off in an hour.

Annotations — proof
  1. Heading does the arguing. "Three people wrote these without being asked" is the entire value proposition of the page in eight words. "Testimonials" or "People Say" would waste the strongest headline on the site.
  2. Ordered by proof rank, not chronology. Pilgrim first because the five-campus save is the lead asset. Bergh second because it is the longest and warmest. Lad last because it is the shortest and it closes the page on a human note.
  3. Full text, not extracts. The instinct will be to trim these to their best paragraphs. Resist it.   5. The Rototuna email keeps its chrome. From, to, date, subject, and the sign-off intact. Do not restyle it into the same card as the other two.

Recommend publishing name and role, withholding contact details, and replacing them with "contactable on request". Needs Brad's confirmation alongside decision 5.

Annotations — resume
  1. Four routes, no switcher. This is the surviving half of the lens hypothesis. Brad sets the variant when he sends the link; the reader never learns a mechanism exists and is never asked to classify themselves. Stage 1 §6 has the full argument and the overlap table behind it.
  2. Content already exists. All four variants are written.
