## 3. Call one — the CanWest sales sheet. Withhold, and correct the number

**The recommendation to withhold stands. Two of the facts behind it did not survive reading the
sheet at full resolution, and both corrections matter.**

### The quarter claim is wrong and must not be used

The brief carried the figure forward as *"172% of quarter target, the highest of seven"*.

The sheet shows July, August and September. **September is 0% for every one of the seven people on
it, and 0% for the team average.** That is not a quarter with a bad month in it. It is a quarter that
had not finished. The 172% is a cumulative total over **two months**.

Publishing "172% of quarter target" above an image whose third row reads zero is the worst available
failure: a claim printed directly over its own refutation. The first numerate reader who enlarges the
image finds it, and what they conclude is not *he rounded up* — it is *what else has he done this to*.

### "Highest of seven" is true by one percentage point

Brad 172%. The next person 171%. The phrase is accurate and, on this sheet, close to empty. It reads
as impressive until someone looks, and then it reads as spin.

### What the sheet does support, and it is better than either

The July shortfall table lists seven people against a $35,000 monthly budget. Six carry a dollar
figure. **Brad's row carries a dash.**

> **The only one of seven on the sales team to make budget that month.**

No averaging, no cumulative arithmetic, no quarter framing, no image. True, checkable against the
document if it is ever asked for, and stronger as a sentence than the number it replaces.

### Why redaction does not rescue the image

The shortfall table is keyed to two-letter initials. The initials sit directly beneath the first
names in the performance table. Redact one and the other re-identifies it. Redact both and what
remains is a single column with no context, which proves nothing and looks like it is concealing
something, because it is.

### And the site does not need it

Lemon's 2006 letter already says *"Monthly KPI targets were always achieved and Brad's dedication to
his role resulted in consistent growth of his client base and overall billing for each quarter."*
Signed, named, attributable, legible, and it exposes nobody. It is better evidence than the
spreadsheet on every measure.

**The cost of withholding is one photograph. The cost of publishing is a page arguing Brad is
trusted, warm and safe to work with, demonstrating in the same viewport that he will put six former
colleagues' figures online twenty years later.** A reader feels that contradiction without being able
to name it, and it discounts every letter on the page.

**Full analysis and the complete figures:**
[`Brad_Friis_Canwest_Sales_Figures.md`](../Brad%20Friis%20Resumes/References/Brad_Friis_Canwest_Sales_Figures.md).
That file and the source image must not be pushed to a public repository.

## 4. Call two — the 2003 problem is real, and the answer is recency plus one caption

**The recommendation from the brief holds: order by recency, work backwards, and make the age the
argument rather than apologise for it.** Five letters with two from 2003 reads as *he had to reach
back to 2003 to get to five*. Six letters in date order, newest first, with the two oldest arriving
as the closing move, reads as *this has been true the whole time*.

The audit adds one thing the brief could not know. **The oldest documents are not the weakest.**
Walker's *"an extraordinarily high strike rate... with little or no material backup what so ever"* and
Lowry's attribution of an industry-wide shift to a deliberate strategy are both more specific than
anything in the 2025 set. Age is not the problem. Ordering is.

**One caption fixes the only real hazard.** Lemon writes about CanWest Media on The Radio Network
letterhead — the ordinary reading is that he had moved employer by 2006, but a hostile reader can
read it as a reference on borrowed paper. State the relationship under the letter: *Brad's manager at
CanWest, writing after both had left.* One line, and the question closes before it is asked.

## 5. Call three — two conflicts the brief did not anticipate, resolved in opposite directions

These are the highest-stakes findings of the session and they are not symmetrical.

### MySimpleSiteMan — withhold

**The referee shares Brad's surname.** The signature reads TJFriis. Nothing on the document discloses
a relationship and nothing rules one out, but a reader does not need it spelled out. Two Friises, one
signing a reference for the other, on a small company's letterhead.

The damage is not confined to the document. A letters page works by accumulation — several
independent people, no shared incentive, saying the same things. One apparent relative does not fail
to add to that; it invites the reader to re-examine the other six for the same problem, silently, and
without ever saying why.

Against that, what it adds is thin. Cold calling, strike rate, relationships carried in from a
previous role — all claimed more credibly by Walker and Lemon, on dated letterhead, by unrelated
signatories. **There is nothing in it that is only in it.** It is also undated, which is a second
disqualification on its own.

The employment is real and belongs on `/record` as a line in the career narrative, stated without the
letter behind it.

### Jessep — publish, second tier, with the friendship in the caption

Jessep's first sentence says *"and best of all becoming his friend"*.

**The right handling is the opposite of the instinct.** Do not bury it and do not crop the sentence:
lead with it. A page that prints "best of all becoming his friend" and lets the reader weigh it is a
page that has earned trust on the other six. A page that quietly omits it is one attentive reader
away from losing all of them.

**Why this resolves differently from MySimpleSiteMan, in one line: Jessep disclosed, and the surname
did not.** That is the whole distinction and it is a principled one.

Three costs to state plainly. It is **unsigned** — typed name, plain paper, no letterhead, the only
unsigned document in the set. It contains a **garbled sentence** that will stop a reader mid-page. And
it puts a hiring statement next to the name **Vodafone**, seventeen years on, which needs the caption
to make clear Jessep wrote personally. Second-tier placement, below the six, handles all three.

## 8. Image treatment, specified

**Recommendation: the documents stay physical objects. Do not flatten them into clean white scans.**

Stage 1 §4 already ruled that the email framing on the Rototuna note *is* the proof. A 2003 letterhead
is the same argument in stronger form. A pristine white scan of a twenty-three-year-old letter looks
typeset — which is to say, it looks like something that could have been typed last week. The crease,
the paper tone and the real angle are what make it a document rather than a claim.

| | Do | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ground** | Cut the page off its background. Retain the paper's true edge | Leave the dark fabric in |
| **Geometry** | Deskew to within one or two degrees. Keep a trace of the real angle | Correct to perfect square, or fully de-keystone |
| **Tone** | Keep paper texture, creases, ink colour, the photocopy grain on Lemon's | Push to pure white and pure black |
| **Depth** | A soft contact shadow so it sits on the site ground as an object | A drop shadow with a visible offset — that is a template look |
| **Crop** | Full page. Letterheads and the Adplus tagline are part of the evidence | Crop to the body text |
| **Delivery** | 1800px long edge, WebP with JPEG fallback, 150–250 KB. 2400px for 2x | Ship 4 MB originals |

### Redaction is visible, not invisible

**Contact details are covered with a solid block in the site's ground colour. They are not cloned
out, blurred, or airbrushed away.**

A visible redaction reads as care — somebody thought about this person's privacy and acted. An
invisible removal is a retouched document, and a retouched document is not evidence. On a page whose
entire argument is *here is the actual paper*, quietly editing the paper is the one thing that cannot
be done.

### What has been produced this session

`Brad Friis Resumes/References/web/` — upright, 1800px, web-weight derivatives of **Lowry, Walker and
Lemon** only. These are the three that carry no redaction requirement and no open judgement call.

Deliberately **not** generated: Jessep and 95bFM, because both must have a home address redacted
before any distribution-ready copy exists; MySimpleSiteMan and the CanWest sheet, because generating
a web-ready asset for a document recommended for withholding is the wrong artefact to leave lying
around.

Background removal and deskew are not possible with the tooling available here. They are a phase 5
design step, specified above.

## 9. The span claim, restated for phase 2

> **Partly overturned in phase 2, 11 August 2026, by reading the same six documents again. The
> section is left standing because the reasoning it got right is still the reasoning that governs.**
>
> **Dropped: "four industries".** Advertising and tourism are the same fifteen months. AR Walker was
> Brad's managing director at Adplus; Hamish Lowry ran Hawke's Bay Tourism, which was an Adplus
> account, and he wrote six weeks before Walker did. Counting them as two industries is arithmetic a
> reader can do unaided, and a reader who does it reads the number as padding — then keeps counting
> and finds that the six documents cover three jobs rather than six. On a page whose whole argument
> is that its numbers survive checking, that is the wrong number to print.
>
> **Kept: everything else.** *Do not say twenty-five years about the letters* stands and is the most
> important sentence in the section. The three phrasings stand, and phrasing 2 — *the newest is from
> June 2025, the oldest from February 2003* — is now an available standfirst on `/letters`.
>
> **Added: two bosses and four customers, 2003 to 2025.** It answers a sharper objection than the
> industry count did, and it cannot be picked apart. Full argument in
> `COPY/letters/letters-page.md` §2.

The old claim, now dead with the count it was built on:

> ~~Five people. Three industries. Twenty-five years. All of it in writing.~~

What the evidence actually supports:

> **Six people. Six organisations. Four industries. The oldest letter is from 2003.**

Six named referees: Bergh, Pilgrim, Lad, Lowry, Walker, Lemon. Six organisations. Four industries —
**advertising, tourism, radio, education software**. Twenty-two years between the oldest and the
newest, 2003 to 2025.

**Four industries is a better number than three and it does more work.** Three was education software
plus two adjacent things. Four with radio in it is a pattern nobody can explain away as a friendly
sector, which was the exact hole the span argument existed to close.

**Do not say twenty-five years about the letters.** Twenty-five is the career figure and it is true of
the career. The letters span twenty-two, and the archive's earliest document — 95bFM, 2000 — is an
artefact rather than a letter. Conflating the two puts an overclaim in the one place the site cannot
afford one.

Three phrasings for phase 2 to work from, in decreasing confidence:

1. **"Six people. Four industries. Twenty-two years apart."** — Shortest. The three facts that matter.
2. **"The newest of these is from June 2025. The oldest is from February 2003."** — No count at all.
   Lets the reader do the arithmetic, which is more convincing than being handed the answer.
3. **"Six people, who have never met, describing the same man twenty-two years apart."** — The most
   confident, and it depends on §10 holding up.

### What decisions 3 and 4 cost, recorded rather than argued away

Both were Brad's calls to make and both are defensible. Neither is free, and the cost is written down
here so that a later session does not rediscover it as a surprise.

**Jessep.** The audit's argument was that a page which prints *"and best of all becoming his friend"*
and lets the reader weigh it has earned trust on the other six, whereas a page that quietly omits it
is one attentive reader away from losing all of them. Leaving the document out entirely avoids the
second failure as well as the first — there is nothing on the site for an attentive reader to catch,
because there is no disclosure to be caught omitting. What is lost is the trust move, not the safety.
A replacement reference from Jessep, written for publication and not opening on the friendship, would
be a straightforwardly better document than the 2009 one, and it takes the count to seven.

**95bFM.** This is the more expensive of the two. It was the earliest document in the archive by three
years, the only evidence of Brad on the **buy** side negotiating against a vendor and winning on
structure rather than price, and one of two independent documents recording a music, nightclub and
events strand of the career **the CV does not mention at all**. That strand now has no evidence behind
it anywhere on the site, so `/record` either omits it or asserts it. Per the governing rule, omit it.

### The consequence nobody has to manage any more

**Nothing on `/letters` is blocked.** The audit's blocking list — Jessep's consent, Deady's consent,
two home addresses to redact, one third party to redact, one open second-tier judgement call — is
empty. The page is six consented documents in one uniform tier with no redaction beyond contact
details. That is a materially simpler page than the one this document was written to plan for.

### The one rule the decisions create

Ten documents exist and six are published. **No copy anywhere on the site may imply the archive is
complete.** Six is a count of what is shown, never a count of what exists. Enforced in
`COPY/letters/letters-page.md` §7, which also records the argument for and against disclosing the
withheld documents, and why disclosing them was rejected.
