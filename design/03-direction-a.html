Direction A — Editorial · specification

Type pairing

Newsreader — display and body. Production Type's open-source text face, with an optical-size axis that is actually used here: opsz 60 on the hero for tight, high-contrast display fitting, opsz 30 on the pull-quote, defaults for body. Chosen over Playfair and Lora because both have been used into the ground, and over Fraunces because Fraunces carries a recognisable house style of its own.

Archivo — furniture only. Navigation, labels, buttons, captions, attributions. It never sets a sentence longer than a line, which is what keeps the page reading as a magazine rather than a website with a serif heading.

A warm ground and a brown-cast ink, so nothing on the page is pure black or pure white. One accent, deep olive, used in exactly three places: the primary button, the rule above the pull-quote, and link underlines. The olive was taken from the jacket in the portrait, which means the photograph sits inside the palette instead of on top of it.

Scale and spacing

  • Display clamp(40px, 5.6vw, 74px), line-height 1.03, tracking −0.021em
  • Case title clamp(30px, 3.6vw, 47px), line-height 1.10
  • Pull-quote 25px italic, weight 300
  • Body 18.5–19.5px, line-height 1.62–1.68
  • Furniture 11.5–14px, tracking +0.14em on uppercase

Roughly a 1.28 ratio through the text sizes with a deliberate break at display, so the hero is a jump rather than a step. Measures are wide by web standards — 60ch in the hero, 64ch in the body — because a serif at 19px holds a long line, and long lines are what make a page read as considered rather than scanned. Vertical rhythm on an 8px base: 84px above the hero, 110px below the case study, 22px between paragraphs.

Component treatment

Rules do the structural work, not boxes. A 1px ink rule opens each section, hairlines in #d9d1bf separate. The pull-quote has no container at all — a 3px olive rule, an oversized italic, and an attribution block. Corners at 2px, which reads as printed rather than as a card.

Photography

Full-bleed 4:5 at 40 percent of the hero width, positioned high so the eyeline sits near the optical centre. Saturation pulled to 92 percent and contrast lifted 2 percent so the studio white recedes into the paper ground. Captioned in the furniture face, the way a magazine credits a picture.

How it handles the hero claim

The three names sit directly under the support paragraph above a hairline, so the claim and its evidence are in the same viewport at every breakpoint. The names are set in the furniture face at 12.5px, which keeps them as a credit line rather than turning them into a second headline.

What it costs

It reads older. The serif, the paper and the wide measures are the visual language of consultancy, law and long-form journalism. That is exactly right for the credibility problem and exactly wrong for the AI-consulting audience, who arrive from `/writing` expecting the person writing about AI for business to look like they work in this decade. This is the one direction where the blog and the resume genuinely pull in different directions.

It flatters age. Brad is a candidate with twenty-five years behind him in a market that is not always kind about that. A warm-paper editorial system quietly reinforces seniority, which is the intended read, but it leaves no room to signal currency.

It is the hardest to keep clean. Editorial systems fail loudly. One heading at the wrong optical size, one measure too narrow, one stock photograph, and it collapses into a template. It also carries the largest font payload of the three at two families with variable axes.
