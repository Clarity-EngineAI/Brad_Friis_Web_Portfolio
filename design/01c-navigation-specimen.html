Six navigation systems, at shipping size

Each bar below is set at the size it would actually ship: 13.5px labels, 26px of vertical padding, on the page ground rather than on a slide. A list of labels always reads better than a navigation bar does, which is how the last set got through.

All six are shown, including the two that fail. The far end of the range is what tells you where the middle is, so the failures are here to be looked at rather than summarised.

Hover or tab onto any label to reveal what sits behind it. The hero line is shown as a placeholder — the previous one was retired on 11 August 2026 and a replacement has not been written. Full argument in 01b-navigation-tone-and-structure.md.
A The reader's job, as verbs Out — tests 5, 6, 7

Label by what the reader came to do rather than what the thing is called. Nobody has to translate a category noun into an intention.

Brad Friis Hire me Work with me Check me out Read me Take the CV — PDF
Hover a label. Hire me. Twenty-five years, the decade selling Schoology and itslearning, and the document you can forward. Work with me. Clarity Engine AI. Positioning, commercial setup and customer experience. Check me out. Five letters, in full, with names on them. Read me. Writing about AI, positioning and customer experience.

Hero line to be written.

Twenty-five years in software, media and agency work. Ten of them running a United States vendor's New Zealand and Australian business as its only local partner.

  • Four first-person verb phrases is a costume. Nothing stands out when everything is odd, which is the second half of test 5.
  • "Hire me" is needy from a man twenty-five years into a commercial career. It inverts the status the rest of the site works to build.
  • It would run unchanged on any freelancer's site. Test 6, outright.
B The questions in the reader's head, in order Recommended — passes all seven

Ordered by what a first-time visitor actually wants to know, with the evidence sitting left of the biography. Who says so is the single unexpected item; everything else is a legible signpost and the wit lives one layer down.

Brad Friis The record Who says so Consulting Field notes Take the CV — PDF
Hover a label. The record. Twenty-five years, told properly. Career, community and the CV. Who says so. Five people put it in writing. Full letters, named, nothing trimmed. Consulting. Clarity Engine AI. What I take on, and what it costs. Field notes. AI, positioning and customer experience, from inside the work.

Hero line to be written.

Twenty-five years in software, media and agency work. Ten of them running a United States vendor's New Zealand and Australian business as its only local partner, where the licensed base went from 2,800 to 29,784 and an account that was actively evaluating a competitor stayed and expanded.

  • The set is not grammatically consistent. An article, a compound, a clause and a bare gerund. Regularising it would create the repeated formula that killed D and F, so the inconsistency is the price of passing and should be defended rather than fixed.
  • "Consulting" is the plainest word in the row and can look like a late addition. The mitigation is on the page, not in the label — it must answer does he actually want a job in its first paragraph.
  • "The record" is cold. All the warmth has to come from the page headline and the community material at the foot of it.
  • It is not a brave navigation. One unexpected item in four is a deliberately conservative setting of the dial.
  • "Who says so" gets one shot. If the letters page ships with two empty placeholder slots, the boldest label on the site sits over the weakest page.
C Hats and roles — the Prolens device made functional Out — test 6

One person, several hats, tagged consistently, so a reader assembles the version of Brad they came for without a switcher. Improves on the template, where the tags are decoration attached to status nouns with nothing behind them.

Brad Friis Account manager Channel partner Consultant Letters CV
Hover a label. Account manager. Retention and expansion. Ten years of it, tagged across every piece of evidence. Channel partner. One territory, one vendor, a decade, three exclusive regions. Consultant. Clarity Engine AI. Positioning, messaging, commercial setup. Letters. Five references, each tagged with the roles it proves.

Hero line to be written.

One person, three hats. Every letter, every role and every article carries the tags it earns, so you can read only the half that concerns you.

  • Three roles in a nav bar is a menu of services — a freelancer trope wearing a different hat.
  • Coloured pills are the template look this project has rejected twice, and without colour the device loses most of its legibility.
  • It fragments the evidence. The five-campus save proves all three roles, so tagging it three times makes the tags mean less, not more.
  • The arithmetic does not work yet. One career entry, five letters and up to five articles is nothing to filter. Same failure Stage 1 §6 caught in the lens switcher.
D Evidence type — shown against claimed Out — tests 4 and 7 · uncomfortable by design

Split the site by what can be shown and what has to be asserted. A principle only available to someone who has documents where everyone else has adjectives.

Brad Friis Signed Shipped Argued Taking work CV
Hover a label. Signed. Five letters with signatures on them. Shipped. What actually happened. 2,800 to 29,784, the save, the decade. Argued. The writing. Positions taken, with reasons. Taking work. Clarity Engine AI, and what I am open to.

Hero line to be written.

Everything on this site is either a document somebody else signed or a number you can check. Nothing here is an adjective.

  • Fatal: every label costs a beat. A reader must work out that Signed means references before they can use it. Test 4, which is the rule "Unsolicited" broke.
  • Four past participles is a formula repeated across labels. Test 7.
  • "Taking work" sits one word from the rejected "Availability" and carries the same reads-as-unemployed risk.
  • What survives: Signed. as the letters page headline. It names the physical fact and makes no claim about how the letters were obtained — worth a great deal after 11 August.
E What he does for you, written as offers Out — test 2 · uncomfortable by design

No biography anywhere. The career has to justify itself inside each offer as the evidence for it, which is the site's governing rule expressed as structure rather than discipline.

Brad Friis Keep your customers Grow your territory Fix your positioning Who says so CV
Hover a label. Keep your customers. Retention and expansion. Ten years of it, with the letters attached. Grow your territory. Channel and partnership. One vendor, two markets, a decade. Fix your positioning. Clarity Engine AI. Positioning, messaging, commercial setup. Who says so. Five letters, in full.

Hero line to be written.

Tell me what is stuck. Twenty-five years of commercial work behind every answer, and five people who put their names to it.

  • Fatal: there is no door that reads as for the recruiter. A hiring manager sees three imperatives addressed to a buyer and concludes Brad is not looking for employment. Test 2.
  • Converting the secondary audience by losing the primary one is not a trade worth making.
  • Three imperative offers is a services-page template of a different family.
  • What survives: all three offers, as the content of the consulting page — which currently has nothing on it.
F Format — what the reader is handed Out — tests 4, 5, 7

Organise by the object the reader leaves with rather than by topic. The smallest and calmest set on offer, and the only one that solves the four-pages-doing-one-job problem by structure.

Brad Friis The documents The work The argument The conversation
Hover a label. The documents. Everything forwardable. CV, five letters, the career record. The work. Clarity Engine AI, Pikle, and what I take on. The argument. The writing. The conversation. Contact, and what I am open to — roles and consulting both.

Hero line to be written.

Four things you can leave with. Everything else on this site is in service of one of them.

  • "The argument" costs a beat. Test 4.
  • Four definite articles is a formula repeated across labels. Test 7.
  • Nothing in it is repeatable a day later. Safe everywhere is exactly how the last attempt failed.
  • Folding the CV inside a page rather than a button costs the recruiter a click on the one thing they came for.
  • What survives: the discipline. Four content routes is the right number, and F is the proof of it.

  • 1 Forty seconds. A hiring manager gives the site forty seconds. Can they name two things it offers from the bar alone?
  • 2 Two doors. Neither audience's first click may land somewhere that reads as for the other one, and neither may be second-class.
  • 3 Say it aloud. Would Brad say the label to a person in a bar?
  • 4 One beat. Wit is allowed; a riddle is not. The rule "Unsolicited" broke.
  • 5 Memorability under contrast. One repeatable thing. Five odd labels is the same failure as none.
  • 6 Only Brad. If the set would work unchanged on somebody else's site, it is generic.
  • 7 Bans. No Proof, no Unsolicited, no consultancy slop, no borrowed slang, no formula repeated across labels, no row of pure abstract nouns.
